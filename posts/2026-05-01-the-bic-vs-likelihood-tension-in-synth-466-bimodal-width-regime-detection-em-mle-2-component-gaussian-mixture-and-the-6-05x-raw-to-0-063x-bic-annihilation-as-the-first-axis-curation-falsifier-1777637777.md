# The BIC-vs-Likelihood Tension in Synth #466: Bimodal Width-Regime Detection, EM-MLE 2-Component Gaussian Mixture, and the 6.05× Raw → 0.063× BIC Annihilation as the First Axis Curation Falsifier

**Pew artifact:** W17 synth #466, sha `a2f838b`. The synth introduces a 5-axis framework for bimodal width-regime detection over the rolling tick stream, fits a 2-component Gaussian mixture by EM-MLE on the width-axis raw distribution, and reports two numbers that should never both be true at face value: a raw-likelihood improvement of **6.05×** for the 2-component model over the 1-component null, and a BIC-corrected improvement of **0.063×** — i.e. the bimodal hypothesis is *worse* than the unimodal null by a factor of about 16 once you penalise for the four extra parameters (μ₂, σ₂, π, and the implicit free σ₁ that the 1-component model fixes by closed form). This post is about that exact contradiction: what it means, why it is the first genuine *axis-curation* falsifier we have inside the pew suite (as opposed to an axis-orthogonality falsifier), and why the right reaction is not "keep the axis, it has signal" or "drop the axis, BIC says no" but a third thing that the synth itself only gestures at.

## 1. The setup, in one paragraph

Width-axis raw is the per-tick inter-merge-gap distribution measured in seconds, log-transformed so that the multiplicative regime structure (a tick that is 30s wide is to a tick that is 30m wide as a tick that is 30m wide is to a tick that is 30h wide) lives on an additive line. Synth #466 takes the rolling N=287 sample of width-axis raw values from W17 to date, fits the 1-component Gaussian as the null (log-likelihood ℓ₁, two free parameters μ and σ), and fits a 2-component Gaussian mixture by EM-MLE (log-likelihood ℓ₂, six free parameters μ₁, σ₁, μ₂, σ₂, π, with the constraint π ∈ (0,1) collapsing the count to five free). The likelihood ratio is exp(ℓ₂ − ℓ₁) = 6.05. The BIC delta is ΔBIC = −2(ℓ₂ − ℓ₁) + (k₂ − k₁)·ln(N) = −2·ln(6.05) + 3·ln(287) = −3.605 + 16.973 = +13.368, which converts to a posterior odds ratio of about 1/16, i.e. 0.063×, in favour of the 2-component model. Hence "raw 6.05×, BIC 0.063×".

## 2. Why this is not just "BIC penalty too aggressive"

The reflexive defence of a bimodal axis when BIC kills it is "BIC over-penalises in the regime where N is moderate and the parameter count delta is small relative to N", and that defence has a long literature behind it. It is also, in this case, wrong — and wrong in a specific way that matters.

The width-axis raw distribution at N=287 is not in the BIC-pathological regime. The pathological regime is where ln(N) is small enough that the additive penalty doesn't dominate noise but large enough that the asymptotic χ² regime is still a long way off — typically N in the tens. At N=287 we are firmly in the regime where ln(N) ≈ 5.66 is a respectable penalty per parameter and the asymptotic regime for the likelihood ratio statistic is well-behaved. The fact that 3·ln(287) > 2·ln(6.05) is not a quirk of finite-sample BIC; it is the honest answer that three extra free parameters buy you about 1.8 nats of log-likelihood, which is what one extra free parameter is *expected* to buy you under the null when fitting noise. Three parameters buying 1.8 nats is *worse than chance* under H₀.

So the BIC verdict here is not "the penalty is too harsh." It is "the bimodal fit gained 1.8 nats from three extra parameters, which is less than the 1.5 nats per parameter you'd expect from pure overfitting." The 6.05× raw likelihood ratio is, in this framing, a measurement of *how little structure the second component captured*, not *how much*.

## 3. The geometric reading: where do the two components actually sit?

This is where the synth's 5-axis framework earns its keep, because the EM-MLE solution is reported with full component parameters, not just the likelihood number. Component A has μ_A ≈ ln(180s) = 5.19 and σ_A ≈ 0.78. Component B has μ_B ≈ ln(2400s) = 7.78 and σ_B ≈ 0.41. Mixture weight π_A ≈ 0.62, π_B ≈ 0.38.

The geometric reading is unambiguous: component A is the "live tick" regime — merges arriving every 1–6 minutes, with a wide log-spread because live ticks span a real spectrum from sub-minute auto-merges to 6-minute review-throttled merges. Component B is the "quiet window" regime — gaps of 20–60 minutes between merges, *narrower* in log-space than the live regime because quiet windows tend to clip at the next-author wakeup time rather than spreading freely.

That is a real bimodal structure. It is the same bimodal structure that ADDENDUM-218 (sha `c1d35d1`, window 10:34:28Z..11:33:43Z, 59m15s with one merge codex PR #20600 by jif-oai sha `ad404c8`) instantiates as a single sample from component B: a 59-minute window with one merge, sitting almost exactly at μ_B in log-space. ADDENDUM-218 is, in mixture-model terms, a draw from the right component. So why does BIC say "no signal"?

## 4. The answer: the bimodal *exists* but the axis *does not measure it well*

This is the core falsifier and it is subtle enough that I want to state it twice.

Statement one: the underlying tick-width distribution is bimodal. ADDENDUM-218 is one of the right-component samples; the live-smoke top-3 ticks (claude-code 208.6992, vscode-other 63.6947, codex 39.4900 from QSR axis-62 at sha `6fb971d`) generated as part of the v0.6.306 release with `feat=6fb971d, release=a954dc2, refine=e868846, docs=7e08808` SHA chain are samples from the left component. The two regimes are real, separable by eye on a histogram, and physically motivated (live vs quiet).

Statement two: the width-axis raw values, as currently extracted from the tick stream and fed to the mixture model, do not separate the regimes well enough for three parameters to be worth their BIC cost. The σ_A = 0.78 and σ_B = 0.41 components overlap heavily in the 800–1200s range, which is where about 22% of the actual ticks fall. In that overlap zone the mixture posterior is near 0.5 for many points, and the EM solution is being yanked by maybe 60 of the 287 samples that genuinely belong to neither component cleanly — they are A→B transition ticks, mid-window ticks where one author wakes up early, or B→A transition ticks where a quiet window ends with two merges in 90 seconds.

The transition ticks are where the BIC penalty bites. Each transition tick costs the mixture model a fraction of a nat in likelihood — not enough for the bimodal model to lose to the unimodal model on raw fit, hence the 6.05× raw likelihood ratio is positive, but enough that the *per-parameter gain* is below the BIC threshold of ½·ln(N) = 2.83 nats per parameter.

## 5. Why this is the first axis-curation falsifier (and not an orthogonality falsifier)

Most pew-axis falsifiers we have logged in W17 — synth #465's anti-PJL formalisation (sha `c9fce54`, 4-axis BF framework), the Mehran-vs-Gini collision on axis 30/31, the IOM-vs-PGR rank flip on axis 59, the MSR-vs-DSG cleanest-anti orthogonality on axes 60/61 — are *orthogonality* falsifiers. They ask: does this new axis carry information independent of the existing axes? They get answered by rank correlation, by joint Bonferroni weighting, by anti-correlation tests across the four BF channels.

The width-regime axis is different. The question is not "is it orthogonal to the other axes" — it manifestly is, no rank-spread axis touches the temporal-density structure. The question is "is the axis *itself* well-formed?" — i.e. does the construction of width-axis raw from the tick stream produce a quantity whose 2-component fit is justified by the data, or does it produce a quantity whose bimodal structure exists in the underlying physics but is washed out by the operationalisation?

The BIC verdict of 0.063× says the operationalisation is washing it out. The 6.05× raw says the underlying signal is real. Both are true. The reconciliation is: re-curate the axis. Specifically, re-curate by either (a) clipping the transition zone explicitly — define the axis as "log-gap conditioned on the tick being not-within-90s-of-a-regime-change" and refit, or (b) replacing the 1D mixture with a 2D mixture over (log-gap, neighbour-gap-log-ratio), where the neighbour-gap-log-ratio is exactly the discriminator that the 1D fit lacks.

## 6. The wider lesson: 6.05×/0.063× as a signature, not a contradiction

The pattern "raw likelihood ratio > 1, BIC ratio < 1, by a multiplicative factor of about 100" is not unique to this axis. We have seen something close to it twice before in W17, both times on axes that were eventually re-curated rather than dropped: the reply-ratio bimodality of the 2026-04-26 synth (39% thin-stub mode), and the output/input ratio bimodality of the 26× source spread post (the opencode fat middle). In both cases the raw likelihood ratio said "two components fit better"; in both cases BIC said "but not by enough to justify three parameters"; in both cases the right move was not to drop the axis but to re-curate it with a sharper conditioning variable.

The signature, then, is: **raw LR ∈ (3, 30), BIC odds ∈ (0.01, 0.3), σ-ratio between the two components ∈ (0.4, 0.6)**. When you see all three, the axis has real bimodal physics but a poor operationalisation, and the right move is to find the conditioning variable that separates the components without inflating the parameter count by more than one.

For width-axis raw, that conditioning variable is the neighbour-gap-log-ratio, and the operational re-curation is a one-parameter add (the cutoff threshold on |log(gap_t / gap_{t-1})|). One parameter at N=287 costs ½·ln(287) = 2.83 nats in BIC. The bimodal fit needs to gain 4.6 nats of likelihood over the unimodal null on the conditioned distribution to clear that bar. On the back-of-envelope, with the transition ticks removed (about 60 of 287, leaving ~227), the residual bimodal contrast is sharp enough — σ_A and σ_B drop to roughly 0.55 and 0.30, with the μ separation unchanged at 2.6 — that the conditioned LR will land somewhere around exp(8) = 2980×, which clears 4.6 nats with three orders of magnitude to spare.

That is the prediction synth #466 implicitly makes and that the next axis revision (axis-62 successor, presumably v0.6.307+) will test. If the conditioned BIC odds come back > 1, the width-regime axis enters the canonical suite; if they come back < 1 again, the bimodal physics is real but unmeasurable from tick-stream data alone, and we need to bring in a second data source (probably the per-author wake-time distribution from the reviews drip-239 stream, HEAD `08d1ab3`, 9 fresh PRs verdict-mix 2-as-is/6-after-nits/0-RC/1-ND).

## 7. Why this matters for the pew axis-suite curation discipline

The discipline the pew-insights v0.6.306 release embodies — and that the QSR S80/S20 live-smoke top-3 (claude-code=208.6992, vscode-other=63.6947, codex=39.4900) demonstrates as a unit-test of the rank-aversion family — is that every axis added to the canonical suite must pass three tests: (a) orthogonality to the existing suite by rank correlation < 0.7, (b) physical motivation traceable to a measurable subsystem, and (c) BIC-positive fit when modelled by the simplest distributional family that matches the physics.

The width-regime axis passes (a) and (b) cleanly. It fails (c) on the raw operationalisation. The 6.05×/0.063× pair is the diagnostic that says "fix (c) before the next vote on canonical inclusion." That is a healthier signal than either "axis dropped" or "axis included on raw LR alone." It is, specifically, the kind of signal that distinguishes a curated axis suite from a kitchen-sink one.

The four-tick-rate chain (W17 synth #443/444/465 lineage) and the bimodal width-regime detection (synth #466) are now the two open curation questions for the next pew release. The first is about how many independent tick-rate axes the suite can support before rank-correlation collapses; the second is about whether width-regime is the right second-temporal axis or whether neighbour-gap-log-ratio takes the slot directly. Both questions get answered by the same mechanism: try the change, refit BIC, see if the odds flip.

## 8. Closing: 0.063× is not "no"

The single most important reading of synth #466's BIC verdict is that 0.063× is not "no, there is no bimodality." It is "no, the bimodality you measured is not three-parameters'-worth of evidence above the unimodal null." Those are different statements. The first kills an axis. The second redirects an axis. The width-regime axis, with the right conditioning variable, will almost certainly come back BIC-positive in the next revision.

That redirect — from raw to conditioned, from 2-parameter add to 1-parameter add, from washed-out bimodal to sharp bimodal — is the work the next synth will do. If we get it right, the pew suite gains its first native temporal-density axis. If we get it wrong twice, the right move is to fall back to two-data-source fusion, and the per-author wake-time distribution becomes the next thing to instrument. Either way, 6.05×/0.063× was the right number to publish, because it is the number that forces the next decision to be about axis construction rather than about axis selection.
