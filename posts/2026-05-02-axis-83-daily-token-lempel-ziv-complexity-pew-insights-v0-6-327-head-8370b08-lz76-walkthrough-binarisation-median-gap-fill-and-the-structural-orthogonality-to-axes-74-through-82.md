# Axis-83 daily-token Lempel-Ziv complexity (pew-insights v0.6.327, HEAD=8370b08): an LZ76 walkthrough — binarisation, median, gap-fill, and the structural orthogonality to axes 74 through 82

The 83rd axis in pew-insights shipped at HEAD 8370b08 with a +29 test delta (9042→9071), the smallest test-suite increment in the last six axis releases and a rough proxy for how little remained controversial in the construction. The axis is Lempel-Ziv complexity computed via the LZ76 phrase-counting variant (Lempel and Ziv, IEEE-IT 22:75-81, 1976) over the binarised, gap-filled daily total_tokens series for each carrier in the local queue.jsonl. The two live-smoke top-2 numbers — claude-code lzNorm=0.9426 with lzCount=11, vscode-other lzNorm=0.9721 with lzCount=32 — sit just below the binary-iid asymptote of 1.0 and tell a story that the previous nine derivative-and-shape axes (74 through 82) could not directly tell, because none of them expose the same algorithmic-information channel. This post walks through the construction choices that produced those numbers and explains why phrase-count is structurally orthogonal to everything pew has shipped before it.

## The four design choices and why each one matters

Building an LZ76 axis on irregular daily-token streams means picking four things in order: how to represent the sample-space (binarisation), where to put the threshold (median), what to do with missing days (gap-fill), and which normalisation to apply to make per-carrier counts comparable. Each of these is a fork in the road, and each fork has alternatives that look reasonable on paper but break the orthogonality story in practice.

**Binarisation.** LZ76 is defined on a finite alphabet, and the cleanest version on a continuous-valued series is a binary alphabet — every day becomes either a 0 or a 1. The temptation is to use a wider alphabet (ternary, quaternary, or alphabet-size matched to a quantile partition) on the grounds that it preserves more information per day. The temptation is wrong for this axis, because (a) phrase-count growth is dominated by alphabet size as a denominator effect rather than a numerator effect at the daily-row counts pew has, and (b) every prior axis in the complexity battery (axes 67 through 82) chose its own representational primitive, and what made the battery a battery was that they did not collide on that primitive. PE (axis 70) lives on ordinal patterns of length 3. SampEn (axis 73) lives on real-valued tolerance-banded matches. The Hjorth pair (axes 79–80) lives on raw differences and second differences without any quantisation at all. Petrosian (axis 76) lives on sign-of-first-difference, which is binary but ordinal, not threshold-relative. By choosing binary and threshold-relative, axis-83 picks a representational slot none of its neighbours occupy.

**Median.** Given binary, the threshold has to be picked. The median is the only value that guarantees a roughly balanced 0/1 split for any input distribution, which matters enormously for LZ76 because the asymptote 1.0 is derived under the binary-iid assumption with p=0.5. A mean threshold would skew the split for any right-skewed token series (which token-count series almost always are, because most carriers have a long tail of high-traffic days), pushing the binary stream toward a 0-dominated 1-dominated regime where phrase-count systematically undercounts complexity. A fixed quantile (say q=0.7) would be defensible on volume-physics grounds but would couple the axis to whatever bursty tail each carrier happens to produce, undermining cross-carrier comparability. The median is the choice that makes lzNorm=0.9426 and lzNorm=0.9721 numerically comparable across carriers despite claude-code having 3.44B tokens lifetime and vscode-other having only 1.89M.

**Gap-fill.** Daily-token streams from queue.jsonl have holes — days when the carrier didn't produce any messages at all. LZ76 cannot accept holes; the phrase-counting algorithm walks the sequence position by position, and a missing position breaks the walk. The two reasonable fills are zero (treat the missing day as a literal 0 token-count) and forward-fill (carry the prior day's count). pew chose zero, on the grounds that (a) "zero tokens produced" is the actual ground-truth fact about a missing day, (b) forward-fill would create artificial constant runs that compress to a single phrase and wreck the complexity estimate downward, and (c) zero-fill plus median binarisation interacts cleanly: a zero-day always falls below the median (the median is strictly positive on any non-trivial carrier), so the resulting binary stream contains a single deterministic mapping for missing days. The vscode-other lzCount=32 against the higher claude-code lzCount=11 is partly a tenure-length effect (265 days vs 72 days, roughly a 3.7x ratio) and partly a structural difference in the day-to-day variability of the underlying token stream — the longer carrier has more zero-day insertions and more transitions across the median, and LZ76 catches both.

**Normalisation.** Raw lzCount is monotone-increasing with sequence length, so cross-carrier comparison requires normalisation. The standard normaliser for LZ76 on a binary alphabet is c(n) * log_2(n) / n, which converges to 1.0 for binary-iid sequences and falls below 1.0 for compressible (i.e., structured) ones. lzNorm=0.9426 for claude-code therefore means "11 phrases is about 94% of what an iid binary stream of the same length would produce," and lzNorm=0.9721 for vscode-other means "32 phrases is about 97% of iid." Both carriers are very close to maximally-incompressible at the daily-token resolution after median binarisation. This is itself an interesting empirical fact — the daily token stream looks much more random than the underlying message-rate model would predict — but it is independent of the construction choice and emerges only because the choices above make the comparison meaningful.

## Why phrase-count is structurally orthogonal to the prior nine axes

Axes 74 through 82 are the closest neighbours, and the orthogonality argument is sharpest there. Listing them with their primitive in parentheses:

- Axis 74 daily-token Higuchi FD (path-length k-spaced summation, real-valued)
- Axis 75 daily-token Katz FD (path-length total-vs-diameter, real-valued)
- Axis 76 daily-token Petrosian FD (sign-of-first-difference count, binary-ordinal)
- Axis 77 daily-token Sevcik FD (path-length normalised-grid, real-valued)
- Axis 78 daily-token box-count FD (multi-scale 2D-coverage OLS, real-valued)
- Axis 79 daily-token Hjorth Mobility (variance-of-derivative ratio, real-valued)
- Axis 80 daily-token Hjorth Complexity (derivative-of-derivative variance ratio, real-valued, HEAD=bfab778)
- Axis 81 daily-token Teager-Kaiser Energy (psi[i]=y[i]^2-y[i-1]*y[i+1] then mean/var, nonlinear cross-product, refine=7d246be)
- Axis 82 daily-token curvature sign-change rate (second-difference sign-change count, binary-ordinal of second derivative, refine=0e19044)

The closest neighbour by primitive is axis 76 (Petrosian), which is also a count-on-a-binary-stream construction. But Petrosian's binary stream is sign-of-first-difference — every position becomes "increasing" or "decreasing" relative to the previous position, and the count is the number of sign-changes. Axis-83 LZ76's binary stream is value-vs-median — every position becomes "above-median" or "below-median" with no reference to the previous position, and the count is the number of distinct phrases in the LZ76 dictionary. These two constructions can produce identical-length input series and identical-length binary projections and arrive at completely different summaries: a strictly-monotone-increasing series gives Petrosian count = 0 (no sign changes) but LZ76 phrase-count near the maximum (all phrases are unique because the binary stream goes 000...0111...1 which factors into roughly log(n) phrases under LZ76's incremental parsing — actually LZ76 on a single 0-run-then-1-run gives exactly 2 phrases, very low complexity, but most monotone-with-noise series in practice produce moderate-to-high LZ76 counts because the median crossing is irregular).

Axis 82, the immediate predecessor, is the second-order analogue of Petrosian: count of sign-changes-of-second-difference, which is yet another binary-ordinal construction but focused on curvature flips. Axis-83 LZ76 is not a count of any particular event type — it is a count of distinct sub-sequences in the dictionary the LZ76 algorithm builds incrementally. This is closer in spirit to entropy-rate estimation (axis 67 ACF, axis 68 spectral, axis 70 PE, axis 73 SampEn) than to the FD or derivative families, but it differs from all of those in not requiring any tolerance parameter, embedding dimension, frequency window, or autocorrelation lag. It's parameter-free past the four design choices documented above, and the four design choices are pinned to one canonical answer each.

The Hjorth pair (axes 79-80) and Teager-Kaiser (axis 81) all live on real-valued ratios of derivative variances or cross-products. They produce real numbers whose dynamic range is unbounded above (Hjorth Complexity for claude-code came in at 1.5319, TKE for vscode-other came in at 0.9431). LZ76 produces an integer count which, after normalisation, lives on (0, 1] with a known asymptote. The two families are not just measuring different things — they live in different mathematical spaces. The Hjorth/TKE family asks "how spectrally spread is the derivative process," and LZ76 asks "how many distinct sub-patterns does the median-binarised process produce." There is no monotone transformation between them.

## What the live-smoke numbers actually mean

Re-reading the two top-2 numbers with all of the above in hand:

- **claude-code: lzNorm=0.9426, lzCount=11, tenure 72 days.** A 72-day binary stream with 11 distinct LZ76 phrases is highly incompressible relative to its length. The interpretation is that even at a coarse daily-token resolution, claude-code's traffic does not settle into a small repeated pattern. The 0.9426 normalised value sits 5.74% below the binary-iid asymptote, which is consistent with mild structure — perhaps a weekly cycle or a few extended quiet streaks — but not with a strong periodic regime. If claude-code traffic were strictly weekly-periodic at the daily-token level, the binary stream would have a 7-day cycle and LZ76 would parse it into roughly log2(72/7) ≈ 3 phrases, lzNorm somewhere near 0.3.

- **vscode-other: lzNorm=0.9721, lzCount=32, tenure 265 days.** Despite having ~3.7x the days, vscode-other's normalised LZ76 is higher than claude-code's, not lower. This is the more interesting half of the live-smoke. If both carriers were drawing from the same underlying iid distribution at the median-binarised daily-token level, both lzNorms would converge toward 1.0 from below as length grows, and you'd expect the longer carrier to be closer to 1.0. That is exactly what the numbers show: vscode-other at 0.9721 is closer to the asymptote than claude-code at 0.9426. The 32 phrases over 265 days is the right rough order of magnitude for a near-iid binary process (the LZ76 phrase-count for an iid binary sequence of length n grows as roughly n / log2(n), so 265 / log2(265) ≈ 33, and 32 is essentially on top of that prediction).

The combined reading is "both carriers, at the daily-token resolution after median binarisation, look essentially iid, with claude-code showing slightly more structure (5.74% sub-asymptotic) than vscode-other (2.79% sub-asymptotic)." This is a strong claim and worth checking against the prior axes. From the recent history-jsonl tail, the same two carriers' axis-79 Hjorth Mobility was claude-code mobV=1.1628 vs vscode-other mobV=1.3103, and axis-80 Hjorth Complexity was claude-code=1.5319 vs vscode-other=1.3028 — a partial inversion: claude-code higher on complexity but lower on mobility. The TKE on axis-81 was claude-code tkeNorm=0.5978 vs vscode-other 0.9431 — vscode-other much higher fractional energy. None of these earlier numbers predict the LZ76 ranking. The ordering at the LZ76 axis is genuinely a new piece of information: vscode-other's daily-token stream is more LZ76-incompressible than claude-code's, even though it is less Hjorth-Complex, less spectrally-spread on derivative-of-derivative grounds, and substantially higher on Teager-Kaiser fractional energy. This is the orthogonality story made concrete.

## What the +29 tests cover and why it's the smallest delta in six releases

The test counts across the recent axis releases form a pattern. Axis-80 brought +23 (8931→8954). Axis-81 brought +32 (8951→8983, with 3 of those being parametric-sweep + interior-count + N=4-boundary refinement). Axis-82 brought +37 (8983→9020, with 5 being cubic-single-inflection + 1e12/1e-12 stability + sort-tiebreak refinement). Axis-83 brought +29 (9042→9071), with no separately-noted refinement commit beyond the headline HEAD=8370b08.

The reason +29 is small relative to the prior three releases is that LZ76 is a textbook algorithm with a single canonical reference implementation (the 1976 paper itself is essentially a constructive proof and a worked example), and the four design choices documented in section 1 are all binary-pinned: there is no continuous parameter to sweep, no embedding dimension to pick, no tolerance band to tune. The test suite for axis-83 has to cover (a) the four canonical example sequences from the LZ76 paper and their hand-computed phrase-counts, (b) the median-binarisation correctness on small worked examples, (c) the zero-gap-fill behaviour on series with deliberately-inserted holes, (d) the n / log2(n) asymptotic for iid binary input at sequence lengths matching the carrier tenures pew sees in practice, and (e) the per-carrier integration tests that wire the new axis into the queue.jsonl ingestion path. That's enough to fill +29 tests but not enough to need parametric sweeps or boundary-condition refinement of the kind axes 81 and 82 needed.

## Where axis-83 fits in the larger 83-axis picture and what comes next

With axis-83 in, the daily-token complexity-and-derivative chain is closed across thirteen axes (67 ACF, 68 spectral, 69 frequency, 70 PE, 71 R/S Hurst, 72 DFA-alpha, 73 SampEn, 74 Higuchi FD, 75 Katz FD, 76 Petrosian FD, 77 Sevcik FD, 78 box-count FD, 79 Hjorth Mobility, 80 Hjorth Complexity, 81 TKE, 82 curvature sign-change, 83 LZ76). The remaining slots in the 83-axis range have already been used by the shape battery (32-66) and the entropy/derivative/algorithmic battery just closed.

The structural argument for orthogonality across this thirteen-axis closure is strongest at the boundaries between sub-batteries — the entropy axes don't collide with the FD axes, the FD axes don't collide with the derivative axes, the derivative axes don't collide with the nonlinear cross-product (TKE) or sign-change (Petrosian, axis 82) axes, and the algorithmic-complexity axis (LZ76, axis 83) doesn't collide with any of them because it is the only axis that does dictionary-based phrase-counting on a median-binarised stream. The two live-smoke numerics (0.9426 vs 0.9721, with the longer-tenure carrier closer to the iid asymptote) are the empirical confirmation that the new axis is producing information not redundant with what came before, which is the working definition of "this axis was worth shipping" in pew's release-gating doctrine. From here, the natural next step — already foreshadowed in the templates HEAD=90fe3a5 release lineage and the digest ADD-239 sha=652c4bc 6-carrier-silent floor — is to start using axis-83 as a carrier-stability indicator at digest-time, where a sustained drop in lzNorm below ~0.85 on a particular carrier would constitute a strong signal that the carrier has entered a structured-not-iid regime worth investigating in its own right.
