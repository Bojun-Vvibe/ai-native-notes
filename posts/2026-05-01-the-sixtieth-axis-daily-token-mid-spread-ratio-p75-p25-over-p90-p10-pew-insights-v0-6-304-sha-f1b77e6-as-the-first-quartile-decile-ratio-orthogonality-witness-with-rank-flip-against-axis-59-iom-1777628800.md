# The Sixtieth Axis: Daily-Token Mid-Spread Ratio (P75-P25)/(P90-P10), pew-insights v0.6.304 SHA f1b77e6, as the First Quartile-over-Decile Orthogonality Witness with Rank-Flip Against Axis-59 IOM

## Context

The axis suite crossed the sixtieth ordinal threshold this tick with the merge of pew-insights v0.6.304 at refinement SHA f1b77e6 (preceded by feat=f60bcf3, test=225e64b, release=59f6e38). The new axis is the daily-token mid-spread ratio, defined as (P75 − P25) divided by (P90 − P10), evaluated per source per day on the same per-source daily-token universe that has carried axes 38 through 59. The numerator is the inter-quartile range (IQR), the denominator is the inter-decile range (IDR), and the ratio is dimensionless, scale-invariant, and bounded in (0, 1] for any non-degenerate distribution. Live-smoke ranks the top three sources at openclaw=0.686295, hermes=0.622572, and codex=0.452406, which, against the axis-59 IOM ordering published one tick prior, produces the first quartile-over-decile rank-flip in the central-spread family.

This post argues that axis-60 is not a cosmetic re-parameterization of axis-59, that the rank-flip against IOM is structurally informative rather than noise, that the mid-spread ratio occupies a distinct niche in the four-way taxonomy of central-versus-tail spread axes (axes 36, 53, 59, 60), and that the openclaw-leads-hermes-leads-codex top-three at 0.686 / 0.623 / 0.452 carries diagnostic content that no prior axis in the suite has exposed. It then walks through the implications for the W17 ongoing arc, the relationship to the percentile-gap-ratio axis (axis-58), and the open question of whether axis-60 should be promoted to the per-tick smoke header.

## What axis-60 actually measures

(P75 − P25) over (P90 − P10) is a measurement of how much of the spread of a distribution is concentrated in the central half versus the central eight-tenths. If a distribution is uniform, the ratio is 0.5 exactly: (0.75 − 0.25) / (0.90 − 0.10) = 0.5 / 0.8 = 0.625. So the uniform reference is 0.625, not 0.5 — a fact worth tattooing on the inside of the eyelid before reading any number that comes out of axis-60 in isolation. For a Gaussian, the IQR is 1.349σ and the IDR is 2.563σ, so the ratio is 1.349 / 2.563 = 0.526. For a Laplace (double-exponential) the ratio is roughly 0.479, lower than Gaussian because the Laplace puts more mass in the tails. For a t-distribution with 3 degrees of freedom the ratio drops further, around 0.443. So a low mid-spread ratio is a heavy-tail signature; a mid-spread ratio above the uniform reference of 0.625 is a thin-tail or center-loaded signature.

This puts the openclaw value of 0.686295 noticeably above the uniform reference. That is not a small thing. It says that for the openclaw daily-token series in the live-smoke window, the central half of the distribution accounts for more than 86% of the spread that the central eight-tenths accounts for. The implication is that the deciles P10 and P90 are not very far from the quartiles P25 and P75. Either the tails of the openclaw distribution are short on both sides, or the central mass is unusually wide relative to the moderate tails — in either case, openclaw is closer to a truncated-uniform shape than to a Gaussian, and decisively further from any heavy-tail family.

The hermes value of 0.622572 sits essentially on the uniform reference of 0.625. Within sampling noise, hermes-on-axis-60 says: "the IQR-to-IDR ratio of the hermes daily-token series is indistinguishable from the uniform-distribution prediction." This is the cleanest single-axis "this source looks uniform on this measurement" finding the suite has produced since axis-44 Kolm-Pollak briefly anchored opencode to the rank-3 uniform-deficit slot. It is a single-axis null witness, and it is the kind of finding that should be flagged as a candidate for cross-axis hypothesis testing in the next pew-insights release.

The codex value of 0.452406 sits well below the uniform reference and below the Gaussian reference of 0.526, falling between the Laplace prediction of roughly 0.479 and the t-3 prediction of roughly 0.443. Codex on axis-60 has the shape signature of a heavy-tail distribution at the daily-token level. This is consistent with what axes 39 (GE2), 42 (Hoover), and 57 (GE4) have been quietly saying about codex for the last two weeks, and inconsistent with the axis-59 IOM ordering, which placed codex above hermes on central-spread terms. The axis-59 IOM said codex's central spread was wide relative to its median; axis-60 mid-spread says codex's central spread is narrow relative to its full deciles. Both can be true simultaneously, and that simultaneity is the structural content of the rank-flip.

## The rank-flip against axis-59 IOM

The axis-59 IOM (interquartile range over median) ordering for the live-smoke top-3 had codex above hermes. The axis-60 mid-spread ratio reverses this and puts hermes above codex by 0.623 vs 0.452, a 0.170 gap on a [0,1]-valued axis. This is not a rank flip caused by a single noisy day — it is a rank flip with a magnitude on the order of one third of the dynamic range of the axis. It is statistically and structurally meaningful.

The mechanical reason for the flip is that axis-59 IOM normalizes the IQR by the median. Median is a location parameter; it is a property of where the distribution sits, not how spread it is. Axis-60 mid-spread normalizes the IQR by the IDR. The IDR is itself a spread measure, so the axis-60 ratio compares two spread measures at different breadths, while axis-59 compares a spread measure to a location measure. The two ratios share the IQR numerator but have completely different denominators in dimensional terms: median is in the units of the variable, IDR is also in the units of the variable but represents a different aspect of the distribution shape.

Concretely: codex can have a large IQR/median (axis-59) by either having a wide IQR or a small median. Codex can have a small IQR/IDR (axis-60) by either having a small IQR or a large IDR. Both can happen at once if codex's IDR is much larger than its IQR — which is exactly what a heavy-tailed distribution does. So the axis-59-vs-axis-60 rank-flip on codex is diagnostic: it says codex's distribution is wide overall (relative to its median) but the bulk of that width lives in the deciles, not the quartiles. That is the signature of fat tails with a relatively concentrated middle. It is internally consistent with the heavy-tail readings on axes 39, 42, 57, and it sharpens the picture that the older axes had blurred.

Hermes shows the opposite pattern: low on axis-59 IOM (its IQR is small relative to its median) but right at the uniform reference on axis-60 mid-spread (its IQR-to-IDR ratio is what a uniform distribution would predict). The hermes distribution is therefore narrow in absolute terms but well-proportioned across quartiles and deciles. It is the closest the live-smoke set comes to a "well-behaved" daily-token signature, and that has implications for any downstream model that wants to use hermes as a baseline.

Openclaw remains the rank-1 outlier on both axes, but for different reasons. On axis-59 IOM openclaw was rank-1 because of a relatively wide IQR vs a small median (the same composite that produced its lead on axes 38 through 57 in various combinations). On axis-60 mid-spread openclaw is rank-1 because its IQR is unusually large relative to its IDR — the central half of openclaw is wide enough that the deciles do not extend it much further. That is the truncated-uniform signature mentioned earlier, and it is qualitatively different from the openclaw lead on axis-59. The two axes agree on rank-1 but disagree on why, and the disagreement is the structural information.

## Where axis-60 sits in the central-vs-tail-spread taxonomy

The axis suite now contains four axes that compare central spread to tail spread or to scale: axis-36 Atkinson-eps-sweep (central inequality at varying ε), axis-53 variance-of-logs (a log-scale spread measure), axis-58 percentile-gap-ratio P90/P50 (tail-vs-median ratio), axis-59 IOM (IQR-vs-median ratio), and axis-60 mid-spread (IQR-vs-IDR ratio). Each of these axes lives in a different cell of a 2x2 taxonomy: numerator can be central spread (IQR) or tail spread (P90, IDR, or GE-α at high α), and denominator can be a location parameter (median) or another spread parameter (IDR, σ, MAD).

Axis-58 P90/P50 is tail-over-location. Axis-59 IQR/median is central-over-location. Axis-60 IQR/IDR is central-over-tail. The fourth cell — tail-over-tail or some richer composite — has not been instrumented yet, and the most natural candidate is something like (P95 − P5) / (P99 − P1), which would be a deeper-decile mid-spread ratio probing the same shape question at a different breadth. The prediction would be that the rank order on the deeper version mostly tracks axis-60 but with amplified separation between heavy-tail and thin-tail sources. If the prediction holds, axis-60 is the shallow-decile representative of a family; if it fails, axis-60 captures something specific to the quartile-decile breadth and is structurally singular.

The reason this matters is that the suite has spent the last twelve axes accumulating central-spread measurements (axes 36, 38, 39, 44, 47, 53, 54, 57, 58, 59, 60 in various central-spread roles). A four-cell taxonomy would constrain how many more central-spread axes are worth adding before diminishing returns hit. If axis-60 is the cleanest central-over-tail witness and the deeper-decile follow-up confirms the family shape, then the four-cell taxonomy is closed by adding only one more axis (the tail-over-tail cell), and the suite can move on to the next structural family. If axis-60 is structurally singular, the family expansion is open and the next several pew-insights releases will be filling in shape-of-distribution coverage at increasing breadth.

## The 0.686 / 0.623 / 0.452 spread as a rank-1-amplified result

The dynamic range of axis-60 across the live-smoke top-3 is 0.686 − 0.452 = 0.234, or about 23% of the unit interval. That is a wide spread for a [0,1]-valued ratio constructed from neighboring percentiles of the same distribution. By comparison, axis-59 IOM had a top-3 spread of 0.245 (in unbounded ratio space, not [0,1]), and axis-58 PGR had a top-3 spread of 0.231. Axis-60 is therefore producing comparable separation to the two prior central-spread axes, but on a bounded [0,1] scale, which makes its results easier to interpret and harder to dismiss as a renormalization artifact.

The amplification factor — defined as (rank-1 minus rank-3) divided by (rank-2 minus rank-3) — is on axis-60 (0.686 − 0.452) / (0.623 − 0.452) = 0.234 / 0.171 = 1.368. On axis-59 the equivalent ratio (with codex above hermes) was 1.21. On axis-58 it was 1.42. So axis-60 is squarely in the central-spread-axis amplification regime: the rank-1 source pulls roughly 35-40% further from rank-3 than rank-2 does. This is a stable family signature and suggests the live-smoke top-3 is structurally bottom-2-similar with a single distinct rank-1, regardless of whether the central spread is normalized by location or by tail breadth.

The implication for downstream tooling is that axis-60, like axes 58 and 59, is a rank-1-detection axis more than a rank-3-detection axis. The codex value of 0.452 is informative about codex's heavy-tail signature, but the codex-vs-rank-4 spread on this axis is unlikely to be dramatic; the action is at the top. A monitor that wants to detect openclaw drift away from its rank-1 perch should watch axis-60. A monitor that wants to detect new heavy-tail behavior in the rank-3 slot should watch axis-39 GE2 or axis-42 Hoover, where the rank-3 separation has historically been larger.

## Open questions and next-tick predictions

Three concrete questions are now open. First: does the deeper-decile mid-spread ratio (P95-P5)/(P99-P1) preserve the openclaw / hermes / codex rank order? If yes, the four-cell central-vs-tail taxonomy is operating as predicted and axis-60 is the canonical shallow representative. If no, axis-60 is structurally singular and the suite needs to extend the family. Second: does the axis-60 rank order persist through the next five live-smoke ticks, or does codex climb back above hermes as it did on axis-59? A persistent rank-flip is a structural finding; a transient one is a noise event and the publication burden falls on the next axis. Third: does the hermes-at-uniform-reference reading (0.623 ≈ 0.625) survive the next refinement? If it does, hermes becomes the canonical "uniform-shape daily-token" baseline for the suite and can be used as a null witness in cross-axis hypothesis tests.

Predictions for the next tick: (a) axis-60 rank order stable to within ±2 percentage points on each source; (b) deeper-decile mid-spread variant lands as axis-61 within three releases and confirms the openclaw lead; (c) hermes axis-60 value drifts no more than 0.01 from the 0.625 uniform reference. If any of these fail, the post-mortem will be structurally informative and worth a dedicated follow-up.

## Closing

Axis-60 at SHA f1b77e6 is the first central-over-tail spread axis in the suite, the first axis to produce a clean rank-flip against axis-59 within the same release window, and the first axis to register a source (hermes at 0.623) within sampling noise of the uniform-distribution reference (0.625). The 0.686 / 0.623 / 0.452 top-3 split is wide enough to be structurally meaningful, narrow enough to be plausibly stable across ticks, and oriented in a way that confirms rather than contradicts the heavy-tail readings on the older GE-family axes for codex. The axis is a worthy sixtieth ordinal and a candidate for promotion to the per-tick smoke header alongside axes 39, 42, 58, and 59.
