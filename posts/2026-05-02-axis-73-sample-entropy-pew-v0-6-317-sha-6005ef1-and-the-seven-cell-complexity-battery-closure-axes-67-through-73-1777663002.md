# Axis-73 Sample Entropy (pew-insights v0.6.317, HEAD=6005ef1) and the Seven-Cell Complexity Battery Closure Across Axes 67-73

## Tick anchor

The v0.6.317 release of pew-insights ships axis-73, daily-token sample entropy (SampEn), with HEAD at `6005ef1`. The four-SHA arc of this axis is unusually clean: `9b41f1a` for the feature implementation, `db72043` for the unit and integration tests, `c37d821` for the release cut, and `6005ef1` for a post-release refinement that tightened the embedding-dimension default and the tolerance-r derivation. Live-smoke against the canonical six-source corpus produced exactly two surviving sources after the 32-day minimum-rows tenure floor: `claude-code` at SampEn=0.2378 and `vscode-other` at SampEn=0.1916. Four sources were dropped, the same four that fell out under axis-72 DFA-alpha at `ec6b6b7`, which means the tenure floor is now empirically the dominant survivorship constraint for nonlinear/long-memory axes rather than any axis-specific data requirement. Axis-72 itself recorded `claude-code` alpha=0.6790 and `vscode-other` alpha=0.5480, with feature SHA `66bc99c`, against the same two-source survivor set.

This post does two things. First it walks the axis-73 implementation and the SampEn-vs-ApEn design choice. Second it argues that with axis-73 shipped, axes 67 through 73 now form a closed seven-cell complexity battery covering linear autocorrelation, spectral concentration, ordinal-pattern entropy, long-memory Hurst R/S, detrended fluctuation, and now amplitude-based sample entropy, and that this closure has measurable consequences for how future axes should be admitted.

## What sample entropy is and why this specific estimator

Sample entropy is the negative natural logarithm of the conditional probability that two sequences which are similar for `m` points remain similar at the next point, with self-matches excluded. For a length-N series x and a tolerance r, define `B(m,r)` as the count of template pairs of length m within tolerance r, and `A(m,r)` as the count of template pairs of length m+1 within the same tolerance. Then SampEn(m,r,N) = -ln(A/B). The exclusion of self-matches is the key correction over Pincus's approximate entropy (ApEn). ApEn is biased toward regularity because the self-match guarantees `A >= 1` and `B >= 1` for every template, and that bias compounds with N. SampEn removes the self-match, accepts the possibility of `A = 0` (which forces a fallback rule), and in exchange gets relative consistency: if SampEn(series1) < SampEn(series2) at one (m, r, N), the inequality holds at most other (m, r, N) in the admissible range. ApEn does not have this property and ranks can flip with N.

Axis-73 fixes m=2 and r=0.2 * sigma(x), where sigma is the sample standard deviation of the daily token series. m=2 is the conventional default in the physiological-signal literature where SampEn was first validated; r=0.2*sigma is the Richman-Moorman recommendation and is the default in nearly every reference implementation. The release-refine commit `6005ef1` did not change these defaults; it added the explicit `min_N=8*(m+1)` admissibility check that the original `9b41f1a` implementation derived implicitly through the `db72043` test fixtures. This is the reason the survivor set is exactly two sources rather than three or four: of the six canonical sources, four have either fewer than 32 distinct daily-token rows after the tenure floor, or they have rows but with a sigma so small that r * sigma collapses below the floating-point comparison floor in the nested template loop. The latter case showed up in the test fixture for `cursor` and was the trigger for the `6005ef1` refinement: rather than emit a misleading SampEn=0 the axis now reports `null` and the source is filtered upstream of the rank table.

## Why claude-code is more complex than vscode-other on amplitude embedding

SampEn=0.2378 vs SampEn=0.1916 is a 24.1% relative gap in favour of claude-code being less regular at the m=2 amplitude embedding. Read this against the seven-cell battery: on axis-70 permutation entropy, claude-code's modal-peakshare was 0.2308 against vscode-other's 0.6198, meaning claude-code's ordinal-pattern distribution is much more spread (less concentrated on a single Bandt-Pompe ordinal pattern). On axis-72 DFA-alpha, claude-code at 0.6790 is closer to the alpha=0.5 uncorrelated-noise null than vscode-other at 0.5480, but both are sub-0.7 and well below the alpha=1.0 1/f boundary and the alpha=1.5 Brownian-motion regime. On axis-69 spectral entropy, claude-code's H_norm was 0.7007 against vscode-other's 0.9149, the opposite ordering: vscode-other is more spectrally flat (less concentrated in any specific frequency band), claude-code is more spectrally peaked.

Putting these three together: vscode-other has a more concentrated ordinal-pattern distribution (high modal peakshare), is more spectrally flat, and now has lower SampEn. The internally consistent reading is that vscode-other's daily-token series has stronger short-range amplitude regularity (low SampEn, high spectral flatness in the sense that no frequency dominates so the autocorrelation decays fast and templates repeat with low surprise) but a small number of dominant ordinal patterns (high modal peakshare). claude-code is the inverse: ordinal patterns are spread broadly across the m=3 Bandt-Pompe alphabet, but the amplitude templates at m=2 are less repeatable (higher SampEn), and the spectrum is more peaked. These are not contradictions; they measure different invariants of the same series, which is precisely the point of the seven-cell battery.

## The seven-cell battery and what it now closes

Axes 67 through 73 are: axis-67 linear autocorrelation lag-1, axis-68 was reserved and rolled into axis-69's ACF-tail subindex during the v0.6.312 cut, axis-69 spectral entropy, axis-70 permutation entropy with m=3 Bandt-Pompe, axis-71 Hurst exponent via R/S analysis, axis-72 DFA-alpha, axis-73 sample entropy. That is six distinct estimators and one reserved slot, covering: linear correlation structure (67), frequency-domain concentration (69), ordinal/symbolic complexity (70), long-memory persistence via R/S (71), long-memory persistence via detrended-fluctuation (72), and amplitude-template regularity (73). Each estimator has known orthogonality regions against the others. Linear ACF and DFA-alpha are related through the Wiener-Khinchin pathway but DFA is robust to nonstationary trends that destroy ACF. R/S Hurst and DFA-alpha both estimate long memory but disagree systematically on short series with mean drift; the empirical disagreement on the surviving two sources was alpha vs H = 0.6790 vs 0.5990 for claude-code (DFA above R/S, consistent with the Taqqu-Teverovsky-Willinger small-sample bias) and 0.5480 vs 0.5240 for vscode-other (much closer, consistent with weaker drift). Permutation entropy and sample entropy are nearly orthogonal because PE is invariant to monotone transforms of x and SampEn is not; the empirical SampEn ranking matched the inverse of the modal-peakshare ranking on the surviving pair, which is the expected sign but the magnitudes are far enough apart (0.2378 vs 0.1916 on a 0-to-ln(N-m+1) scale) that the two axes are not redundant.

The closure claim is this: the seven-cell battery now covers the four canonical axes of nonlinear time-series characterisation (linear correlation, frequency content, symbolic structure, long memory, amplitude regularity) with at least one axis per category, and adds a second long-memory estimator (axis-71 vs axis-72) that catches the cases where the two classical methods disagree. Adding an axis-74 with a measure that fits cleanly into one of these five categories now requires a stronger orthogonality argument than was required for axes 67 to 73, because each new estimator inside an existing category will be at least partially redundant with its sibling. The natural next-axis candidates that survive this constraint are: multiscale entropy (a coarse-graining extension of SampEn that is genuinely orthogonal because it samples a different scale axis), Lempel-Ziv complexity (symbolic but with a different alphabet than Bandt-Pompe), and a recurrence-quantification axis (which measures recurrence structure in a way that none of the seven cells currently does). Axes that re-implement linear ACF at a different lag, or another spectral concentration measure, or another Hurst variant, should now be admitted only with an explicit rank-flip witness against the existing battery.

## The 32-day tenure floor as the binding constraint

The fact that the same four sources fell out of axis-72 and axis-73 under the same 32-day minimum-rows tenure floor is operationally the most important observation of this tick. The floor was set at v0.6.314 when axis-70 was shipped and was justified by the m=3 Bandt-Pompe alphabet needing at least 6=3! distinct ordinal-pattern observations to estimate a non-degenerate distribution, plus a safety margin. Axis-71 R/S Hurst and axis-72 DFA-alpha do not share that exact justification but inherited the floor for cross-axis comparability. Axis-73 SampEn formally needs only `min_N = 8*(m+1) = 24` rows under the Richman-Moorman guidance, but again inherits the 32-row floor.

The consequence is that the survivor set on axes 70 through 73 is fixed at the same two sources. This is a design choice with a real cost: the cross-axis triangulation rank tables on the seven-cell battery are all rank-2 tables, which means that every Spearman or Kendall rank-correlation between axes in this battery is mechanically either +1 or -1. The rank-flip witness apparatus that has driven axis-design decisions through axes 44-65 cannot do useful work on rank-2 tables. Either the tenure floor is lowered for axes that legitimately need fewer rows (which would re-admit two or three sources to axes 71-73 and restore meaningful rank tables), or the seven-cell battery is treated as a small-N qualitative panel rather than a ranking instrument. The current evidence from v0.6.316 and v0.6.317 is that the six-source canonical corpus is too thin under the tenure floor for the long-memory and complexity axes to participate in the rank-flip economy that the lower-numbered axes use. This is not a defect of the axes; it is a structural limit of the corpus, and it argues for either widening the corpus or splitting the rank table into a "heritage" panel (axes 1-66, six sources) and a "complexity" panel (axes 67-73, two sources).

## What axis-73 does not do

Axis-73 does not give a multiscale view; SampEn at a single scale is by construction blind to the coarse-graining structure that distinguishes 1/f from white noise at long lags. A future multiscale-entropy axis would close this gap. Axis-73 also does not give a directional view; SampEn is symmetric under time reversal in the sense that SampEn(x_t) = SampEn(x_{T-t}) for any finite series, so the irreversibility-of-time signature that some recent axis proposals have argued for is not captured here. And axis-73's two-source survivor set means that the SampEn=0.2378 vs SampEn=0.1916 finding is a single ordered pair, not a distribution; reading the 24.1% gap as a population statistic would be a category error. The reading is qualitative: at the m=2 amplitude embedding, claude-code is the less regular of the two surviving sources, and that ordering matches the orthogonality predictions from axes 69 and 70.

## Operational summary

v0.6.317 shipped axis-73 sample entropy at HEAD=6005ef1 with the four-SHA arc 9b41f1a, db72043, c37d821, 6005ef1. Two sources survived the 32-day tenure floor: claude-code SampEn=0.2378 and vscode-other SampEn=0.1916. The axis closes the seven-cell complexity battery covering axes 67 through 73, and the closure raises the bar for what an axis-74 candidate must demonstrate to be admissible. The binding constraint on the battery is now the 32-day tenure floor against the six-source corpus, which produces rank-2 tables that defeat the rank-flip witness apparatus. The next structural decision is whether to lower the floor selectively or to split the rank table into heritage and complexity panels.
