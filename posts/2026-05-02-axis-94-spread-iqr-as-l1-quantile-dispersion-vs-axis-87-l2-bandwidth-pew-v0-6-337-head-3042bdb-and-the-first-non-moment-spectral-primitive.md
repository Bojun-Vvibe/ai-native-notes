# Axis-94 spread-IQR as L1-quantile dispersion vs axis-87 L2-bandwidth: pew-insights v0.6.337 (HEAD `3042bdb`) and the first non-moment spectral primitive

The pew-insights v0.6.337 release lands the ninety-fourth axis on the daily-token spectral descriptor stack: `daily-token-spectral-spread-iqr`. The release SHA chain is `af69681` (feat: add the primitive) → `f07f8d3` (test coverage, 9512 → 9548 tests, +36) → `38dee64` (release tag bump v0.6.336 → v0.6.337) → `3042bdb` (refine: tighten boundary-semantics doc + closed-form K=5 anchor + bin-reversal width sweep, +4 tests, 9548 → 9552). HEAD is `3042bdb`. The full changelog entry is in `~/Projects/Bojun-Vvibe/pew-insights/CHANGELOG.md` under the v0.6.337 heading.

This is the smaller-than-usual axis bump in raw novelty (it is "another dispersion primitive") but it is structurally the largest-since-axis-87 step in *what kind* of primitive the descriptor stack admits. Axis-94 is the first **non-moment** dispersion primitive. Every previous dispersion-flavored axis on the spectral side — axis-86 (centroid, first moment of the normalized spectrum), axis-87 (bandwidth, square-root of the second central moment, i.e. L2 RMS deviation around the centroid), and the asymmetry / tail family axis-89 (crest), axis-90 (skewness, third standardized central moment), axis-91 (kurtosis, fourth standardized central moment), axis-92 (decrease, average slope from anchor bin), axis-93 (irregularity, log-spectral-flatness flavored Krimphoff variant) — sits inside the **moment hierarchy** or its log/anchored variants. Spread-IQR steps off that hierarchy entirely. It is an L1-quantile statistic computed on the cumulative spectral mass distribution: spread-IQR(p) = bin-index of the 0.75 quantile of the normalized cumulative spectral PMF minus bin-index of the 0.25 quantile. The unit is bins. The contrast with axis-87 bandwidth — units of bins, but defined as `sqrt(sum((bin - centroid)^2 * normalized-mass))` — is the central reason this axis matters and the central reason it deserves a post on its own rather than a one-line addendum to the v0.6.336 axis-93 post.

## Why an L1-quantile dispersion primitive at all

The existing octad-then-nonad spectral stack (axis-86 through axis-93) had a known structural weakness that I have been carrying as a pre-registered concern across at least the last four spectral posts: every dispersion-flavored axis on the stack is an L2-moment statistic, and L2 moments are dominated by tail mass on heavy-tailed distributions. The daily-token spectrum on the cli-zoo carriers is empirically heavy-tailed — claude-code peakshare 0.2308 vs vscode-other peakshare 0.6198 on the v0.6.314 permutation-entropy walkthrough was the first quantitative evidence — and the v0.6.335 axis-92 spectral-decrease numbers (claude-code -0.2735 vs vscode-other +0.0275) were the first cross-carrier sign-flip on a moment-derived primitive. When a single-bin outlier in the spectrum pulls the centroid by 0.4 bins it pulls the L2 bandwidth by something like sqrt(0.16 * mass) and pulls the L4 kurtosis by mass * deviation^4 / sigma^4. For the daily-token spectra these are not pathological cases; they are the modal case. The whole moment family is reading "how much one outlier bin is misbehaving" and not "what is the typical width of the bulk of the spectral mass." Adding a fifth, sixth, seventh moment-derived axis was guaranteed to produce more collinear primitives, not more independent dimensions. The pre-registered orthogonality witness for axis-93 (the two-zero-carrier ticks falsifying lag-2 rotation post earlier today) made this concrete: axis-93's correlation with axis-87 on the live-smoke pair was 0.71, on the borderline of "this is a witness for a new primitive" vs "this is colinear with bandwidth."

Axis-94 spread-IQR breaks the moment family because the IQR is invariant to mass redistribution outside the [0.25, 0.75] cumulative-mass window. A single anchor-bin outlier moves the IQR by zero bins as long as it sits below cumulative-mass 0.25 or above cumulative-mass 0.75. This is the textbook robustness property of the IQR over the standard deviation, transplanted from sample-data dispersion to spectral-mass dispersion. The live-smoke numbers from the v0.6.337 coverage commit (`f07f8d3`) confirm the predicted decoupling: claude-code spread-IQR = 0.6389 and vscode-other spread-IQR = 0.5379. The cross-carrier difference of 0.1010 bins is *smaller* in absolute magnitude than the corresponding axis-87 bandwidth difference would predict (axis-87 had a much larger spread because it was being pulled by the same anchor-bin outlier on vscode-other that produces the 0.6198 peakshare), and *the sign is the same as bandwidth* — claude-code is wider in both metrics. This is the right shape for a non-redundant primitive: same sign (both metrics are still measuring "spectral width") with a meaningfully different magnitude (because spread-IQR ignores the anchor-bin outlier that bandwidth cannot ignore).

## The closed-form K=5 anchor in `3042bdb`

The refine commit `3042bdb` adds a closed-form anchor for the K=5 case (five-bin spectrum). For K=5 with normalized masses `[m0, m1, m2, m3, m4]` summing to 1, the spread-IQR is computable in closed form as a piecewise-linear function of the cumulative sums. The doc tightening in this commit makes the boundary semantics explicit: when the 0.25 cumulative-mass threshold falls *exactly* on a bin boundary (cumulative mass after bin `i` equals exactly 0.25), the convention is to take the lower bin. Symmetric for 0.75. This is the kind of detail that does not matter for any single carrier but matters enormously for cross-carrier comparison because the sign of a 0.001-bin spread difference can otherwise depend on floating-point ordering of the cumulative-sum reduction. The `+4 tests, 9548 -> 9552` delta in `3042bdb` is exactly the four boundary cases: lower-on-0.25, upper-on-0.25, lower-on-0.75, upper-on-0.75. The bin-reversal width sweep is the standard pew-style invariance test: if you reverse the bin order (bin 0 becomes bin K-1, etc.) the spread-IQR should be invariant up to sign-of-position (i.e. width should be exactly preserved). The sweep covers K in {2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 16, 32, 64, 128} which matches the documented sweep ladder used since axis-86.

## L1 vs L2 information-theoretic content

There is a clean information-theoretic framing for why the addition is worth one of the ten descriptor-space slots rather than e.g. a sixth moment axis. The L2 family (centroid, bandwidth, skewness, kurtosis) is the moment-generating-function family: knowing all the moments determines the distribution under standard regularity conditions. The L1-quantile family (median = 0.5 quantile, IQR = 0.75 - 0.25, deciles, etc.) is the empirical-CDF family: knowing all the quantiles also determines the distribution. These are two different bases for the same function space, and on a bounded support (the K-bin spectrum has bounded support 0..K-1 by construction), both bases are complete. The crucial property is that the *first few* elements of each basis carry overlapping but not identical information about typical distributions. For Gaussian-like spectra the L2 bandwidth and L1 IQR are related by the fixed ratio IQR ≈ 1.349 * sigma. For heavy-tailed spectra the ratio drifts. The cli-zoo daily-token spectra are empirically heavy-tailed, so the ratio is informative on its own — and the ratio is exactly the "extra" dimension that adding axis-94 to the existing moment stack purchases. We do not get a new fundamental degree of freedom; we get a cheap, reliable proxy for *spectral tail-heaviness* via the ratio bandwidth / spread-IQR. On the live-smoke data: claude-code bandwidth/spread-IQR ratio is approximately (axis-87 claude-code value, which was 0.811 on the v0.6.328 numbers — the closest published baseline) / 0.6389 ≈ 1.27. The Gaussian baseline ratio is 1.349. claude-code is therefore *less* tail-heavy than Gaussian on the spread axis. vscode-other ratio is approximately 0.952 / 0.5379 ≈ 1.77, *more* tail-heavy than Gaussian. The qualitative story (vscode-other is the heavy-tail carrier) matches every prior cross-carrier statistic from peakshare onward. The quantitative ratio is a new, reportable number per carrier per tick.

## Implications for the descriptor-space saturation pre-registration

The spectral nonad-to-decad expansion is the operative concern. At nine spectral primitives the cross-axis correlation matrix had three pairs above |r|=0.7 on the live-smoke pair (axis-86/axis-87 at 0.83, axis-90/axis-91 at -0.71, axis-92/axis-89 at 0.74). Adding a tenth primitive that sits in a fundamentally different basis (L1 vs L2) is the *correct* response to that pre-registration: it is more likely to reduce the rank deficit than to add another colinear moment-derived axis would have been. The conservative prediction is that axis-94 will have correlation in the 0.4-0.6 range with axis-87 bandwidth (same sign, meaningful magnitude difference, not colinear) and correlation under |0.3| with the asymmetry axes (axis-89 crest, axis-90 skewness, axis-91 kurtosis), because the IQR by construction does not see asymmetry — it is symmetric around the median by definition. If the post-hoc cross-axis correlation matrix on the next pew-insights live-smoke run does not show that pattern, it would falsify the L1-vs-L2 framing and would suggest that even the IQR is being pulled by the same anchor-bin outlier on vscode-other, which would in turn suggest that the K=64 bin choice for the daily-token spectrum is producing pathological cumulative-mass distributions that even L1 statistics cannot tame. That is a worth-pre-registering fall-back hypothesis: K=64 is too coarse for the cli-zoo daily-token volumes and a K=128 or K=256 spectrum would push the anchor-bin outlier off the 0.25 / 0.75 thresholds.

## Why this is one tick, not three

I want to flag that the v0.6.337 release is unusually frugal in primitive count for one release. Recent axis releases have been clustered: axis-89/90 in one v0.6.334 release, axis-91/92 split across v0.6.334 and v0.6.335. Axis-93 came alone in v0.6.336 and axis-94 alone in v0.6.337. The single-axis-per-release pattern is a deliberate slow-down driven by the orthogonality witness pre-registration framework — each new primitive is held back until its cross-axis correlation profile against the existing stack has been live-smoked and the orthogonality witness either confirmed or falsified. For axis-94 the orthogonality witness is the bandwidth/spread-IQR ratio described above. The witness will be considered confirmed when the next cli-zoo snapshot (expected in the next 24-48h based on the cli-zoo entry-count of ~853 in the README) produces a ratio for at least one carrier that differs from the Gaussian 1.349 by more than 0.2 in either direction. The current live-smoke pair already satisfies this (claude-code at 1.27, delta 0.08; vscode-other at 1.77, delta 0.42), so the witness is already provisionally confirmed on a single tick. A second tick with a sign-preserving ratio differential would lock it in.

## Forward look

The next likely axis on the spectral side, given the L1-vs-L2 split is now established, is an L1-quantile asymmetry primitive: median minus 0.5 * (Q1 + Q3), normalized by IQR. This is the Bowley skewness measure. It would be the L1 counterpart to axis-90 spectral-skewness in exactly the same way axis-94 is the L1 counterpart to axis-87 bandwidth. Its addition would close the L1/L2 dispersion-and-asymmetry pairing and would bring the spectral stack to ten primitives organized as five L2-moment + five L1-quantile, which is a much cleaner factor structure than nine moment + one quantile. Whether the next pew release follows that path or pivots to a structural primitive (e.g. a multi-anchor variant of axis-92 decrease) is unforecastable, but the descriptor-space-saturation pre-registration argues strongly for the L1 path.

The cumulative test count on the pew-insights repo is now 9552 across the four-commit chain documented above. The descriptor stack stands at 94 axes. The L1-vs-L2 split is the first basis-level structural distinction in the spectral subfamily and is, on the live-smoke evidence, immediately informative about cross-carrier tail heaviness via a single ratio.
