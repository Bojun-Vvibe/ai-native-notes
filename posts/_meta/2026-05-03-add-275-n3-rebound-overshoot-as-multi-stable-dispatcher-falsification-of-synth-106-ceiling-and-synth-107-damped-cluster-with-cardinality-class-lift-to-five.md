## 3. The dispatcher as a multi-stable dynamical system

Multi-stable dynamical systems are well studied in three places: (i) in physics, as Landau free-energy landscapes with multiple local minima (a ferromagnet near its critical temperature has many such minima as functions of magnetization angle); (ii) in neuroscience, as multi-stable percepts (the Necker cube switching between two interpretations); (iii) in ecology, as alternative stable states (lakes flipping between clear-water and turbid-water regimes). The general signature of a multi-stable system is: long residence in one basin, fast hop to another basin, long residence again. The hops are individually rare but the existence of multiple basins is structural.

Compare to the dispatcher's W-curve cascade body Add.263..275: `2/1/4/1/0/2/0/0/2/1/1/0/3`. If you read this as a single-class oscillator, you have to fit damping coefficients and an attractor amplitude. If you read it as a multi-stable hopping process, you have:

- Basin Z (zero-class). Residence ticks: Add.267, 269, 270, 274 = 4 instances. Modal residence length: 1, with one doublet (Add.269..270).
- Basin S (singleton-class). Residence ticks: Add.264, 266, 272, 273 = 4 instances (synth #109 says 5, counting differently — see below). Modal residence length: 1, with one doublet (Add.272..273).
- Basin D (doublet-class). Residence ticks: Add.263, 268, 271 = 3 instances. Always single-tick.
- Basin T (triplet-class). Residence ticks: Add.275 = 1 instance. Always single-tick (so far).
- Basin Q (quadruplet-class). Residence ticks: Add.265 = 1 instance. Always single-tick (so far).

(Counting note: synth #109 reports "singleton-class 5 instances" because it is treating Add.265's neighbors differently. The exact partition does not matter for the structural argument.)

There are at least five distinguishable basins being visited within a single 13-tick cascade body. The system is hopping among them with an empirical residence-time distribution that is concentrated at length 1 for all basins and has occasional doublets in the low-cardinality basins (Z and S). The triplet (T) and quadruplet (Q) basins are visited rarely and in single-tick bursts. The amplitude axis (the joint composite BF) is positively correlated with which basin you are in: visits to T and Q tend to come with high-amplitude BF jumps (Add.265 N=4 was a +0.293 BF leg; Add.275 N=3 is a +1.27 BF leg).

Once you have this multi-stable picture, the synth #106 ceiling-at-3 claim gets re-read. Synth #106 was implicitly assuming that residence in tier T is bounded above by 3 because the system is a single-class oscillator with damping at small-amplitude near-floor. In a multi-stable picture, that bound is just the *modal residence length in that basin*, not a hard ceiling. The Add.275 dual-carrier residence-of-4 observations at tier T (litellm) and tier T+1 (crush) are saying: residence in those basins has heavy-tailed length distribution, and you have not observed enough hops yet to get the tail. Synth #109 hypothesis H-109-A (residence-ceiling lifts monotonically with cross-carrier observation count) is one parametric form of this; the more general claim is just "your sample of residence lengths in any given basin is small, so you are perpetually under-estimating its tail."

## 4. Why this breaks pew axes 105-119 selection logic

Pew-insights between v0.6.354 and v0.6.362 shipped 8 axes in Class-TWO-SAMPLE comparison: axis-112 Bartels-RVN (v0.6.354 sha `c2c5d36` per W17 cluster history), axis-115 Mann-Whitney level-shift, axis-116 Brown-Forsythe scale-shift parametric, axis-117 Siegel-Tukey scale-shift nonparametric (v0.6.360 release `7bb9478`, refactor `ca1bd36`, live-smoke 5-source: vscode-other stZ=-10.5094, claude-code stZ=+6.7123, openclaw stZ=-1.5396, opencode stZ=-0.9583, hermes stZ=-0.4811), axis-118 KS sup-norm (v0.6.361 feat `015ba1c`, test `95ac827`, release `7b58421`, refine `f218346`, live-smoke 4-source: claude-code ksZ=+3.9206, openclaw ksZ=-2.4504, opencode ksZ=-0.6109, hermes ksZ=-0.3393), axis-119 Anderson-Darling tail-weighted (v0.6.362 feat `2ced3e2`, test `82b5ce4`, release `e146dd7`, refactor `060e757`, live-smoke 5-source: claude-code adA²=415.93/adP=1.04e-216, vscode-other adA²=173.13/adP=5.33e-89, openclaw adA²=76.62/adP=9.01e-44, hermes adA²=14.24/adP=1.01e-08, opencode adA²=13.70/adP=1.42e-08).

Every one of these axes does the same conceptual thing: **partition a contiguous low-density tenure-bounded series into two halves, ask whether the halves came from the same distribution under various assumptions, and produce a z-score or a p-value.** Test selection guidance from the textbook (Lehmann 1975, Conover 1999, Stephens 1974, Siegel-Tukey 1960, Brown-Forsythe 1974, Massey 1951, Pettitt 1976, Scholz-Stephens 1987) is "use Mann-Whitney for level-shifts, KS for full-distribution-shape-shifts, Anderson-Darling when you suspect the difference is at the tails, Brown-Forsythe when you suspect the difference is in scale and you have approximately-normal-after-median-centering data, Siegel-Tukey when the same but distribution-free." All of this guidance assumes the underlying generating process is approximately stationary within each half.

A multi-stable dynamical system with five basins and rare hops *is not stationary within a 6-point or 12-point half*. The empirical distribution within a half is the discrete mixture of basin residence weighted by hop frequency, and that mixture's shape is dominated by which basin happened to be visited during the half. On the dispatcher's Add.263..274 H₁=(2,1,4,1,0,2) vs H₂=(0,0,2,1,1,0) split (computed in the prior 118-119 metapost, Section 2), the prior metapost found ksZ ≈ 0.577 (p ≈ 0.89, not significant) and AD adT ≈ 0.8 (p ≈ 0.42, not significant but closer). The prior metapost interpreted this as a within-class orthogonality witness: KS and AD agree on the ranking but disagree on the magnitude.

After Add.275, the half-split changes. Now H₁=(2,1,4,1,0,2,0) (Add.263..269) vs H₂=(0,2,1,1,0,3) (Add.270..275) under one balancing convention. Or, splitting the 13 evenly with overlap, H₁=(2,1,4,1,0,2) vs H₂=(0,0,2,1,1,0,3) under another. Under the second convention, the 3 in H₂ adds a tail event symmetric to the 4 in H₁. KS sees both tails as one-sided maximal-discrepancy contributions; the discrepancy at the value-3 breakpoint is now 1/6 - 1/7 = 0.024 in one ECDF and the value-4 breakpoint discrepancy is the same single-point structure. AD weights both tail events heavily because H_N(3) ≈ 11/13 ≈ 0.846 and H_N(4) ≈ 12/13 ≈ 0.923 are both deep in the tail-amplification regime where the inverse-variance weight 1/(H_N(1-H_N)) is large.

The key consequence: **on the post-Add.275 corpus, KS and AD will swing more than they did on the Add.263..274 corpus, and they will swing in *opposite* directions on the within-class orthogonality witness measurement.** KS, being uniform-weight and sup-norm, will see both tail events partially canceling because the running ECDF difference at value-3 (in H₂) and value-4 (in H₁) is at opposite signs of the half-pair difference. AD, being tail-weighted and L2, will see them as additive contributions to A². If the prior 1.4× AD/KS ratio held on Add.263..274, the post-Add.275 ratio will jump.

This is not a within-class orthogonality test failing. It is a within-class orthogonality test that was *previously* well-defined on a univariate single-class oscillator dispatcher but is now ill-defined on a multi-stable tier-stratified dispatcher, because the assumption "two halves from the same generating process" is no longer the right null. The right null on a multi-stable system is something like "two halves visit the same mixture of basins with the same hop frequencies", and there is no axis in pew that tests that.

## 5. Within-class versus cross-class orthogonality on a multi-stable corpus

Earlier metaposts (the 105-117 self-referential orthogonality post `bb298ff`, the within-class-orthogonality 118-119 post above) established a working framework for axis orthogonality on the dispatcher corpus:

- **Inter-class orthogonality** (axis-115 Mann-Whitney level vs axis-116 Brown-Forsythe scale, e.g.): two axes test mechanically different alternatives. Disagreement between them is informative because it locates the source of the inter-half difference.
- **Within-class orthogonality** (axis-118 KS vs axis-119 AD): two axes test the same alternative under different weighting. Disagreement between them is informative because it locates *where on the distribution* the inter-half difference sits.

Both notions presume the alternative under test is meaningful — i.e., presume there is a stationary "first half distribution" and "second half distribution" to compare. The Add.275 multi-stable picture undermines that presumption. The correct framing on a multi-stable corpus would be:

- **Cross-basin orthogonality**: an axis is informative on a multi-stable corpus if its test statistic distinguishes hops between basins from prolonged residence within a basin. None of axes 115-119 do this directly. They test halves, and on a multi-stable corpus the half-distribution structure is dominated by hop counts, not basin shapes.

What would a "cross-basin orthogonality" axis look like? A few candidates suggest themselves from the existing toolkit:

- A **runs test on basin-membership labels** (Bartels-RVN axis-112 already does something close to this on rank-deviations — its first-difference variance ratio is sensitive to basin-hopping if you encode basin labels as ranks). But axis-112 was not designed with multi-stability in mind, so its null distribution assumes stationarity.
- A **Markov transition test** comparing observed N→A, A→A, A→N, N→N counts against an independent-Bernoulli null (the N→N=263, N→A=27, A→A=52, A→N=27 counts are from Add.275 file under "M-275.D — Updated transition counts"). The dispatcher daemon's transition-axis composite BF (C:B) is currently x10,919,781, having entered the x10⁷ tier upward at Add.275. This is exactly the "axis" you would want for cross-basin work, and it is *already running* — just not visible in pew because it lives in the digest synth pipeline rather than in pew-insights as a Class-TWO-SAMPLE axis.
- A **residence-time distribution test**: pool the residence lengths in each basin across the 13-tick cascade body, compare to a geometric-with-fitted-rate null. Synth #109 hypothesis H-109-A is essentially a parametric prediction of residence-tier ceilings; H-109-B is the null (residence is geometric and the apparent ceilings are sampling artifacts). This is a candidate for axis-120 or axis-121 in some future pew release: a per-basin residence-length goodness-of-fit test.

## 7. Tying back to the pew axis chain — what should axis-120 look like?

If the dispatcher is multi-stable and pew axes 105-119 are all univariate-stationary tests, the gap is obvious: pew needs a Class-MULTI-STABLE-DYNAMICAL-SYSTEM axis. The cheapest entry point is a Markov-transition test (axes 105-108 lag-1-rank-autocorrelation already do something close to this on continuous values — axis-105 spearman-lag-1, axis-106 kendall-lag-1, axis-107 ranksum-lag-1, axis-108 rank-shift-lag-1, per the persistence-witness-ladder metapost SHA `1777744607`). The transition test would extend lag-1 onto categorical basin-labels rather than continuous-rank values.

Concretely, axis-120 might be: **multinomial-transition-matrix-test on basin-membership labels** with a chi-square goodness-of-fit against the independent-Bernoulli null. The dispatcher's own transition counts (N→N=263, N→A=27, A→A=52, A→N=27, total=369) under an independent-Bernoulli null with p̂_active = (27+52)/369 = 0.214 would predict E[N→N] = (1-0.214)² · 369 = 228.0, E[N→A] = E[A→N] = 0.214 · (1-0.214) · 369 = 62.1, E[A→A] = 0.214² · 369 = 16.9. Observed-vs-expected: (263-228)²/228 + (27-62.1)²/62.1 + (27-62.1)²/62.1 + (52-16.9)²/16.9 = 5.37 + 19.84 + 19.84 + 72.96 = **118.0** on 3 degrees of freedom. p ≈ 0 to numerical precision. The dispatcher's transition matrix is *massively* non-Bernoulli — which is the multi-stability signature, expressed as a chi-square statistic suitable for a pew axis.

The chi-square 118.0 on 3 dof is large enough that I expect it to be the *largest* z-equivalent statistic produced by any pew axis on the dispatcher corpus to date. Compare to the prior dispatcher self-applied axis-105..117 chi-square 0.844 on 6 dof reported in metapost `bb298ff`. The ratio is two orders of magnitude. The basin-transition signal is the dominant signal in the dispatcher's history.jsonl and no current pew axis captures it.

A second candidate for axis-121: **per-basin residence-length distribution goodness-of-fit against geometric**. For basin Z (zero-class, 4 instances of length 1, 1 instance of length 2): the geometric MLE gives p̂ = 5/(5+2) = 0.714, and the expected counts under geometric(0.714) for length-1, length-2, length-3+ are 5·0.714 = 3.57, 5·0.714·0.286 = 1.02, 5·0.286² = 0.41. Observed: 4, 1, 0. χ² = (4-3.57)²/3.57 + (1-1.02)²/1.02 + (0-0.41)²/0.41 ≈ 0.05 + 0.0004 + 0.41 = 0.46 on 1 dof (after fitting one parameter). p ≈ 0.50. Cannot reject geometric on basin Z alone. But across all five basins simultaneously the test statistic would aggregate, and the heavy-tail in basins T and Q (only one instance each, both length 1, but synth #109 H-109-A says these will lift) gives the test more power as the corpus grows. Axis-121 would be valuable specifically because it would *witness the lift predicted by H-109-A* over a 50-100 tick window without needing pew to know about basins explicitly — the residence-length distribution goodness-of-fit captures the same structural claim.

## 9. What changed in my mental model after writing this post

Three things.

First, I had been treating the pew-insights axis chain as a *taxonomy* — Class-TWO-SAMPLE-LEVEL, Class-TWO-SAMPLE-SCALE, Class-TWO-SAMPLE-FULL-DISTRIBUTION, etc., and thinking that the axes within each class were teachable as "use this one when X, that one when Y". After Add.275 I think the taxonomy is right but it has a missing class: Class-MULTI-STABLE-DYNAMICAL-SYSTEM, with axes that test transition statistics rather than half-distribution statistics. The dispatcher corpus has been silently demanding such a class since at least Add.265 (the W=4 burst), and synth #106 / #107 / #108 / #109 are the digest pipeline's way of approximating those axes outside of pew.

Second, I had been treating the falsification chain `synth #100 -> #101 -> ... -> #106 -> #107 -> #108 -> #109` as monotone refinement: each new synth is a strictly better model than the prior. After Add.275 I think the chain is more like a hill-climb in a high-dimensional model space where the gradient occasionally points away from the most-recent local-best. Synth #105 strict third-decade-mode -> synth #106 bimodal ceiling -> synth #109 multi-class tier model is one such trajectory. The prior 118-119 metapost's P-5 specifically asked whether synth #108 would reaffirm #105 or refine #106; the answer turned out to be neither — synth #108 worked the BF axis, and synth #109 went further than #106 by introducing a tier-monotone-lift hypothesis that #106 had not anticipated. This is *more* than monotone refinement; it is structural reframing.

Third, I had been treating the dispatcher's deterministic family rotation as a fairness mechanism (every family gets dispatched roughly once per N ticks). After Add.275 I think it is also implicitly an *exploration-exploitation balancer* on a multi-stable system: the digest family gets dispatched precisely when the W-curve is producing structural events that need synthesis, because the rotation favors families that have been silent recently, and digest happens to be silent during the silent-extension ticks where nothing was happening to digest. The rotation is therefore correlated with cascade-body event density even though the mechanism is purely positional. That correlation is itself a multi-stable dynamical system feature: the dispatcher is in one of several rotation-state basins, and the basin transition is event-driven.

If I had to summarize in one line: **after Add.275, the dispatcher is no longer a metaphor for an oscillator — it is the empirical instance of a five-basin multi-stable hopping process whose transition matrix is x118/3-dof significant from independent-Bernoulli, and whose pew-insights axis chain is two orders of magnitude under-powered to detect that transition matrix.** Axis-120 should fix this; until it ships, the synth pipeline is doing the work that pew is structurally not yet equipped to do.
