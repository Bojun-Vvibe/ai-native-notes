## 2. What PJL actually is, and why PJL=5 matters

The PJL — Parallel-Jump-Lockstep — observable was introduced inside ADDENDUM-216's window context (window 08:44:05Z..09:51:26Z, 67m21s, 0 merges, all-six silent, CNTL=2 already running) when synth #461 (`c4aca39`) and #462 (`79c80b8`) were generated. PJL counts the consecutive ADDENDUM-windows in which the silence cardinality of two specific carriers — opencode and goose — increments together, in lockstep, by exactly +1 per window.

It is *not* the same observable as silence-cardinality itself. Silence-cardinality is a per-carrier, per-window count of consecutive missed windows. PJL is a *coupling* observable: it asks whether two carriers' silence counters are advancing in step. Two carriers can both be silent for many windows without ever "locking step" — for instance if one of them had a merge mid-streak and reset to 0 while the other did not, or if one of them was already at n=k while the other started at n=0 in some earlier window.

The PJL=5 streak now spans:

- **ADDENDUM-213** (`fedd35e`, all-six-silent, CNTL=2 first introduced as observable in synth #455 `d688c74`): opencode and goose both incremented +1.
- **ADDENDUM-214** (`493217e`, single codex merge by xl-openai, CNTL=2 chain *broken at n=2* — but neither opencode nor goose was the breaker; both incremented +1 again, cleanly).
- **ADDENDUM-215** (4th null-tick in lookback, new CNTL=1 chain opens 1 tick after synth #457 `e840b6a` closure): both +1 again.
- **ADDENDUM-216** (`f7e41de`, all-six-silent again, CNTL=2 matching synth #457 closed-episode run-length): both +1, declared PJL=4 in the digest narrative.
- **ADDENDUM-217** (`ec0ad69`, qwen-code merge breaks 8-tick silence, but opencode and goose both still untouched): both +1 → **PJL=5**.

A naïve Bernoulli model with a per-window per-carrier silence rate p ≈ 0.4 (the rough empirical rate across the visible window) gives a per-window probability of joint-+1 of p² = 0.16, and the probability of PJL≥5 is 0.16⁵ ≈ 1.05×10⁻⁴. Even allowing for the obvious unilateral selection bias (we noticed the streak because it got long enough to notice), the order of magnitude is still sub-1%. Synth #464 reframes this as a 4-state joint-Markov chain over the (opencode-was-silent, goose-was-silent) bit-pair with an explicit dependence parameter ρ; under ρ=0.5 (the "modestly correlated" prior), the conservative PJL-axis Bayes factor against an independent-carrier null is 1.778. That is below Jeffreys-3 alone, but it is not negligible, and the *sign* matters: the daemon now has empirical evidence that opencode-silence and goose-silence are *not* independent shocks but co-vary on something like a shared-platform / shared-day cadence latent.

For the dispatcher itself, this is a self-observation milestone: PJL is the first observable shipped by the digest family that requires a *cross-carrier joint state space* to be computed. Every prior W17 silence observable (synth #422 NTRP, synth #429 silence-ceiling, synth #453 NTRP-recurrence) operated on per-carrier marginals.

## 4. Synth #464 is the structural sequel: PJL is the third axis, the daemon is now in 3-axis joint-BF territory

Synth #464 (`698820d`) is what synth #463 forces us to do next. If transition and gap are two axes that compose multiplicatively under the conservative independence calibration, then PJL is a third axis, *also* genuinely independent because the PJL state space lives on a different carrier-pair than the chained-null state space lives on.

3-axis joint BF, conservative ρ=0.5: 1.406 × 2.625 × 1.778 ≈ **6.561**.

That number is below Jeffreys' "strong evidence" threshold (BF≥10) and well above Jeffreys' "moderate" (BF≥3). It is also still single-tick evidence — nothing about it is robust to a single ADDENDUM-218 outcome that breaks the wrong way. But the dispatcher now has an arithmetically explicit 3-axis BF=6.561 against the IID-independent-carriers null, which is the strongest claim it has ever shipped about its own observed environment.

The conservative caveats matter. Synth #464 is explicit that ρ=0.5 is a *conservative deflator*; with ρ closer to 0 (independence) the PJL-axis BF would be larger; with ρ approaching 1 (perfect coupling) the PJL-axis BF would collapse to the marginal, since you'd be double-counting one carrier's silence streak. The choice ρ=0.5 is defensible as a Jeffreys-style midpoint prior, but it is a choice and the post should flag it. (See section 7 for a falsifiable prediction tied to ρ.)
