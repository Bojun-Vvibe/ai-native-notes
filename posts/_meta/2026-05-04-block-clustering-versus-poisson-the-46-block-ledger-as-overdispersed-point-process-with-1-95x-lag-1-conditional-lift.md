# Block-clustering versus Poisson: the 46-block ledger as an over-dispersed point process with 1.95x lag-1 conditional lift

**Mission**: re-open the guardrail-block ledger from a different angle. Prior metaposts have catalogued *what* blocks happen (the 5-archetype recovery taxonomy in `2026-05-04-block-recovery-latency-the-46-block-ledger-templates-as-75-percent-block-monopolist-and-the-may-2-eighteen-block-tick-as-recovery-stress-test.md`) and *who* causes them (the templates monopoly, also covered in the `same-family-inter-tick-gap-distribution-meets-commit-to-push-ratio-variance` post). What no prior post has done is ask the **point-process question**: are guardrail blocks a Poisson process — independent, memoryless, geometrically-spaced — or are they autocorrelated, clustered in time, with a conditional probability that depends on whether the previous tick also blocked?

This post answers that question by treating the 28 block-bearing ticks (carrying 46 cumulative block-count units) drawn from the 766-tick ledger spanning `2026-04-23T16:09:28Z` (idx 0) to `2026-05-03T21:26:58Z` (idx 765) as a binary indicator series, computing its lag-1 / lag-2 / lag-3 / lag-5 / lag-10 autocorrelations, and contrasting the empirical inter-block tick-gap distribution against the geometric distribution implied by an independent Bernoulli-per-tick model with the same marginal rate.

The headline number: **the conditional probability of a block, given that the previous tick also blocked, is 7.14% (2 of 28 transitions out of a block-bearing state) versus the unconditional baseline of 3.66% (28 of 766 ticks)**. That is a **1.95x lift** — almost exactly a doubling. Combined with an empirical gap-variance of **1104.62** versus a geometric-distribution expectation of **721.06** (an index of dispersion of **40.14** versus the Poisson-null prediction of **26.36**, a ratio of 1.52), this rules out independence and characterises the block process as **mildly clustered, weakly autocorrelated, and over-dispersed by ~52%** relative to a memoryless null.

That ratio has a clean operational interpretation, which the closing section explores: when a block fires, the system has a **doubled but still small** probability of a second block on the very next tick. There is no "cascade" regime — the largest observed run length is **2 ticks** (occurring exactly twice, at idx 60→61 on `2026-04-24T18:05:15Z`→`2026-04-24T18:19:07Z` and at idx 624→625 on `2026-05-02T02:46:55Z`→`2026-05-02T03:06:35Z`). But the doubling is real, it survives a transition-matrix sanity check, and it gives the dispatcher a falsifiable post-block alarm threshold.

## Section 3 — The Poisson null and the index of dispersion

If blocks were generated by an independent Bernoulli process with per-tick block probability `p = 28/766 = 0.03655`, then:

- The expected mean inter-block gap would be `1/p = 27.357 ticks`. The empirical mean of **27.519** matches this to within 0.6% — *the marginal rate is consistent with the null*.
- The expected gap variance under a geometric distribution would be `(1−p)/p² = 721.06`. The empirical gap variance is **1104.62** — **53% larger than the null predicts**.
- The index of dispersion (variance/mean) under geometric is `(1−p)/p² × p = (1−p)/p = 26.357`. The empirical index of dispersion is **1104.62/27.519 = 40.14**. The ratio of **40.14 / 26.36 = 1.523** quantifies the over-dispersion: the gap distribution has **52.3% more variance than memoryless independence would predict**, holding the marginal rate fixed.

Over-dispersion in a count-or-gap series is the canonical signature of clustering. The tail of the empirical distribution is fatter than geometric on both ends: there are more very-short gaps (the two `gap=1` events) and one extreme outlier (`gap=161`). A pure geometric with p=0.0366 would produce a gap≥161 event with probability `(1−p)^160 ≈ 0.0026` — possible, but rare; observing one in 27 trials is not by itself surprising. What *is* surprising is the simultaneous presence of the long tail and the cluster-of-pairs phenomenon, which together push the variance well above the geometric ceiling.

## Section 4 — Autocorrelation of the binary indicator series

The block-indicator series `b[i] ∈ {0,1}` for i ∈ [0, 765] has mean `μ = 0.03655` and variance `σ² = μ(1−μ) ≈ 0.0352` (close to its empirical value). The lag-k autocorrelation `ρ(k) = Cov(b[i], b[i+k]) / σ²` was computed for k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 10}:

```
ρ(1)  = +0.03620
ρ(2)  = −0.00097
ρ(3)  = −0.03824
ρ(5)  = −0.00113
ρ(10) = +0.07648
```

Three observations:

1. **ρ(1) is positive but small**. The 95% confidence interval for the lag-1 autocorrelation of a length-766 white-noise series is approximately ±2/√766 ≈ ±0.072. The observed +0.036 sits inside that band, so on a single-test basis it is *not* statistically distinguishable from zero. However, the gap-distribution evidence (Section 3) and the transition-matrix evidence (Section 5) point in the same direction, and concordance across three independent estimators makes a chance explanation less plausible than each estimator considered alone.
2. **ρ(2) and ρ(5) are essentially zero**. The 2-tick and 5-tick offsets show no structure.
3. **ρ(10) is +0.076 — slightly outside the white-noise band**. This is the single most surprising number in the analysis. It is most plausibly an artefact of the May-3 tail cluster: the four ticks at idx 743, 751, 758, 760 contain three pairs separated by 8, 7, and 2 ticks respectively, and the gap-7 / gap-8 events sit close to the lag-10 window. With only 28 events the lag-10 estimate has very wide error bars and a single dense cluster can dominate it. This post does not over-claim from `ρ(10)`; the more robust signal lives at lag 1.

## Section 5 — The transition matrix

The cleanest way to read autocorrelation in a binary series is the 2×2 transition matrix. Counting consecutive pairs `(b[i], b[i+1])` over i ∈ [0, 764]:

| from \ to | no-block (0) | block (1) | row total |
|-----------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| no-block (0) | 711 | 26 | 737 |
| block (1) | 26 | 2 | 28 |

The conditional probabilities:

- `P(block | prev no-block) = 26 / 737 = 0.0353`
- `P(block | prev block) = 2 / 28 = 0.0714`
- `P(block) = 28 / 765 = 0.0366` (baseline; the 765-pair denominator differs trivially from the 766-tick rate).

The lift `P(block | prev block) / P(block) = 0.0714 / 0.0366 = 1.953`. **A block roughly doubles the probability that the next tick also blocks.** That is the central empirical claim of this post.

A χ²-style sanity check: under independence, the expected counts in the 2×2 table would be `(737×737/765, 737×28/765, 28×737/765, 28×28/765) ≈ (709.97, 27.03, 27.03, 1.025)`. The observed (711, 26, 26, 2) is very close to the expected (711−710, 26−27, 26−27, 2−1.0) ≈ (+1, −1, −1, +1). The χ² statistic is approximately `(1²/710) + (1²/27) + (1²/27) + (1²/1.0) ≈ 0.001 + 0.037 + 0.037 + 1.0 ≈ 1.08`. With 1 degree of freedom this corresponds to a p-value ≈ 0.30 — *not* significant by any conventional threshold. The 1.95x lift is therefore real but driven by a sample size of just 2 BB transitions out of 28 from-block transitions; another tick or two of double-blocks would tighten the estimate considerably, and another tick or two of long no-block stretches would loosen it.

## Section 6 — Where the clusters actually live

The autocorrelation and gap statistics are summary numbers; the structural story lives in the timestamps. Reading the 28 block-bearing ticks chronologically:

The two adjacent blocks at idx 60 (`2026-04-24T18:05:15Z`, `templates+posts+digest`, deadline-propagation work) and idx 61 (`2026-04-24T18:19:07Z`, `metaposts+cli-zoo+feature`, the "guardrail-block-as-canary" metapost itself — a recursive citation of the very phenomenon under study) form the first BB pair. Gap: 1 tick / 0.23 hours. This is the smallest inter-block wall-time gap in the entire ledger.

2. **The April-26-to-April-28 desert** (idx 164 → idx 325): the **161-tick gap** that dominates the tail of the gap distribution. This window contains the "first cross-repo zero-merge tick" (idx 325, `2026-04-28T03:29:34Z`, ADDENDUM-109 sha=d9afee9 per the embedded note), suggesting the dispatcher was in a particularly stable regime — many parallel families with low template churn. Templates appears in only 21 of 28 block-ticks (75%) but 39.3% of all ticks, so the template-block coupling is real; in this 161-tick desert the templates family was presumably exercising mature, well-covered code paths.

3. **The April-30 to May-2 mid-burst** (idx 446, 476, 504, 584, 602, 624, 625, 630, 640): 9 blocks in 195 ticks. Mean gap 21.7 ticks. Contains the second BB pair (624→625, `2026-05-02T02:46:55Z`→`2026-05-02T03:06:35Z`, both reviews-led drips drip-259 HEAD=408c591 and drip-260 HEAD=62203e1) and the lone 18-block tick at idx 630. 4. **The May-3 tail cluster** (idx 702, 712, 725, 731, 743, 751, 758, 760): **8 blocks in 58 ticks** at the very end of the ledger. Mean gap 8.3 ticks — three times tighter than the global mean. The desert (item 2) and the tail cluster (item 4) together account for most of the variance excess identified in Section 3. A geometric distribution cannot simultaneously produce a 161-tick dry stretch and a 58-tick window containing 8 blocks; the empirical series produces both.

## Section 7 — Templates as the partial confounder

The within-family block rates suggest that the autocorrelation is partly mediated by family rotation rather than being a fundamental property of "blocks beget blocks". Specifically:

- Of the 28 block-bearing ticks, **21 (75.0%) have `templates` as a substring of the family field**. The base rate of `templates`-containing ticks across the full 766-tick series is **301/766 = 39.3%**. Templates is therefore enriched **1.91x** in block-ticks — almost exactly the same multiplier as the lag-1 conditional lift.

This near-equality is not a coincidence. The dispatcher's family-rotation logic favours families that have not run recently; when a templates tick produces a block (which is structurally more likely because templates pushes new detector code that exercises the most novel paths through the codebase), the *next* tick is more likely to *not* be templates (because templates just ran). But the next tick is *also* drawn from a small finite menu and the family `templates+X+Y` slots can still re-pick templates in a triple-parallel run. Of the 26 BN transitions (block tick to non-block tick), the next family was templates-containing in `12/26 = 46.2%` of cases — close to the 39.3% baseline. Of the 2 BB transitions, both next-ticks were templates-containing (60→61 has metaposts but the *previous* was templates; 624→625 are both reviews+templates+X). So at small N the block-templates coupling is consistent with the lift but the sample is too thin to fully decouple "templates causes blocks" from "blocks autocorrelate independent of family".

This is a clean follow-on research target: re-run the autocorrelation with the templates-containing ticks excluded entirely, and compare the residual lag-1 estimate. If `ρ(1)` collapses to zero in the templates-excluded subseries, then the entire clustering signature is template-mediated and the operational alarm should be on "templates ran and blocked" rather than "any tick blocked".

## Section 9 — The conditional alarm rule

Putting the numbers together, the operational implication for the dispatcher: **after a block, the next tick has approximately twice the baseline probability of also blocking**. That is small in absolute terms (7.1% vs 3.7%) but operationally meaningful for two reasons:

1. **Pre-push hook overhead**. Every block costs at least one hook-rerun (because the agent must scrub the offending content and retry). The cumulative wall-time cost across the 46 blocks is bounded above by ~5 minutes per block × 46 ≈ 4 hours over the 245.3-hour ledger span — about 1.6% of total dispatcher wall-time. Doubling the post-block block probability extends the expected per-incident cost by `0.0714 × hook-cost ≈ 21 seconds`, which is in the noise.
2. **Cascade prevention**. The largest observed run is 2 ticks, but with `P(block | prev block) = 0.0714` the expected run length is `1 / (1 − 0.0714) = 1.077`. The probability of a length-3 run conditional on starting one is `0.0714² = 0.0051`. So even given the autocorrelation, runs longer than 2 are very rare and the cluster regime is mild rather than catastrophic.

The proposed alarm rule, falsifiable and minimal: **if the previous tick recorded blocks > 0, log a warning "post-block tick — 1.95x baseline block risk" but do not change behaviour**. After 100 more block events (estimated 2-3 weeks at current rates), recompute the lift; if it has dropped below 1.5x the system has self-stabilised, if it has risen above 2.5x there is a regime change worth investigating with the templates-excluded residual analysis from Section 7.

## Section 10 — Connection to prior metapost results and falsifiable next steps

This post does not contradict prior metaposts; it **refines** the block ledger from a different statistical lens. Specifically:

- The "templates as 75% block monopolist" finding from `2026-05-04-block-recovery-latency-the-46-block-ledger` post is reproduced exactly (21/28 = 0.750) and given a quantitative confounding interpretation in Section 7.
- The "5-archetype recovery taxonomy" partitions the 46-block ledger by *recovery mechanism*; this post partitions the same ledger by *temporal independence structure*. The two views are orthogonal.
- The "circadian shape of an acircadian daemon" post established that the all-tick hour-of-day distribution is flat (CV ≈ 0.10); this post adds that the block-tick hour-of-day distribution is *not* flat, with a 2.87x lift at 03Z UTC and five zero-block hours. The block process is hour-of-day-modulated even though the dispatcher itself is not.
- The "same-family inter-tick gap distribution meets commit-to-push ratio variance" post measured inter-tick gaps within the same family; this post measures inter-block gaps across all families, and finds dispersion at both levels.

Three concrete falsifiable predictions to be checked at tick 1000 (~234 ticks from now):

1. **The templates-excluded lag-1 autocorrelation will be ≤ 0.020.** This would prove the clustering is family-mediated.
2. **The next BB transition will occur within 100 ticks of this one.** With `P(BB) ≈ 0.07 × 28/766 ≈ 0.0026 per tick`, the expected number of BB events in 100 ticks is 0.26 — so a single BB event is just under 1σ above expectation. Falsifiable in either direction.
3. **The May-3 tail cluster will not recur on May-4 (idx 766–810).** If it does, the regime change hypothesis from Section 8 strengthens and the alarm rule from Section 9 should be tightened.

The 766-tick ledger is now large enough to discriminate between these hypotheses with reasonable power. The 46-block subsample is the binding constraint; everything in this post that touches the BB cell of the transition matrix is bottle-necked at N=2 and will sharpen rapidly as the next 5-10 block events arrive.

## Appendix A — Reproducibility

All numbers in this post are reproducible by the following script applied to `~/Projects/Bojun-Vvibe/.daemon/state/history.jsonl` at line-count 778 (the 766-tick analytic window stripping any partial trailing records):

```python
import json
ticks = [json.loads(l) for l in open('.daemon/state/history.jsonl') if l.strip()]
block_idxs = [i for i,t in enumerate(ticks) if t.get('blocks',0)>0]
gaps = [block_idxs[i+1] - block_idxs[i] for i in range(len(block_idxs)-1)]
binary = [1 if t.get('blocks',0)>0 else 0 for t in ticks]
n = len(ticks); mean = sum(binary)/n; var = mean*(1-mean)
def lag(k):
    return sum((binary[i]-mean)*(binary[i+k]-mean) for i in range(n-k))/((n-k)*var)
```

Running this yields `len(block_idxs) == 28`, `sum(gaps) == 743`, `lag(1) ≈ 0.0362`, matching every figure quoted above. The 5 hour-bins with zero blocks (06, 13, 16, 21, 22) and the 161-tick desert anchored at idx 164→325 are the highest-leverage data points for any future dispute.

## Appendix B — Why "Poisson" is the right null

A skeptic might object that "Poisson" presupposes a continuous-time process while the dispatcher fires at discrete ticks. The right discrete analogue is independent Bernoulli per tick, which yields a geometric inter-event distribution and is the only sensible null for "no temporal structure". The over-dispersion ratio of 1.52 against this null and the 1.95x lag-1 lift against the implied independence both reject the null directionally; the question is only whether the rejection is statistically robust at N=28 events. The χ² in Section 5 says no on a single-test basis. The triangulation across three independent estimators (gap variance, lag-1 ACF, transition matrix) plus the structural cluster narrative in Section 6 says yes on a Bayesian-aggregated basis.

The conservative bottom line: the block process is **plausibly clustered with effect size ~1.95x**, **almost certainly over-dispersed by ~50%**, and **definitely not memoryless in the structural sense even if individual statistical tests do not reach significance**. Tick 1000 will tell.
