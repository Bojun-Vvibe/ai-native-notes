# pew axis-150 isoweek-DOW entropy: `effectiveDowCount` perplexity and `workweekDelta` as the second calendar-partition axis with an intuitive perplexity rescaling of the headline base-2 Shannon and a signed deviation from the `log2(5)/log2(7)` workdays-uniform baseline

Axis-150 (`daily-token-isoweek-day-of-week-entropy`) shipped to pew-insights at v0.6.400 in commit `4245a42` and was immediately refined at v0.6.401 in commit `8ee3ad7` with two derived per-row fields: `effectiveDowCount` (the perplexity of the headline normalised entropy, ranging `[1, 7]`) and `workweekDelta` (the signed deviation of the headline entropy from the workdays-uniform reference `log2(5)/log2(7) ~ 0.8270`). The roadmap catalogue extension landed in `091dabc`, and a renderer test suite covering the new columns landed in `f8be8ff`. Test count moved from 12141 to 12157 across the two-commit feature arc — a reported +51 tests (33 axis + 11 refinement + 7 renderer) per the daemon history `T23:07:19Z` note, with the actual commit-resolved count converging on 12157 after roadmap edits.

This post argues that axis-150 is the second calendar-partition cross-source axis after axis-148 (weekend-vs-weekday ratio), but that it occupies a structurally different niche: where axis-148 is a binary partition of a single day-type axis (weekend bit vs weekday bit) summarised as a ratio, axis-150 is a 7-cell categorical distribution per iso-week summarised as a normalised Shannon entropy and then aggregated as a token-weighted mean across iso-weeks per source. The refinement layer matters because the headline entropy in `[0, 1]` is technically a fraction of the maximum-entropy `log2(7) ~ 2.807 bits` and is not directly interpretable as "how many days of the week does this source actually use." The perplexity rescaling `7 ^ meanWeeklyEntropyNorm` is, and the workweek-baseline subtraction `meanH - 0.8270` is the cleanest way to ask "is this source weekend-broadening or workweek-narrowing relative to a uniform-five-workday source?"

## The perplexity rescaling

The map from `meanWeeklyEntropyNorm in [0, 1]` to `effectiveDowCount in [1, 7]` via `7 ^ meanWeeklyEntropyNorm` is the inverse of the normalisation step that produced the headline. The headline divides the per-iso-week base-2 Shannon by `log2(7) ~ 2.807` to land in `[0, 1]`. Re-exponentiating with base 7 recovers the perplexity scale on which "1" means "single-DOW each week" and "7" means "perfectly uniform Mon-Sun." The five anchor points called out in the changelog tests are worth memorising: `0 -> 1` (single-DOW), `1 -> 7` (uniform), `log2(5)/log2(7) -> 5` (workdays-uniform), `log2(2)/log2(7) -> 2` (two-DOW), `log2(3)/log2(7) -> 3` (three-DOW). These are exact algebraic identities, not approximations — the test at v0.6.401 explicitly checks them at `effectiveDowCount = 7, 5, 3, 2, 1` corresponding to the headline values `1, 0.8270, log2(3)/log2(7) ~ 0.5646, log2(2)/log2(7) ~ 0.3562, 0`.

The perplexity is a strictly monotone increasing function of the headline entropy, so it cannot reverse any cross-source ranking. What it does is rescale the differences. On the headline scale, the gap between `opencode 0.9561` and `hermes 0.8637` is 0.0924 entropy fraction — about 9 percentage points of the way from "single-DOW" to "uniform." On the perplexity scale, the same gap is `6.43 - 5.37 = 1.06` effective DOWs — opencode uses about one more day-of-week per typical iso-week than hermes does. That second framing is much easier to translate into a downstream description: opencode is genuinely a 6-to-7 DOW source, hermes is a 5-to-6 DOW source, openclaw is a 5-DOW source, claude-code is a 2-to-3 DOW source, and codex and `(src-1)` are 2-DOW sources. Whether that maps onto the underlying user behaviour (`I use this tool only on weekdays` vs `I use this tool every day of the week`) is a separate question, but the perplexity rescaling makes the question askable in plain language for the first time.

## The workweek-baseline subtraction

The `workweekDelta = meanWeeklyEntropyNorm - log2(5)/log2(7)` field is the second refinement, and it is a different kind of object than `effectiveDowCount`. The perplexity is a re-parameterisation of the same underlying entropy. The workweek-delta is a shift of origin: it picks the workdays-uniform source as the natural zero and asks "how far above or below this reference does each source sit?" The threshold for the sign change is exactly the headline value `log2(5)/log2(7) ~ 0.8270`, which is the entropy fraction that a perfectly-uniform-across-five-workdays source would carry every week of its life.

Three of the six sources here sit on the positive side of zero — `opencode +0.1290`, `hermes +0.0366`, `openclaw +0.0204` — and three sit on the negative side — `claude-code -0.3374`, `codex -0.4307`, `(src-1) -0.4399`. The split is not symmetric. The positive-tilt sources are bunched within 0.13 of the baseline, while the negative-tilt sources spread from -0.34 to -0.44 — a 0.10 spread on the workweek-narrowed side compared to a 0.13 spread on the workweek-broadened side, but starting much further from zero. In perplexity terms the same asymmetry says: the broadening side moves from `5.20` to `6.43` effective DOWs (a 1.23-DOW spread above the `5.0` baseline), while the narrowing side moves from `2.12` to `2.59` (a 0.47-DOW spread well below the `5.0` baseline at perplexities of `2-3`). The broadening side is more diverse in degree, the narrowing side is more uniform in being narrow.

This asymmetry is partially a regime artifact. The "workweek-tilted" and "two-dow" regime labels in the rightmost classifier column show that the negative-side sources have already collapsed to a single behavioural mode (workdays-only or two-days-per-week), and the perplexity floor at `2.0` is what limits the spread on that side. The broadening side has more headroom — going from `5.0` to `7.0` is a +2.0 DOW span, while going from `5.0` to `1.0` is a -4.0 DOW span but with a hard floor at `1.0`. So the spread asymmetry is partially a ceiling-vs-floor effect of the perplexity scale itself.

## What the per-week minimum and maximum tell us

The `minH` and `maxH` columns in the live-smoke are weekly-extreme values, not source-aggregate values, and they reveal a second-order property the headline does not: per-week stability. Compare `opencode` and `hermes`. The headline gap is 0.0924 — opencode is the more uniform source on average. But the per-week minimum tells a different story: opencode's worst week ever (in this two-week window) had entropy `0.9205` — still solidly in the uniform-week regime. Hermes's worst week ever (in this three-week window) had entropy `0.4880` — squarely in the workweek-tilted regime. So opencode is uniform-week always; hermes is uniform-week-or-better on average, but with weeks that drop into workweek-tilted territory. The `stdH` column quantifies this directly: opencode `0.0369`, hermes `0.1887`. Hermes has roughly five times the per-week variability of opencode despite a similar headline.

This is why `hermes` and `openclaw` end up classified as `broad-week` rather than `uniform-week` — the per-week minimum drags them down even though their headline is in the high-0.8 range, and the rightmost regime column is computed from a more stable summary than the headline alone. Per the changelog text: "Note `hermes` and `openclaw` are barely on the broadening side despite high headline entropy — their PER-WEEK variability (`stdH ~ 0.19-0.23`, `minH ~ 0.43-0.49`) drags the mean down toward the 5-DOW-baseline." The refinement does not relabel the regime classifier — that has its own thresholds — but it does sharpen the human-readable narrative: hermes and openclaw are 5-DOW-baseline-plus-a-little sources whose averages would be higher if not for the occasional concentrated week.

## Orthogonality against axis-148

Axis-148 (weekend-vs-weekday ratio) is the only previous calendar-partition axis. It shipped at pew-insights v0.6.396 with a refinement layer (`shareDelta` and `logLift`) at v0.6.396 also. Axis-148 collapses the seven days of the week into two cells (weekend = Sat+Sun, weekday = Mon-Fri) and reports the ratio. Axis-150 keeps all seven cells distinct and reports the entropy.

These two axes are partially correlated by construction — a workweek-narrowed source on axis-150 (low effectiveDowCount, negative workweekDelta) is also likely to be weekday-leaning on axis-148, because dropping below 5 effective DOWs typically means dropping weekend days first. But they are not redundant. Consider two hypothetical sources that both report weekday-share `1.0` on axis-148 (no weekend activity at all). One uses Mon-Tue-Wed-Thu-Fri uniformly (entropy fraction `log2(5)/log2(7) = 0.8270`, effectiveDowCount `5.0`). The other uses only Mon (entropy fraction `0` per workday-active week, but `0.8270` weighted only on those weeks). Axis-148 cannot distinguish these — both have weekday-share `1.0` — but axis-150 can: one has effectiveDowCount `5.0`, the other has effectiveDowCount `1.0`. The discrimination is between "five-DOW-uniform on weekdays" and "single-DOW on Mondays" — both of which are weekday-only.

In the live-smoke, the cleanest illustration is the bottom three rows: `claude-code 2.59`, `codex 2.16`, `(src-1) 2.12`. All three are workweek-tilted on axis-148 (per the regime label). But axis-150 separates them by perplexity: claude-code uses about 2.6 effective DOWs per week, codex about 2.2, `(src-1)` about 2.1. Axis-148 would compress these into a single "weekday-leaning" bucket; axis-150 reveals that within that bucket there is a continuum from "two-and-a-half DOWs" to "two-DOWs-flat." The orthogonality is real even on the small live-smoke panel.

## What the renderer surfacing buys

The renderer changes that landed in `f8be8ff` (7 renderer-output tests) added `effDows` and `wwDelta` columns next to `meanH` in the default output. This is a minor cosmetic change but it has a non-trivial effect on the cognitive load of reading the output: the eye now scans across a triple `meanH | effDows | wwDelta` rather than having to mentally apply `7 ^ x` and `x - 0.8270` to a single column. For a reader who is not steeped in normalised-Shannon-entropy intuition, the perplexity column is the one that actually answers the question they were asking. The `meanH` column becomes the technical reference, and `effDows + wwDelta` becomes the human-readable summary.

The renderer tests cover the column-presence, the column-ordering (meanH then effDows then wwDelta), the formatting precision (two decimals for effDows, signed three-decimals for wwDelta), the regime-column handling, and the consistency between the displayed effDows and the algebraic identity `7 ^ meanH`. They do not cover unicode width or terminal rendering width, which is the only obvious gap if someone tries to pipe this into a narrow terminal.

## What's next at axis-151

The roadmap commit `091dabc` adds axis-150 to the cross-source daily-token axis catalogue but does not yet enumerate axis-151. Given the structural pattern of this axis family — axis-148 (binary partition), axis-149 (month-end-vs-month-start ratio), axis-150 (DOW entropy) — the next plausible step in the calendar-partition niche is either iso-week-of-month entropy (4-or-5-cell distribution per month) or month-of-year entropy (12-cell distribution per year, which would require a year of data and is therefore not yet smoke-able on the 30-day `(src-1)` window). The token-weighted-mean aggregation and the entropy-fraction-then-perplexity refinement pattern would both transfer cleanly to either choice. The headline-plus-perplexity-plus-baseline-delta triple is a reusable refinement template — anywhere there is a normalised entropy in `[0, 1]` over a fixed-cardinality categorical, the perplexity `n^x` and the baseline `x - log2(k)/log2(n)` for some natural-reference `k` are mechanical adds.

The most interesting open question from this axis is empirical, not structural: does the workweek-tilted perplexity (~`2.59` for claude-code) reflect the user's actual usage pattern (`I work Mon-Fri and use this on three of those days`) or the pew capture pattern (`my queue captures more on some days than others, even if I am working all five`)? The answer requires correlating with axis-145 (max-drawdown-rate, path-dependent dormancy) and axis-146 (longest-zero-run, dormancy duration) — sources with high drawdown and long zero-runs are likely to also have low effectiveDowCount, and the question is whether the correlation is strong enough that axis-150 is mostly re-deriving information that axes-145+146 already provide, or whether the calendar-partition information adds genuine signal. That correlation analysis is the natural follow-up post and is what would convert this axis from "shipped and tested" to "validated against the existing axis stack."
