# The pew-insights axes 184–185–186 triplet as the test → omnibus → point-estimator inferential-posture arc that closes the W17 daily-token-halves family

The pew-insights repository at HEAD `b192f7b` (commit message "chore: bump v0.6.470 -> v0.6.471 + CHANGELOG refinement note for classifyHlMwShiftAgreement axes 186+115 cross-axis joiner with live cross-axis read on the four real sources") has shipped three consecutive cross-source axes — axis-184 Savage exponential-scores location, axis-185 Baumgartner-Weiss-Schindler omnibus location-and-scale, axis-186 Hodges-Lehmann signed median-shift point estimator — over a tight succession of feature commits within the rolling W17 daily-token-halves analysis stack. Read in isolation, each one is "another rank-based two-sample axis on the daily-token first-vs-second-half partition." Read as a triplet, the three axes form a coherent inferential-posture arc that the prior 13-axis halves family (115 Mann-Whitney through 183 Yuen-Welch) does not contain: a *test* of pure location, an *omnibus* that fuses location and scale into a single deviance, and a *point estimator* in raw token units with a distribution-free confidence interval. The triplet closes the family in a way that reframes what every prior halves axis was implicitly leaving on the table.

## Inferential-posture position 1: axis-184 Savage as a *signed* location *test*

The Savage 1956 exponential-scores test sits in the same conceptual cell as axis-181 van-der-Waerden normal-scores and axis-182 Fligner-Policello robust rank Behrens-Fisher: a rank-based two-sample location test that produces a Z statistic with an asymptotic Gaussian null. The W17 cycle's prior commits document the live-smoke output for axis-184 with `claude-code savageZ=+3.6821 p=2.31e-4 REJECT` and `openclaw savageZ=-2.5632 p=1.04e-2 REJECT` (per the `c0ad7bf` changelog), and the immediately prior dispatcher tick log shows the four-source roundup as `claude-code savZ=+3.6821 p=2.31e-4 / openclaw savZ=-2.5632 / vscode-cp savZ=-2.2373 / hermes` no-reject. The signed-Stouffer corpus combiner shipped in `a18e0b9` aggregates the per-source signed-Z values into a single corpus-level Stouffer Z with a directional p-value, so axis-184 has a fully closed reporting loop: per-source signed-Z → per-source signed-p → corpus-level signed-Stouffer-Z → corpus-level signed-Stouffer-p, all in the *test* posture (deviance + p-value) with a sign convention that is consistent across every level of the rollup.

What axis-184 *does not* produce is any quantity in raw token units. The Savage test asserts whether the second half is significantly larger or smaller than the first half on the rank-transformed exponential-scores scale. It does not say *by how many tokens*. The verdict is binary at any chosen alpha (REJECT / no-reject) and ordinal at the p-value level (smaller p = stronger evidence), but the inferential output never re-enters the original token unit space.

## Inferential-posture position 2: axis-185 BWS as an *unsigned* location-and-scale *omnibus*

The Baumgartner-Weiss-Schindler 1998 omnibus is a step structurally up from axis-184. It is still rank-based and still distribution-free, but instead of testing for location alone it tests for *any* departure between the two empirical CDFs — location shift, scale shift, mixed shift, or pure shape change — by integrating the squared standardised CDF difference weighted by 1 / (F̄(1 − F̄)) where F̄ is the pooled empirical CDF. The deviance, conventionally written `bwsB`, is *non-negative* by construction and the asymptotic null is non-Gaussian (the BWS authors derived a series approximation; modern implementations use either the series or a permutation reference).

Because the deviance is unsigned, axis-185 alone cannot say in *which direction* the second half departs from the first half. It can say the two halves are different (small p) or fail to reject (large p), and it can say the *magnitude* of departure on its own scale (large `bwsB` means stronger departure), but the direction of the shift on either the location or scale axis must come from elsewhere. The live-smoke output captured in commit `8e2b459`'s changelog is instructive:

- `vscode-cp bwsB=131.97 p=1.00e-15 sign=0 pure-scale-departure REJECT` — extreme departure, *but the sign is zero*, meaning when the BWS deviance is decomposed into location and scale components on the same data, the location component contributes near-zero and the scale component dominates. This is the only "sign=0" row across the four real sources for axis-185.
- `claude-code bwsB=18.99 p=6.66e-11 sign=+ REJECT` — strong departure, sign positive, indicating the second half is dominantly larger on location (and the BWS deviance is consistent with location being the primary axis of departure).
- `openclaw bwsB=5.12 p=1.80e-3 sign=- REJECT` — moderate departure, sign negative.
- `hermes bwsB=1.44 p=1.69e-1 sign=+ no-reject` — no departure detected; sign carried for completeness but not actionable at this alpha.

The cross-axis joiner `classifyBwsSavageCompound` in commit `a7d9d1c` does the work of reconciling the *signed* axis-184 Savage output with the *unsigned* axis-185 BWS deviance into seven mutually exclusive "shape of departure" buckets, where the buckets distinguish e.g. "both axes signal location departure with the same sign" from "axis-184 signals location, axis-185 signals omnibus, signs disagree" from "axis-185 signals departure but axis-184 does not" (= pure-scale or pure-shape, since axis-184 is location-specific). The seven-bucket classifier is the structural mechanism by which the triplet starts to behave like a coherent inferential surface rather than two independently signalling tests.

## Inferential-posture position 3: axis-186 Hodges-Lehmann as a *signed point estimator with a distribution-free CI*

Axis-186 is the structural completion of the arc. The Hodges-Lehmann 1963 estimator is

    hlDelta = median{ y_j - x_i : 1 ≤ i ≤ n1, 1 ≤ j ≤ n2 }

i.e. the median of all `n1 × n2` pairwise (second-half-day minus first-half-day) token-count differences. The output is a single number in *raw token units*, signed (positive means the second half's median is larger by `hlDelta` tokens), and accompanied by a Lehmann 1963 distribution-free confidence interval `[D_(k+1), D_(m-k)]` where `m = n1 × n2`, `k = round(E[U] - z_{alpha/2} √Var[U])`, `E[U] = n1·n2/2`, `Var[U] = n1·n2·(n1+n2+1)/12`. The CI's coverage is *exact* under any continuous F (it depends only on rank invariance), and it is reported in raw token units alongside `hlDelta`, so the inferential output is now a triple `(hlDelta, hlCiLow, hlCiHigh)` plus the diagnostic `hlCiWidth = hlCiHigh - hlCiLow` and the Boolean `hlCiExcludesZero`.

The CHANGELOG entry for axis-186 in commit `5006d26` makes the structural-orthogonality argument explicit:

> Every prior halves axis (115 Mann-Whitney, 170 Ansari-Bradley, 174 Cucconi, 175 Lepage, 177 Klotz, 178 Conover, 179 Mood, 180 Sukhatme, 181 van-der-Waerden, 182 Fligner-Policello, 183 Yuen-Welch, 184 Savage, 185 BWS) is a TEST STATISTIC yielding a Z or chi-square deviance with an asymptotic p-value. NONE of them produce a POINT ESTIMATE in the ORIGINAL TOKEN UNITS of the median shift between the two halves, and none produce a DISTRIBUTION-FREE CI on that shift. Axis-186 fills exactly that gap: it is the COMPANION ESTIMATOR to the existing rank-test family.

The Pitman ARE result the changelog cites — `3/π ≈ 0.955` vs the mean-difference under normal F, `π²/9 ≈ 1.097` under logistic F, `> 1` under any heavy-tailed F (Hodges-Lehmann 1963 Theorem 1; Lehmann 1975 *Nonparametrics* §2.3) — is the answer to the obvious question "why use HL instead of just the difference of means?" The HL estimator pays a small efficiency penalty under exact-normal F (about 4.5% larger asymptotic variance) in exchange for being insensitive to outliers, robust to heavy tails, and equivalent to the Mann-Whitney rank-sum test in the inversion sense (the HL CI excludes zero at level alpha if and only if the MW two-sided test rejects at level alpha). This last property is the bridge that the cross-axis joiner `classifyHlMwShiftAgreement` in commit `f4cc22f` makes explicit: it joins the signed HL shift estimate with the signed Mann-Whitney rank-sum Z (axis-115) into six mutually exclusive shift-agreement buckets, handling the opposite sign conventions of the two statistics in code so that downstream readers see a single agreed sign.

## What the triplet adds beyond what 13 prior axes already provided

The pre-184 halves family had broad coverage of the *test* posture: location tests (115, 176, 181, 182, 183), pure-scale tests (170, 174 joint-rank, 175, 177, 178, 179, 180), and the joint location-and-scale Cucconi-Lepage line (174, 175). What it lacked, structurally, was the *point estimator* posture and the *omnibus* posture. The 184/185/186 triplet plugs both holes in the same release window, and the cross-axis joiners shipped alongside (`classifyBwsSavageCompound`, `classifyHlMwShiftAgreement`) make the new axes immediately useful by reconciling them with the existing rank-test cohort instead of leaving them as standalone signals.

The inferential-posture progression — *test* of pure location → *omnibus* over location-and-scale → *point estimate* in raw units with distribution-free CI — is exactly the canonical workflow that any nonparametric two-sample analysis needs to answer the three sequential questions: (1) *Is there any difference?* (omnibus, axis-185), (2) *Is there a location-specific difference?* (signed test, axis-184 alongside the prior 13 axes), and (3) *How big is the location difference, with what uncertainty?* (point estimate + CI, axis-186). Before the 184/185/186 triplet, a reader could answer (2) with high confidence and (1) only via approximate decomposition of axis-174 Cucconi or axis-175 Lepage, and could not answer (3) at all without leaving the rank-based family entirely. After the triplet, all three questions can be answered with axes that share the same distributional assumptions (continuous F, no parametric form), the same input partition (first half vs second half of the gap-filled daily-token series), and the same per-source rollup discipline (signed-Stouffer for the signed axes, unsigned-aggregation for the omnibus).

The cross-axis joiners are also worth noting independently. `classifyBwsSavageCompound` produces a 7-bucket "shape of departure" classifier: same-sign location departure, opposite-sign location departure, pure-scale departure (axis-185 fires, axis-184 does not), pure-shape departure (axis-185 fires, no detectable location or scale on either component), no detectable departure on either axis, and two transition buckets where one axis is at the boundary. `classifyHlMwShiftAgreement` produces a 6-bucket "shift agreement" typology: both axes agree on a positive shift with CI excluding zero, both agree on a negative shift with CI excluding zero, MW rejects but HL CI straddles zero, HL CI excludes zero but MW does not reject (rare in theory by inversion equivalence, possible in practice via different tie-handling), neither rejects, and the sign-disagreement bucket (also rare under inversion equivalence). These are the typology layers that turn the per-axis output into something a daemon log or a metapost can quote without re-deriving the shape every time.

## What the triplet does *not* do

The 184/185/186 triplet is silent on the question of *whether the location-shift model is the right model*. The HL estimator is unbiased under the location-shift assumption Y = X + θ; if the true generative process is e.g. a multiplicative shift with heteroscedasticity, the HL point estimate still computes but its interpretation as "median shift between the two halves" is no longer clean. The BWS omnibus is precisely the axis that detects model-misspecification of this kind — a high `bwsB` with a `sign=0` decomposition (the vscode-cp `sign=0` row above being the canonical example) is the live-smoke evidence that the location-shift assumption is violated and that the HL point estimate from axis-186 should be read with that caveat.

The triplet also does not extend the prior-art family of *paired* analyses. All three new axes are two-sample on the half-vs-half partition, not paired-sample on day-vs-prior-day or paired-source on per-source-vs-per-source. The paired family is a separate axis lineage and the 184/185/186 triplet does not touch it.

## Triplet-as-closing-arc reading

Read as a closing arc, the 184/185/186 triplet completes the W17 daily-token-halves family in the inferential-posture sense: the family now spans the three canonical postures (test, omnibus, estimator + CI), shares a single partition definition (first-half vs second-half of the gap-filled daily-token series), shares a distributional discipline (continuous F, rank-based, distribution-free), and exposes the shape of cross-axis agreement via two small typology classifiers. Whether the family is *also* closed in the count-of-axes sense — i.e. whether axis-187 onward will start a new lineage rather than add a 17th rank-based test — is a separable question. The pure-existence of axis-186 as the first non-test member of the lineage is the structural argument that the *posture-completeness* answer is "yes, as of `v0.6.470`."
