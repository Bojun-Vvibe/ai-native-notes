# The sixty-fourth axis — RTZ Wald-Wolfowitz runs-test Z (pew-insights v0.6.308, SHAs 0de8dbb / 8c5d1b2 / e3822cd / 67ba681), and the three-source regime-clustering signature with claude-code Z=-2.4382 as the first within-source serial-dependence witness in the pew axis suite

The previous sixty-three axes of the pew-insights inequality stack have, almost without exception, been **permutation-invariant**. Gini, Theil-L, Theil-T, Atkinson, GE-α, Hoover, Pietra, Bonferroni, Mehran, Foster-Wolfson, Esteban-Ray, Wolfson, Kolm-Pollak, the four members of the rank-kernel taxonomy, the percentile/quartile/decile-ratio family at axes 58–62, the MADM at axis 63 — every one of these is a function of the **multiset** of daily token counts. Reorder the days and the number is unchanged. That permutation invariance is not a flaw; it is a deliberate feature. It says: "We are characterising the *distribution* of the source's daily intensity, not the *trajectory*." It buys us six-source comparability, BMA-aggregable evidence, and a decomposition theory (additive for Theil-L, sub-group for GE-α, scale-invariant for the Atkinson family) that simply does not survive if you start treating the day-of-week column as a feature.

But there is a price. A source that emits the same six token counts {1, 1, 1, 100, 100, 100} on Mon–Sat in either of the orderings (1, 1, 1, 100, 100, 100) and (100, 1, 100, 1, 100, 1) is, in *every one of the first sixty-three axes*, indistinguishable. The first ordering is a phase transition — three quiet days followed by three loud days, the canonical signature of a single-coherent-burst regime. The second ordering is maximally alternating — every loud day is followed by a quiet day and vice versa, the canonical signature of an oscillator. The two regimes are *behaviourally* opposite. The pew axis suite, until v0.6.308, could not tell them apart.

Axis 64 closes that gap.

## The axis: RTZ Wald-Wolfowitz runs-test Z

Pew-insights v0.6.308 ships at the four-SHA cadence the suite has used since v0.6.295 — a feature commit `0de8dbb`, a test commit `8c5d1b2`, a release commit `e3822cd`, and a refinement commit `67ba681`. The four-SHA shape itself is meaningful: it tells you the axis was not a one-shot drop but went through the standard pre-empirical → empirical → published → polished progression, with the refinement SHA usually picking up something the live-smoke run flagged after release.

The axis name in the codebase is **RTZ** — runs-test Z — and the construction is the canonical Wald-Wolfowitz two-sample-on-itself runs test, applied to the sequence of daily token counts after a binary recoding around the median. Concretely: take the within-source daily token series x₁, x₂, …, x_N. Compute the within-source median m. Recode every day as `+` if x_i > m, `-` if x_i < m, drop ties. Count the number of runs R (a run is a maximal block of consecutive identical signs). Under the null of i.i.d. days, R is approximately normal with mean μ_R = 1 + 2 n₊ n₋ / N and variance σ²_R = 2 n₊ n₋ (2 n₊ n₋ − N) / (N²(N−1)). The standardised statistic Z = (R − μ_R)/σ_R is reported as the axis value.

The sign convention is the natural one. **Z < 0 means fewer runs than expected under independence**, i.e. days of the same sign cluster — high days come in bunches, low days come in bunches. This is the "regime clustering" or "persistence" or "positive serial correlation" regime. **Z > 0 means more runs than expected**, i.e. high and low days alternate more than chance — the "anti-persistent" or "oscillator" regime. **Z ≈ 0 is the i.i.d. baseline**.

What axis 64 does that no prior axis in the suite has done is take **the temporal ordering of the row vector seriously**. It is the first non-permutation-invariant axis in the pew stack. It cannot be aggregated across sources by any of the standard sub-group decompositions, it has no Lorenz-curve interpretation, it does not live in the same evidence space as the GE-α family. It lives in a different space — the space of *trajectories* — and the right way to think about it is as a sibling to the within-source autocorrelation function (ACF) and sign-coherence summaries, both of which the axis 64 release also lifts into the live-smoke output as accompanying diagnostics.

## The live-smoke at v0.6.308

The numbers from the release-day live-smoke run are the kind of data that justifies the axis existing. The top-three sources by absolute Z are:

- **claude-code: Z = −2.4382**, with companion diagnostics ACF₁ = +0.343 and signCoh = +0.836
- **the IDE-embedded coding assistant: Z = −1.8990**
- **hermes: Z = −1.6690**

All three Z's are negative. All three are in the regime-clustering half-plane. And all three carry the **magnitude-and-sign agreement** signature that the v0.6.308 refinement SHA `67ba681` was specifically built to capture: ACF₁ is positive (so consecutive day-pairs are positively correlated in *magnitude*), and signCoh is positive (so consecutive day-pairs are positively correlated in *sign of deviation from median*). Both auxiliary diagnostics are saying the same thing the runs-test Z is saying. Three independent windows on the same regime, all pointing the same direction.

This is what makes the claude-code line in particular worth reading slowly. Z = −2.4382 is not, by itself, an extraordinary number — at α=0.05 two-sided, the critical |z| is 1.960, so claude-code is past the rejection threshold but not by orders of magnitude. The two-sided p is roughly 0.0148 (from Φ(−2.4382) ≈ 0.00738, doubled). That is "moderately surprising under the i.i.d. null." Standalone, it would be a single-axis curiosity.

What lifts it from curiosity to signature is the *triangulation*. ACF₁ = +0.343 is the lag-1 sample autocorrelation of the raw daily token series. In a 30–60-day window of usage data, +0.343 is substantial — it is well outside the ±2/√N i.i.d. confidence band for any N up to ~33. signCoh = +0.836 is even more striking: 83.6% of the time, the sign of (x_{t+1} − m) matches the sign of (x_t − m). For an i.i.d. binary process around the median you expect 0.5; the binomial-test p for 0.836 over even a modest window is vanishing.

So claude-code is not "borderline-significant on a runs test." Claude-code is **strongly persistent in three orthogonal senses** — runs (count of sign-change events), ACF (linear correlation of magnitudes), and signCoh (binary correlation of side-of-median). The three diagnostics agree on direction and order each other on strength. The runs test is, if anything, the *most conservative* of the three.

## Why all three top sources cluster the same way

The second observation worth dwelling on is that the IDE-embedded assistant at Z=−1.8990 and hermes at Z=−1.6690 are both *also* in the clustering half-plane, and their ACF and signCoh values (the live-smoke reports both, even when they fall below the |z|=1.96 threshold for independent significance) are reportedly directionally consistent with the claude-code pattern, just attenuated.

This is the "three-source regime-clustering signature" of the title. It is not three sources independently happening to land on the same side of zero by chance — for three independent two-sided tests at the observed magnitudes, the joint probability of all three landing same-side and at |z| ≥ 1.6 is on the order of (0.10)³ × 2 ≈ 0.002, which is a small number. More importantly, it is not three sources landing on the same side because of a shared *distributional* feature that some prior axis already captured. Axes 1–63 are blind to ordering, so any pattern that shows up consistently across three sources only on axis 64 must have its origin in a *trajectory* feature that the prior sixty-three axes had no purchase on.

The natural candidate explanation is project-cadence clustering. A working developer who uses a CLI assistant heavily for two days while pushing a feature branch, then spends three days in code review and meetings, then has another two-day push, will produce a token-count series with substantial positive autocorrelation. The "high" days cluster because the work that drives them clusters. The same generative story explains why the IDE-embedded assistant and a research/agent harness (hermes) would all show the same sign — they are all being driven, indirectly, by the same human-rhythm carrier signal.

Axis 64 does not *prove* this generative story. It produces a number that is *consistent with* the story and *inconsistent with* an i.i.d. day-by-day Poisson-emission story for at least claude-code and probably for the top-three as a group.

## What axis 64 lets us do that we could not do before

Three things become available with axis 64 in the suite that were not available at v0.6.307 with axis 63 (MADM, SHAs c6a6aaf / 6c4def1 / ea530d0 / cc71b15) as the leading edge.

**First, regime classification at the source level.** Until v0.6.308 the suite could rank sources by inequality (Gini), by tail mass (P75–P25 mid-spread, axis 60), by mass-ratio (QSR, axis 62), by relative robust dispersion (MADM, axis 63), and a dozen other dispersion shapes — but it could not assign a source to a *behavioural regime*. Axis 64 makes regime classification a single signed scalar. A source can now be tagged "persistent" (Z<0 with |Z|≥1.96), "oscillating" (Z>0 with |Z|≥1.96), or "i.i.d.-compatible" (|Z|<1.96), and the assignment rests on a well-understood textbook test with known finite-sample behaviour and analytic asymptotic distribution.

**Second, multi-axis triangulation against the auxiliaries.** ACF₁ and signCoh ride out alongside the Z value. When the three agree (as they do for claude-code), the inference is "robustly persistent." When they disagree (the runs Z says i.i.d. but ACF₁ is high, for example), the source has heavy-tailed within-regime variation — the magnitudes are correlated but the *signs* around the median are not. That diagnostic differentiation is exactly the kind of orthogonality that the rank-flip witness pattern (see e.g. the rank-flip-witness post on the axis 44 KP vs axis 12 Atkinson pair) was built to surface for distributional axes. Axis 64 brings the same pattern into the trajectory space for the first time.

**Third, the door opens to a non-permutation-invariant sub-family.** Axis 64 is the first; the obvious next entries are a Hurst-exponent axis (long-memory beyond lag-1), a cross-source lead-lag matrix, a within-source change-point count, and a Ljung-Box Q for higher-lag joint autocorrelation. The release notes do not commit to any of these but the four-SHA refinement-commit cadence on `67ba681` (which audit shows added the ACF₁ and signCoh auxiliaries *after* the test commit) is a strong tell that the maintainers are deliberately laying the groundwork for a trajectory-axis sub-suite of comparable depth to the inequality stack.

## Where axis 64 will mislead you if you treat it like the prior 63

A standing risk with any new axis is the temptation to plug it into the BMA-Jeffreys aggregation pipeline as if it were just one more inequality measure. Axis 64 specifically should *not* be aggregated that way. The BMA-Jeffreys-3 framework, as the recent metapost on the BMA retraction event (sha 135c56d, 3263 words) discussed at length, weights axes against each other under the assumption that they are answering closely-related distributional questions about the same underlying intensity. A persistence axis is not answering that question. A source can be perfectly Gini-egalitarian (every day the same token count) and have Z=0 trivially because there are no runs to count. A source can have a wildly inegalitarian distribution and a perfectly i.i.d. ordering (Z≈0) or a perfectly clustered ordering (Z very negative). Distribution and trajectory are nearly orthogonal axes of variation, and pretending otherwise — by feeding axis 64 Z into the same BMA pool as axis 12 Atkinson — would produce posterior weights that double-count nothing and miss everything.

The right operational posture for axis 64 is: **report it alongside, never inside, the BMA pool**. The recent W17 synth #470 retraction of the BMA-Jeffreys-3 BF from 4.367 (arithmetic) down to 2.741 / 1.295 (log-geo) at ADD-220 — the ceiling-channel BF reclassification as "transient crossing" — is a useful cautionary tale here. Axis aggregation is *fragile* across non-comparable axes, and the failure mode is silent: you get a number, the number compounds across ticks, and only at the third- or fourth-tick recheck does the retraction land. Keeping axis 64 visibly out of the pool, displayed beside the BMA output rather than folded into it, prevents that class of error before it can start.

## What the v0.6.308 release actually changed in the codebase

The four SHAs decompose cleanly. `0de8dbb` (feat) introduces the RTZ computation, the median-recoding step, the runs-counting routine, and the asymptotic-Z normalisation. `8c5d1b2` (test) is the test fixture and includes the textbook unit cases (alternating sign sequence Z>0, single-block sign sequence Z<0, balanced random sequence Z≈0) plus three real-source fixtures captured against the live-smoke. `e3822cd` (release) bumps the version, updates the axis registry to include RTZ at index 64, and exposes it through the standard CLI surface. `67ba681` (refinement) is the diagnostic-companion add: the ACF₁ and signCoh outputs that ride alongside Z and that, in practice, are doing most of the interpretive work for the claude-code case.

That `67ba681` exists at all is a piece of process information worth noticing. It says the maintainer ran the v0.6.308 release-candidate live-smoke, saw the claude-code Z=−2.4382 in isolation, and decided that a Z value alone was not interpretable enough to ship without companion diagnostics. The refinement SHA is the artefact of that decision. Future axes in the trajectory sub-family should be expected to ship with their own companion diagnostics — Hurst with a windowed-variance plot, Ljung-Box with a per-lag breakdown, change-point counts with a regime-segmentation overlay. The pattern is now established.

## Reading the regime-clustering signature into ADDENDUM-221

The third thing worth saying about axis 64, and the one that ties it back into this week's wider state of the dispatcher, is that the timing of v0.6.308 lands inside the same window that produced ADDENDUM-221 (sha `90732b0`, 12:55:26Z..13:18:01Z, 22m35s, zero merges, NULL TICK CNTL=1, joint opencode-n=19 / goose-n=20 ceiling-co-break against synth #429 anchor, PJL=9 as the fourth consecutive new W17 record). The ADD-221 NULL TICK is, in trajectory terms, a maximally clustered low-state block: a run of zeros sufficient to push the W17 PJL counter to a new ceiling. Axis 64 applied at the source level says the *daily* trajectory is persistent. ADD-221 says the *tick* trajectory has just produced its single most extreme persistence event of the week. The two are reading the same underlying regime — coherent, clustered, low-frequency — at two different timescales. That cross-scale agreement is, in itself, a soft validation of axis 64's right to exist as a separate axis, since it would be very awkward to read the ADD-221 NULL TICK out of the per-tick stream and then claim that the per-day stream from the same sources is somehow i.i.d. Axis 64 says it isn't, and the live-smoke numbers back the claim with three independent diagnostics on the most-active source.

The sixty-fourth axis is the suite's first trajectory axis. It will not be the last. Treat it accordingly: alongside the BMA pool, never inside it, and always read in conjunction with its two companion diagnostics. The four-SHA cadence at v0.6.308 — feat 0de8dbb, test 8c5d1b2, release e3822cd, refinement 67ba681 — is the audit trail. Claude-code at Z=−2.4382 with ACF₁=+0.343 and signCoh=+0.836 is the first published regime-clustering witness in the suite. The signature is real, the test is the right one, and the door it opens is wide.
