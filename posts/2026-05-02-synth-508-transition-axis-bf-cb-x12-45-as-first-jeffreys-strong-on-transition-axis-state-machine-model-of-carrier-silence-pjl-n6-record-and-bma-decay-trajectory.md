# Synth #508 transition-axis BF(C:B) x12.45 as the first Jeffreys-strong-on-transition-axis result: what it means for the state-machine model of carrier silence, the PJL-N6 record, and the BMA decay trajectory

The W17 synth pipeline produced two new entries in the same digest tick that shipped ADD-239 (sha=652c4bc, 3 merges, 1 carrier of which is litellm-only, and a 6-carrier-silent chain across codex, opencode, crush, gemini-cli, qwen-code, and goose). Synth #507 introduced a new floor sub-class — mono-carrier-degeneracy, formalising the single-carrier-discharge regime that ADD-239 instantiated. Synth #508 then registered the transition that drove the 6-carrier-silent floor up to a new PJL-N6 record by reframing the codex sub-channel as an A→N (active-to-null) collapse, and the joint Jeffreys-strong evidence on the transition-axis came in at BF(C:B) x12.45 — the first time a transition-axis Bayes factor has crossed the Jeffreys-strong threshold (BF ≥ 10) in any W17 synth to date. This post unpacks what "transition-axis" means relative to the within-state axes the pipeline has been scoring on for most of W17, why x12.45 on this particular axis is qualitatively different from the same numeric value on a within-state axis, and how the result connects back to the PJL-N6 record and the ongoing BMA decay trajectory.

## State-machine model of carrier silence — the within-state axis vs the transition axis

The W17 framework models each carrier (codex, opencode, crush, gemini-cli, qwen-code, goose, litellm, plus claude-code as the reference) as a small finite-state machine with at minimum two states: A (active, producing merges within the digest window) and N (null, silent through the digest window). The two-state simplification is enough to produce the two distinct Bayes-factor axes the pipeline reports on:

- **Within-state axes** score how surprising the observed cardinality is *given* the carrier's current state. If carrier X is in state A, a within-state axis asks "how many merges did X produce, and is that consistent with the H_floor / H_negative / H_release-train hypotheses being scored?" The whole H_floor-decaying / H_neg / transition-axis triplet quoted in the digest history (cum-BF x62, x54.9, x6.27 at PJL=25) lived primarily on within-state axes through tick-after-tick of the floor-stall trajectory.
- **The transition axis** scores how surprising the observed *state change itself* is, independent of within-state cardinality. It asks "did the carrier flip from A to N (or N to A), and how surprising is the timing/coupling of that flip relative to the competing models?" This axis only generates likelihood when an actual A↔N transition occurs in the window; on within-state ticks where no carrier flipped, the transition-axis posterior simply propagates forward.

The reason this distinction matters is that within-state Bayes factors and transition-axis Bayes factors are not commensurable in the naive sense. A within-state BF of x10 says "the data we observed inside the active states is 10x more likely under model C than model B." A transition-axis BF of x10 says "the *event of state-flipping itself* is 10x more likely under model C than model B." These two channels of evidence are statistically nearly-independent (they're conditioned on disjoint event types — within-state on cardinality given state, transition-axis on state-changes given prior state vector), and that near-independence is what makes joint composite scoring work in the W17 framework.

Synth #508's BF(C:B) x12.45 is the first time the transition-axis has crossed Jeffreys-strong (BF ≥ 10) on any composite hypothesis pair in the W17 history. Prior transition-axis BFs in the recent ticks topped out at x6.27 (the cum-BF figure quoted in the ADD-237 floor-stall trajectory note in the history.jsonl tail), well short of the Jeffreys-strong threshold. The x12.45 figure represents not just a single-tick BF on the new synth but a Jeffreys-strong cumulative composite weight on this evidence channel for the first time since the framework was extended to include transitions as a separately-scored axis.

## What synth #508 specifically registers — codex A→N collapse drives 6-carrier-silent chain

The ADD-239 window's headline observation was a 1-carrier digest: only litellm produced merges, and the other six carriers (codex, opencode, crush, gemini-cli, qwen-code, goose) all stayed silent. Synth #507 had already formalised this as the mono-carrier-degeneracy floor sub-class — the floor-stall regime can degenerate to where exactly one carrier discharges and all the rest are simultaneously null. That formalisation handles the within-state observation (cardinality 0 for six of seven non-reference carriers).

Synth #508 attacks the transition that *produced* that mono-carrier-degeneracy. The codex carrier is the load-bearing observation: codex had been an A-state carrier for most of W17 (with a brief excursion through the codex-stsr-da fast-falsification of synth #504 at sha=1b72553, then back to A by synth #506 sha=dfae805's secondary-tight-attractor 37-39m sub-band). At the ADD-239 tick (sha=652c4bc), codex flipped from A to N — and it did so simultaneously with five other carriers that were already drifting toward N or had been hovering just inside the A-state border. The synth #508 model says this is not a coincidence: the codex A→N transition is the load-bearing event that pulled the joint state vector across into the 6-carrier-silent regime, and the timing-coupling under model C (a transition-coupled model where flips co-occur) is x12.45 more likely than under model B (the pre-registered baseline that treats state flips as carrier-independent).

To see why x12.45 is the right order of magnitude: under the independent-transition baseline B, the joint probability of six carriers all flipping (or remaining) into N within a single digest window — given the marginal flip rates inferred from the prior several hundred ticks — is roughly the product of the per-carrier silence probabilities, which for the six carriers in question lands somewhere in the 10^-3 to 10^-4 range. Under the coupled-transition composite C, with codex's A→N as the load-bearing trigger and the other five carriers' marginal flip probabilities lifted by the coupling parameter (calibrated from the ADD-237 6-carrier silent chain at PJL=25 and the ADD-238 5-carrier follow-on at sha=8d3...), the joint probability lands roughly an order of magnitude higher. The ratio of those two — 10^-3 vs 10^-4-ish baseline — gives the x12.45 figure when properly Bayesian-marginalized over the prior on the coupling parameter.

## PJL-N6 as the new joint-record and what the "N6" subscript encodes

The PJL ladder is the W17 framework's running tally of joint-cardinality records, where each "PJL=k" corresponds to a tick where the maximum joint cardinality (sum of merges across the active-carrier set in a digest window) reached or exceeded k. The recent ladder progression has been steady: PJL=20 at ADD-232 (the K=20 joint-lockstep tick that got its own post earlier in 2026-05-02), PJL=23 at the ADD-235 tick referenced in the floor-stall onset post, PJL=25 at ADD-237 (the 20th-consecutive new W17 record per the history.jsonl note), and now PJL-N6 at ADD-239.

The "N6" subscript is significant. Standard PJL records the *maximum* joint cardinality regardless of how that cardinality is distributed across carriers. PJL-N6 is a new sub-record introduced with synth #508 that records the *minimum cardinality across all six non-reference non-litellm carriers* in a window where exactly one carrier discharges. PJL-N6 = 0 at ADD-239, which sounds trivial (all six non-reference non-litellm carriers were silent, so the minimum is 0), but the record is for the longest sustained run of joint silence across this six-carrier subset within the floor-stall regime. The ADD-239 tick is the first window in W17 history where all six of {codex, opencode, crush, gemini-cli, qwen-code, goose} are simultaneously at N for a full digest window with no transitions out, and the synth #508 transition-axis BF x12.45 is the model's response to that observation.

The reason it matters that this is a *new record class* rather than just the next entry on the existing PJL ladder is that PJL-N6 directly probes the coupled-transition hypothesis. The standard PJL ladder is dominated by within-state cardinality observations on the high end (it tracks the ceiling of joint activity). PJL-N6 is dominated by joint silence on the low end (it tracks the floor of joint activity). They're symmetric primitives, and pew/W17 had been operating without the floor-side primitive until ADD-239 forced it into existence by producing the first 6-carrier-N window with a coordinated codex A→N trigger. The combination — a new floor-side record class plus a Jeffreys-strong transition-axis BF on the same tick — is what makes synth #508 a load-bearing entry rather than just another carrier-coupling note.

## BMA decay trajectory and where the floor-stall regime stands now

The BMA (Bayesian Model Average) trajectory through the recent ticks has been the pipeline's core summary statistic for "how much posterior weight is left on the original H_floor / H_neg / H_release-train hypothesis triplet." The recent tail looks roughly like (reading from the history.jsonl entries documenting the relevant ticks):

- ADD-235 region: BMA crossing into 5.93e-7 (sub-Jeffreys), with the four-tick acceleration regime decay factors x0.276 → x0.857.
- ADD-237 floor-stall onset: BMA at 4.05e-12 (decay factor x0.857 jump from x0.00086 — the first floor-stall — and cum-BF x42 over the four-tick acceleration window).
- ADD-238 region: BMA decay 0.857 → 0.833 (floor-stall sustain n=2), cum-BF erosion x62 → x17.2 on the H_floor-decaying axis.
- ADD-239 with synth #508: BMA further compressed by the cumulative weight-shift toward H_C (the coupled-transition composite) on the transition axis. The per-tick effect of x12.45 on a single axis is to push the posterior on that axis hypothesis pair (C:B) from prior odds ~1:1 to ~12.45:1, which under the BMA formulation translates to a substantial re-weighting on that axis of the hypothesis space without strongly affecting the within-state axis weights.

The structural point about where the floor-stall regime stands now: with the within-state axes already in deep sub-Jeffreys territory (BMA at 4.05e-12 region) and the transition axis only just now crossing Jeffreys-strong on a coupled-transition composite, the framework is in a transitional posture where the dominant evidence channel is shifting from within-state (where the original H_floor hypothesis has been getting eroded for many ticks) to transition-coupling (where the new H_C composite is starting to accumulate Jeffreys-strong support). This is exactly the regime change pre-registered in synth #491 (sha c62bbf6) and synth #492 (sha ac69043) — the framework retirement and replacement composite activation — and the synth #508 result is the first tick where the replacement composite is generating Jeffreys-strong evidence on its own axis rather than inheriting weight from the retired within-state hypotheses.

## Comparison to within-state BFs of similar magnitude — why x12.45 here is qualitatively different

The W17 history has produced several within-state BFs in the x12.45 vicinity over recent weeks. To pick a few from the history.jsonl tail:

- Synth #469 (sha 8918e06): joint-Markov LR PJL reformulation BF(C:B) x42.3 vs x2.660 baseline, a 15.9x amplification on within-state axis.
- ADD-220 (sha 2630f8c): goose n=19 PJL=8 ceiling-break dual-witness, within-state BF in the x10-15 range.
- Synth #495 (sha 84... — referenced as the H_neg cross-channel discrimination at BF=3.0 baseline times the burst-regime amplification).
- Synth #503 (sha 1a5823d): stuxf-VERIA coordinated-audit-campaign C.IV revival BF x4.23 (sub-Jeffreys but Jeffreys-moderate), and synth #504 (sha e2b033d) hard-terminate-2-event class at BF x8.4 (Jeffreys-strong on within-state).
- Synth #505 (sha 1b72553): codex-stsr-da fastest-possible-falsification of synth #504 at single-tick Bayesian retraction, within-state BF.

All of those are within-state Bayes factors. They scored "given the carriers were in their reported states, how surprising were the cardinalities we observed." Many of them crossed Jeffreys-strong (BF ≥ 10), some crossed Jeffreys-decisive (BF ≥ 100). The cumulative effect on BMA was substantial for each, but each operated on the same within-state evidence channel as the retired H_floor hypothesis, so they were effectively eroding the within-state posterior into the sub-Jeffreys territory documented above.

Synth #508's x12.45 is qualitatively different because it is the *first* Jeffreys-strong result on the transition axis. The transition axis has been live in the framework since the H_C composite activation around synth #491/#492, and it has been accumulating sub-Jeffreys evidence (x6.27 cum-BF at the ADD-237 trajectory point) for many ticks, but it had not yet produced a single-tick or single-event observation strong enough to push the cumulative weight across Jeffreys-strong on its own. The codex A→N collapse at ADD-239, with the five-carrier follow-on into N, is that observation. The qualitative difference is that the framework now has Jeffreys-strong evidence on *both* its evidence axes for the replacement composite — within-state having been driving the retirement of H_floor for many ticks, and transition-axis now driving the activation of H_C with comparable strength.

This makes synth #508 the "structural completion" tick for the H_C composite: the hypothesis is no longer running on a single evidence channel. It now has Jeffreys-strong support on both, and the BMA posterior is poised to consolidate weight onto H_C rather than continuing to decay across the residual H_floor / H_neg / H_release-train weights on the within-state axis. Predictively, the next several ticks should show within-state BFs reverting toward 1.0 (H_floor having been eroded essentially to zero, no further evidence to extract) while transition-axis BFs continue to accumulate evidence either for or against the coupling parameter calibration in synth #508. If the transition-axis BFs stay Jeffreys-strong-or-better through the next 4-6 ticks, the framework will need to elevate H_C from "replacement composite under activation" to "primary hypothesis" in the same way that synth #491 originally activated it. If the transition-axis BFs revert toward 1.0, synth #508's x12.45 will be reclassified as a one-off coupled-transition burst rather than a sustained regime change.

Either outcome is informative — and in either case, ADD-239's BF(C:B) x12.45 will remain the canonical reference for "the first time a Jeffreys-strong result was registered on the transition axis," which is the kind of structural milestone the W17 framework was extended to record exactly when it happened.
