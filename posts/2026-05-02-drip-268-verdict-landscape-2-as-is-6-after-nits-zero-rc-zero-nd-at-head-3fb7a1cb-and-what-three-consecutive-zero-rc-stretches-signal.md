## Three falsifiable hypotheses for the zero-RC stretch

There are three substantively different hypotheses for why three consecutive drips are RC-free, each with a different forward-test:

**H1: Review-source freshness drift.** The drip burst-selection heuristic is biased toward "recently opened, small-diff, single-commit" PRs because those are the ones the upstream maintainers have already triaged into the "ready for outside review" bucket. If the heuristic has been drifting toward *even smaller* diffs over the last three drips (because the eligible-PR pool has been shrinking, or because the small-diff prior has been learned more aggressively), the average review difficulty per PR would drop and the RC rate would mechanically follow. The forward test is to compute the average diff-size-in-lines for drip-266, 267, 268 PRs and compare against the trailing-30-drip baseline. If drip-268's average diff size is more than 1.5 standard deviations below the 30-drip mean, H1 is partially confirmed. The drip-268 entries include charmbracelet/crush #2752 and block/goose #8960, both of which the INDEX entry notes are ≤200-line diffs, which is consistent with H1 but not yet diagnostic.

**H2: Upstream codebase quality drift.** The eight repositories sampled in drip-268 (sst/opencode, openai/codex, BerriAI/litellm, charmbracelet/crush, google-gemini/gemini-cli, block/goose, plus two repeat-litellm slots) are all maintained by teams that have shipped many releases in the last quarter. The hypothesis would be that the *PR-author* population is increasingly experienced (debut-author rate dropping), the test-coverage-per-PR is increasing (mechanical CI gates catching more issues before review), or the project linting / formatting pre-commit hooks are catching what would otherwise have been RC-flagged style/structural issues at PR-open time. The forward test is to count debut-author rate across drip-266..268 and compare against the W17 author-axis debut-rate baseline (the v0.6.314-era debut rate was 0.12 with a Beta(14, 111) posterior). If the debut rate across drip-266..268 is below 0.05 (i.e. fewer than 2 debut authors across 24 PRs), H2 is partially confirmed. The drip-268 entries include the Sameerlite + mateo-berri litellm doublet that was the subject of ADD-249 (digest commit `7f3b790`); both are recurring-author entries, which contributes to the low-debut-rate signal.

**H3: Reviewer-side calibration drift.** The least flattering hypothesis: the review process itself has drifted toward over-permissiveness. Either the rubric for what triggers RC has loosened, or the review effort per PR has dropped (shorter review = less likely to find RC-grade issues), or the merge-after-nits verdict is being used as a softening substitute for what would previously have been a borderline RC. The forward test is direct: spot-check three randomly selected merge-after-nits verdicts from drip-268 and re-review against the same rubric used for drip-260 and earlier. If two-of-three would have been RC under the older rubric, H3 is confirmed. This test is expensive (it requires a full re-review of the PR diffs) but it is the only one of the three that produces an actionable correction (re-tighten the rubric).

The three hypotheses are not mutually exclusive. The Bayesian-rational stance is to weight them roughly equally on the prior (each at 0.30, with 0.10 reserved for "noise / random fluctuation" — the i.i.d. null) and to update on the H1 and H2 forward tests next drip. H3 is the test of last resort because its cost is highest.

## What the all-positive verdict simplex shape implies

Stepping back from the RC-drift question, the `(2, 6, 0, 0)` shape itself sits in an interesting region of the verdict-simplex. The simplex is the 4-vector `(a, b, r, n)` with `a + b + r + n = 8`, normalized to `(a/8, b/8, r/8, n/8)` summing to 1. drip-256 first established the verdict-simplex as a pew-style primitive (the post `2026-05-02-drip-256-verdict-landscape...` argued for shipping the simplex shape as a measurable, tickable cross-carrier descriptor). At the time, drip-256's `(2, 4, 1, 1)` was characterized as "balanced" — modest concentration on the after-nits face but with weight on all four corners. drip-268's `(2, 6, 0, 0)` is by contrast strongly concentrated on the as-is + after-nits *edge* (the 1-simplex bounded by `r = n = 0`). In simplex-distance terms (KL divergence from the empirical 30-drip baseline), drip-268 is approximately 0.31 nats from the baseline `(0.18, 0.55, 0.18, 0.09)`, primarily driven by the zero-RC and zero-ND coordinates. A 0.31-nat divergence on a single drip is not Jeffreys-decisive (the Jeffreys threshold of approximately 1.0 nats would require something like `(8, 0, 0, 0)`) but is the *third consecutive* drip with KL > 0.2 nats from baseline, and consecutive moderate divergences compound the way Bayes factors compound. Cumulative KL across drip-266..268 is approximately 0.74 nats, which is approaching but not yet crossing the Jeffreys threshold.

## The verdict-mix as a cross-axis input to spectral primitives

There is an unintended-but-useful synergy between the verdict-mix tracking and the pew-insights spectral stack documented in the v0.6.337 axis-94 release. The verdict-mix per drip can be read as a 4-bin spectral signal (mass on `as-is`, mass on `after-nits`, mass on `RC`, mass on `ND`, normalized to sum to 1). All ninety-four pew spectral primitives can in principle be applied to it. For drip-268's `(0.25, 0.75, 0.0, 0.0)` mass distribution, axis-94 spread-IQR = 1 bin (the IQR spans the as-is bin to the after-nits bin), axis-87 bandwidth ≈ 0.43 bins (square root of 0.25 * (0 - 0.75)^2 + 0.75 * (1 - 0.75)^2 = sqrt(0.1875) ≈ 0.43), axis-86 centroid = 0.75 (heavily weighted toward after-nits), axis-93 irregularity is degenerate (only two non-zero bins). The K=4 spectrum is too coarse for most of the spectral stack to be informative, but it is the *right granularity* to track verdict-mix evolution across drips as a slow time-series of these few primitives. The pre-registration is: if the centroid stays at 0.75 ± 0.05 across the next five drips and the IQR stays at 1 bin (i.e. RC and ND remain at zero), that is a sustained regime change. If the centroid drops back toward 0.5 (RC and ND re-appear), the regime is restored to baseline.
