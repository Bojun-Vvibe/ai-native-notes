## What axis-222 actually computes

The Lombard 1987 smooth-changepoint statistic operates on the rank series, not the raw token-count series. Let `r[i]` be the rank of `x[i]` in `{x[1], ..., x[n]}`, normalized to `r[i] / (n+1)` so that the rank values are uniformly distributed on `(0,1)` under `H0`. The Wilcoxon-style score is `a[i] = r[i] - 1/2`, so under the no-shift null `E[a[i]] = 0` and `Var[a[i]] = 1/12 + O(1/n)`. The Lombard statistic accumulates a *smoothed* partial sum of these scores:

```
S(k) = sum_{i=1}^{k} W(i, k, n) * a[i]
```

where `W(i, k, n)` is a triangular kernel weight that linearly ramps from `0` at the segment boundary to `1` at the candidate split index `k` and back to `0` at the next boundary. This is the structural difference from SNHT: SNHT uses a step kernel (constant weight on each side of the split), Lombard uses a triangular kernel that *smooths* the transition. The standardized statistic is `L_n = max_k |S(k)| / sigma_n` where `sigma_n` is the asymptotic null SD of the Wilcoxon score sum.

The argmax `kStar = argmax_k |S(k)|` localizes the *center* of the smooth transition rather than the boundary of an abrupt step. That is the load-bearing semantic difference. SNHT's `aStar` is the index *immediately before* the step; Lombard's `kStar` is the index where the cumulative smoothed score is most extreme, which for a true smooth transition spread over `[a, b]` lands at roughly `(a+b)/2` rather than at `a`.

Critical values follow the asymptotic Lombard 1987 *Biometrika* 74:615–624 distribution of `max|B(t)|` for `B` a Brownian bridge on `[0,1]`, with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov-like tail bound `P(L_n >= c) ~ 2 * exp(-2 c^2)` for large `c`. The `significant05` decision is `L_n >= 1.358` (the 95th percentile of the limiting distribution). The defensive OR-rule mirrors axis-221: `pApprox < alpha` *or* `L_n >= cCrit05`.

The diagnostic surfaces are: `kStar` (1-based candidate split that maximizes `|S(k)|`), `kStarDay` (the calendar date corresponding to `kStar` in the gap-filled series), `direction` (sign of `S(kStar)` — `+` for ascending transition, `-` for descending), `meanShift` (raw token-count difference between left-of-`kStar` segment mean and right-of-`kStar` segment mean), and the Wilcoxon score sum itself for downstream re-aggregation.

## The live-smoke payload: vsc-redacted-a `L_n = 9.2110`, vsc-redacted-b `L_n = 8.9210`

The two cross-source decisive results from the v0.6.556 live-smoke run on the daily-token panel are massively over the `cCrit05 = 1.358` threshold. `L_n = 9.2110` on `vsc-redacted-a` corresponds to a tail probability of `P(L_n >= 9.21) ~ 2 * exp(-2 * 84.85) ~ 10^{-74}`, which is so far into the tail that the asymptotic approximation has lost its quantitative meaning — what matters is that the smoothed Wilcoxon score sum has more than nine SDs of evidence against the no-shift null. `L_n = 8.9210` on `vsc-redacted-b` is at the same order of magnitude. These are not borderline rejections.

The localizations are sharp. `kStar = 194` on `vsc-redacted-a` corresponds to `kStarDay = 2026-02-09`, with direction `+` and `meanShift = 2222` raw tokens/day. `kStar = 36` on `vsc-redacted-b` corresponds to `kStarDay = 2026-03-19`, with direction `-` and `meanShift = 91466871` raw tokens/day. The structural reading of the two `kStar` values is informative on its own. `vsc-redacted-a` localizes deep into the gap-filled series at `k = 194`, suggesting the smooth ramp-up is concentrated in the *late* portion of the observation window. `vsc-redacted-b` localizes early at `k = 36`, suggesting the descending transition is concentrated in the *early* portion of the window.

The `meanShift` magnitudes are not directly comparable across sources because the carriers operate at vastly different baseline volumes — `vsc-redacted-a` at a steady ~2222 tokens/day baseline, `vsc-redacted-b` at the ~10⁸ tokens/day scale where a step of `91466871` tokens/day is roughly an order-of-magnitude ratio rather than an SD-bearing scalar. The standardized `L_n` is the right cross-source comparison surface. On that scale the two sources are decisive at comparable strength (`9.21` vs `8.92`).

The directions are opposite. `vsc-redacted-a` ramps up; `vsc-redacted-b` ramps down. That is itself a structurally significant cross-source observation: in the same observation window two carriers executed transitions in opposite directions, both with overwhelming statistical evidence. A single-axis trend detector like axis-220 Hamed-Rao corrected MK would flag both as decisive but cannot capture the smoothness of the transition; axis-221 SNHT would localize both to step-shift candidate indices but, as we'll see, those candidate indices disagree with axis-222 by enough to fall outside the proximity guard.

## Why axis-222 vs axis-221 is the smooth-vs-abrupt dual on the same null

Both axis-221 SNHT and axis-222 Lombard test the same null hypothesis — *no structural break in distribution location* — and both surface a *single most-likely changepoint position*. They differ on the *transition shape* assumed under the alternative. SNHT's alternative is `x[i] = mu_1 * 1{i < t*} + mu_2 * 1{i >= t*} + epsilon[i]`: a hard step at index `t*`. Lombard's alternative is `x[i] = mu_1 * (1 - phi((i - t*)/h)) + mu_2 * phi((i - t*)/h) + epsilon[i]`: a smooth transition centered at `t*` with bandwidth `h`, where `phi` is a cumulative kernel function. As `h -> 0`, Lombard's alternative collapses to SNHT's; as `h -> n`, Lombard's alternative reduces to a global linear trend (which is what axis-220 Hamed-Rao tests as a special case of a monotonic ranking). The triangular Wilcoxon-score kernel is the asymptotic average across plausible smooth transitions of moderate bandwidth, which is why Lombard 1987 is the canonical *smooth-changepoint* construction even though no explicit `h` is fit.

This makes axis-222 vs axis-221 the *transition-shape dual*, distinct from the parametric-vs-rank dual that axis-221 vs axis-154 Pettitt closes. The full single-changepoint compound classifier on `pew-insights` is now a 3-axis structure:

1. **axis-221 SNHT**: parametric, abrupt-step, magnitude-bearing (squared deviations)
2. **axis-154 Pettitt**: rank, abrupt-step, sign-only (Mann-Whitney split)
3. **axis-222 Lombard**: rank, smooth-step, magnitude-bearing-on-ranks (triangular Wilcoxon score sum)

The three detectors triangulate the changepoint surface along two independent dimensions — `(parametric, rank)` × `(abrupt, smooth)` — with axis-221 occupying `(parametric, abrupt)`, axis-154 occupying `(rank, abrupt)`, and axis-222 occupying `(rank, smooth)`. The fourth quadrant `(parametric, smooth)` is structurally the next axis to ship if the suite is to close the 2×2 cross-product, though the canonical parametric-smooth construction (Csörgő-Horváth weighted CUSUM with truncated Wiener limits) has substantially more bandwidth-selection complexity than Lombard's parameter-free Wilcoxon construction.

## The axis-222 × axis-221 compound classifier and the agree-misaligned bucket

The v0.6.556 release ships a 5-bucket compound classifier on the smooth-vs-abrupt pair, structurally identical to the v0.6.552 axis-221 × axis-154 4-quadrant compound but with the alignment semantics reinterpreted. The buckets are `agree-aligned`, `agree-misaligned`, `lombard-only`, `snht-only`, and `no-evidence`.

The interesting case is `agree-misaligned`: both detectors decisive, but the localized changepoint indices disagree by more than `proximityGuard` days. For a true smooth transition spread over a window `[a, b]`, the SNHT step-shift detector will tend to localize to one of the boundary indices (typically the one with the larger marginal contribution to the squared-deviation sum), because the SNHT `T(a)` curve for a smooth ramp is not unimodal at the center but bimodal at the boundaries. The Lombard smoothed-score statistic localizes to the center of the ramp at `(a+b)/2`. The displacement between SNHT's `aStar` and Lombard's `kStar` is therefore approximately `(b-a)/2`, the half-bandwidth of the smooth transition. Whenever that half-bandwidth exceeds the proximity guard (default 5 days), the compound classifier flags `agree-misaligned`.

That is exactly what both `vsc-redacted-a` and `vsc-redacted-b` show on the v0.6.556 live-smoke. Axis-221 SNHT on `vsc-redacted-a` localizes its `aStar` to a nearby but distinct index from `kStar = 194`. The displacement is consistent with a smooth ramp of bandwidth substantially greater than 10 days. The diagnostic interpretation is: the `vsc-redacted-a` daily-token series did not execute a sharp regime change at one date — it ramped up smoothly over a multi-week window centered on 2026-02-09, and any single-step detector applied to it will produce localization noise on the order of the ramp half-width.

The same reading applies to `vsc-redacted-b`: a smooth descending transition centered on 2026-03-19, with bandwidth wide enough that SNHT localizes outside the proximity guard around `kStar = 36`. The `agree-misaligned` flag is in this case *not* a sign of two distinct changepoints in the series (the structural reading the same bucket has in the axis-221 × axis-154 compound), but rather a sign that the single underlying transition is *smooth*, and the abrupt-step model misfits its localization to a boundary of the transition window.

## Why the same bucket name has different semantics across compounds

This is a subtle point worth flagging. In the axis-221 × axis-154 compound (parametric-vs-rank dual on abrupt step shifts), `agree-misaligned` means the series probably has more than one structural break — SNHT picks up the larger magnitude-shift, Pettitt picks up the larger median-shift, and they disagree on which is the dominant break. The diagnostic next step is a multi-changepoint axis (binary segmentation on either statistic).

In the axis-222 × axis-221 compound (smooth-vs-abrupt dual), `agree-misaligned` means the underlying transition is smooth and the abrupt detector is mislocalizing to a boundary of the smooth ramp. The diagnostic next step is *not* a multi-changepoint axis — it is to take Lombard's localization as ground truth and ignore the SNHT mislocalization, because the abrupt-step model is mis-specified for the alternative.

The compound classifier returns the bucket name, but the semantics depend on which compound is firing. Downstream consumers therefore need the compound identifier as well as the bucket name to interpret the result correctly. The v0.6.556 surface returns both: `compound = "axis-222-x-axis-221"` plus `bucket = "agree-misaligned"`, and the consumer can dispatch on the compound name to apply the right structural reading. This is the same pattern that axes 184–186 (Savage / Klotz / Capon scale-shift triplet) used for the omnibus / point-estimator inferential-posture arc, but applied to the orthogonal transition-shape axis.

## Cross-reading axis-220 Hamed-Rao corrected MK with axis-222 Lombard

Axis-220 Hamed-Rao on `vsc-redacted-a` produces a decisive corrected `Z`, which says: there is a monotonic component in this series, significant after correcting for autocorrelation. Axis-222 Lombard on the same series produces `L_n = 9.2110` with `kStarDay = 2026-02-09` and direction `+`. These results are complementary in the same way axis-220 + axis-221 are complementary, but with a sharper structural reading available because Lombard explicitly assumes a *smooth* transition.

The Hamed-Rao monotonic-component signal could in principle be produced by any of three mechanisms: uniform drift, abrupt step, or smooth transition. Axis-221 alone disambiguates uniform drift from step. Axis-222 alone disambiguates uniform drift from smooth transition. The three-axis combination axis-220 + axis-221 + axis-222 disambiguates all three: uniform drift fires axis-220 only; abrupt step fires axes 220 and 221 (and 222, because a step is a degenerate smooth transition with zero bandwidth, but the bandwidth penalty in Lombard's score-summing kernel reduces the magnitude vs SNHT's step kernel); smooth transition fires axes 220 and 222 with axis-221 mislocalizing to a boundary, producing the `agree-misaligned` flag in the axis-222 × axis-221 compound.

The `vsc-redacted-a` empirical state — axis-220 decisive, axis-222 `L_n = 9.2110` with sharp `kStarDay = 2026-02-09` localization, axis-221 SNHT decisive but mislocalizing relative to `kStar` — is the *smooth transition* fingerprint in this 3-axis triangulation. The most parsimonious interpretation is that `vsc-redacted-a` daily-token consumption ramped up smoothly over a multi-week window centered on 2026-02-09, with the ramp bandwidth large enough to mislocalize an abrupt-step detector by more than the proximity guard.
