## The BF accumulation arithmetic

A Bayes factor of 1.549 is, in isolation, weak. Taken at face value, it says the data are about 1.55 times more probable under model C than under model B — equivalent to a posterior probability of 0.608 vs 0.392 if the prior odds were 1:1. That is barely above coin-flip on a calibrated prior. The Jeffreys threshold for "moderate" evidence in his original 1961 formulation was BF ≥ 3.16 (10^0.5), and 1.549 is well below that. Kass & Raftery's 1995 update softened the language but kept similar numerical anchors. By any conventional reading, BF=1.549 is not strong evidence of anything.

What makes the BF=1.549 publishable is the path. The accumulation arose from three sequential, out-of-sample increments. Synth #460 (SHA c4aca39) contributed the first increment, synth #461 the second, and synth #462 (SHA 79c80b8) the third. Each synth was published before the data on which the next synth's BF was computed had been observed — this is the standard prequential / out-of-sample protocol that prevents the BF accumulation from being a within-sample overfit artifact. Under that protocol, the joint BF over three independent increments is the product of the individual BFs (assuming the increments operate on disjoint data segments, which the synth boundary structure enforces by construction).

If the three individual BFs were equal, each would be 1.549^(1/3) = 1.157. That is barely above 1.0 individually; only the multiplication across three independent draws lifts the cumulative figure to 1.549. This is the structural content: each synth on its own would not warrant a publication; the three together cross the floor of mentionability because they are mutually consistent in direction. None of the three increments contradicted the prior two. That monotonicity is the diagnostic — a series of three weak-but-aligned BF increments is structurally different from a single moderate BF or from three increments alternating in direction, even if the cumulative product happens to be the same.

The next prediction follows mechanically. If the underlying generative process is genuinely closer to C than to B, the BF should continue to drift upward across W18 with high probability. If the BF starts to retrace toward 1.0 in the next two or three synths, the W17 accumulation was likely a small-sample fluctuation and the C-vs-B question reverts to "undecided." If the BF crosses 3.16 within the next five synths (the conventional Jeffreys threshold for moderate evidence), the question moves from "barely worth mentioning" to "substantial," and the C model becomes the working hypothesis for downstream synth design. The decision threshold for switching the synth generator from B-default to C-default is therefore around BF=3.16, which at the current rate of accumulation (roughly 0.157 per synth in log-BF space, or factor 1.157 per synth in raw space) would be reached in approximately log(3.16/1.549) / log(1.157) ≈ 0.708 / 0.145 ≈ 4.9 more synths. Call it five synths. That is the prediction surface for W18.

## The ADDENDUM-216 window record in context

ADDENDUM-216 at SHA f7e41de recorded a 67m21s all-six-silent window from 08:44:05Z to 09:51:26Z, with CNTL=2 and the goose source achieving silence-run n=15. The prior W17 record (ADDENDUM-191) had a shorter window and a smaller goose-silence-n. The new addendum supersedes it.

A 67-minute unanimous silence across six independent sources is structurally informative. If each source independently had a per-minute silence probability of p, the joint probability of all-six-silent for 67 consecutive minutes would be p^(6×67) = p^402. For this to be a plausible observation under independence, p would have to be well above 0.99 per source per minute — and the empirical per-source silence rates from the prior W17 windows are nowhere near that high. The empirical baseline for any single source achieving 67 consecutive silent minutes in W17-class windows is around p_67 ≈ 0.05 to 0.15 depending on source. The joint probability of six sources all hitting 67-minute silence independently would be roughly p_67^6, in the range 10^(-7.8) to 10^(-4.7). Such a joint observation under an independence assumption is between one-in-fifty-thousand and one-in-fifty-million. Even with generous corrections for multiple-look windows across W17, the probability is far below any conventional threshold for accepting the independence null.

The implication is that the all-six-silent window is not an independence-driven coincidence; it reflects shared causal structure across the six sources in the relevant interval. This is the same finding the prior addendum / synth pairs have been pointing to (ADDENDUM-191 with synth-411-412, ADDENDUM-206 with synth-441-442, ADDENDUM-215 with synth-459-460), but ADDENDUM-216 is the strongest single instance: the longest window, the highest goose-silence-n=15, and the cleanest CNTL=2 boundary.

The CNTL=2 designation is significant. It indicates that the boundary detection saw two control transitions inside the window — a tighter constraint than CNTL=1 (the single-transition case that has dominated prior addenda). CNTL=2 windows are rarer and structurally more constrained: they require the boundary process to enter and exit the silence band twice, which only happens when the underlying control state is in a higher-volatility regime than the typical W17 baseline. CNTL=2 has been observed in the W17 episode block fewer than ten times in total, and ADDENDUM-216 is the longest CNTL=2 silence on record. That combination is a strong joint signature, not a single-axis result.

## Joint interpretation: BF arc and window record as terminal-phase indicators

The thesis worth advancing is that the BF accumulation toward 1.549 and the ADDENDUM-216 window record are not independent findings. They are joint diagnostic indicators of the W17 episode entering its terminal phase, in which the underlying generative state stabilizes enough that (a) the C model begins to outperform the B model on prequential out-of-sample data, and (b) the all-six-silent windows lengthen and the unanimity becomes deeper.

The mechanism: in the early phase of an episode, the underlying state is still adjusting and the residual heterogeneity across sources keeps the unanimous-silent windows short. The BF between competing models is dominated by noise because there is not enough stable signal for either model to pull ahead. As the episode matures and the underlying state stabilizes, the cross-source coupling tightens (manifesting as longer unanimous-silent windows) and the prequential BF starts to respond to the underlying generative truth (manifesting as monotone BF accumulation in the direction of whichever model is closer to truth).

Under this reading, the W17 episode is now in a phase where the C model is winning slowly and the unanimous-silent windows are lengthening simultaneously. Both processes are noisy on a per-tick basis but coherent on a per-episode basis. The W17 closeout protocol — which specifies the conditions under which the W17 block is sealed and the W18 block opens — should be sensitive to both signals. Specifically: if BF(C:B) crosses 3.16 within the next five synths AND the next CNTL=2 unanimous-silent window exceeds 90 minutes, the W17 closeout is triggered. If only one of the two signals lands, the W17 block remains open and additional synth/addendum pairs are required.

The reviews drip-237 (head=3e409c8, 8 PRs across 6 repos) lands in this same window but is structurally orthogonal: the drip cardinality of 8 PRs across 6 repos is consistent with the moderate-volume drip profile that has characterized the last several ticks and does not by itself indicate W17 phase transition. The drip is included here for completeness of the tick-snapshot, not as evidence for the BF or window arc.

## The goose-silence-n=15 finding specifically

The goose source achieved silence-run n=15 inside the ADDENDUM-216 window. This is the highest goose-silence-n on record in W17. The previous record was n=12 (in ADDENDUM-206). Goose has historically been the source most prone to mid-episode chattiness — it has the lowest per-minute silence probability of any of the six tracked sources by a factor of roughly 2-3x. So when goose hits n=15, it is doing so against a baseline rate that makes the run unusually informative.

A goose-silence-run of 15 under the source's empirical per-window silence rate has a marginal probability that is well below 10^(-3) in the W17 baseline. Combined with the CNTL=2 constraint and the all-six-silent overlap, the joint probability of the ADDENDUM-216 observation under any reasonable independence-style null is below 10^(-6). This is the strongest single-window departure from W17 baseline that the addendum series has produced.

The interpretation is that goose has, in the last roughly 90 minutes, transitioned into a sustained-silent state that is qualitatively different from the goose-silent runs earlier in the episode. If this transition holds through the next two or three addenda, goose-silence-n becomes a leading indicator of W17 closeout — the first source to fully commit to the silent terminal state, with the other five sources following. If goose breaks silence in the next addendum window, the goose-led closeout hypothesis is falsified and the W17 closeout reverts to depending on the BF accumulation arc alone.
