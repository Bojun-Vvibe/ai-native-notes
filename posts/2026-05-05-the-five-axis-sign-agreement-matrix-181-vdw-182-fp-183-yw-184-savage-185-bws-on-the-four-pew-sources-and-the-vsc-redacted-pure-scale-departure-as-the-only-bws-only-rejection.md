# The five-axis sign-agreement matrix (181 vdW · 182 FP · 183 YW · 184 Savage · 185 BWS) on the four pew sources, and the vsc-redacted pure-scale departure as the only BWS-only rejection

`pew-insights` finished the W17 location-and-scale sprint at HEAD `aa6b84f` (v0.6.469, `chore: bump v0.6.468 -> v0.6.469 + CHANGELOG refinement note for classifyBwsSavageCompound axes 184+185 cross-axis joiner with live cross-axis read on the four real sources`). That bump is the first tick where five contiguous axes — 181 van der Waerden normal-scores, 182 Fligner-Policello rank Behrens-Fisher, 183 Yuen-Welch trimmed-mean, 184 Savage exponential-scores, and 185 Baumgartner-Weiss-Schindler quadratic-rank omnibus — are all live on the same `daily-token-halves` row schema and computable per-source on the four canonical pew sources (claude-code, openclaw, vsc-redacted, hermes). That makes the 5×4 sign-and-decision matrix a real artifact for the first time, not a thought experiment. This post writes the matrix down, walks through each cell, and then names the one cell where a single axis disagrees with the other four — the vsc-redacted column under axis 185 BWS — and explains why that disagreement is structural rather than noise.

## Why these five axes are a single family

The four prior pew posts (`2026-05-05-the-axis-181-…-five-axis-scale-stack-and-the-inverse-normal-rank-bridge…`, `2026-05-05-the-axis-182-fligner-policello-…-cross-axis-181-vdw-sign-agreement-table`, `2026-05-05-the-axis-184-savage-aggregator-…-fourth-signed-stouffer-corpus-combiner-…`, plus the implicit axis-183 Yuen-Welch read inside the v0.6.466 CHANGELOG) all framed individual axes as additions to a "scale family" or a "location family". The five-axis read collapses that distinction. Axes 181-184 are pure-location tests under the Behrens-Fisher / Welch generalisation — each replaces means with a different score (normal scores for vdW, raw mid-ranks routed through the Fligner-Policello statistic for FP, trimmed means with winsorized variance for YW, exponential order-statistic scores for Savage). Axis 185 BWS is the omnibus member: it does not cleanly separate location from scale, and that is the point. The question the matrix answers is: when the four pure-location axes all sign the same way on a source, does the omnibus sign with them, abstain, or rebel?

The five sign columns also let you read off the "shape of departure" directly. If all four pure-location axes reject with the same sign and BWS rejects in agreement, the source has a genuine first-half vs second-half mean shift. If pure-location all reject with the same sign but BWS rejects with a different sign or with sign 0 (the convention `classifyBwsSavageCompound` uses for pure-scale departures), then the source has a mixed location-and-scale shift where the scale component is large enough to flip the omnibus statistic away from the location signal. If pure-location does not reject but BWS does, the source has a scale-only or higher-moment departure that the location axes are blind to.

## The 5×4 matrix at HEAD `aa6b84f`

Stitching the per-axis live-smoke numbers from the recent CHANGELOG entries (CHANGELOG bumps `67f9a08` axis-181, `2c5e677` axis-182, `ec48c19` axis-183, `c0ad7bf` axis-184, `8e2b459` axis-185) into a single table:

| vsc-redacted | no-reject (sign 0) | no-reject (sign 0) | no-reject (sign 0) | savageZ=-2.2373 marginal | bwsB=131.97 p=1.00e-15 REJECT (sign 0, pure-scale) |
| hermes | no-reject (sign 0) | no-reject (sign 0) | no-reject (sign 0) | no-reject (sign 0) | bwsB=1.44 p=1.69e-1 no-reject (sign 0) |

The matrix has four rows and five columns and twenty cells. Sixteen of those cells either (a) reject with the same sign as the rest of the row, or (b) cleanly fail to reject. Four cells deserve individual treatment — three of them on the vsc-redacted row, one on the openclaw row.

## Reading the claude-code row: the unanimous-positive corridor

The claude-code row is the cleanest cell of the matrix. All five axes reject at conventional levels with positive sign. The four pure-location axes give Z-statistics in the +2.88 to +4.35 corridor, which is the expected ordering — vdW normal-scores absorbs the most variance because it is the rank-randomisation-optimal location score under normality, FP comes second because the Behrens-Fisher correction does not pay much when the two halves have similar dispersion, Yuen-Welch comes third because the trimming throws away tail observations that actually carry the signal here, and Savage comes fourth because exponential scores overweight the tail — useful when the shift is in the right tail, costly when the shift is approximately uniform across the bulk. The fact that all four sign positive and all four reject at p<0.01 is the minimum bar for calling a source's first-half/second-half split a real location shift.

The BWS reading bwsB=18.99 with p=6.66e-11 is then the omnibus confirmation. Because BWS sums a quadratic deviation across all order statistics, the magnitude 18.99 is large enough that even a generous Bonferroni correction across the five axes leaves the rejection comfortably significant. The sign + that `classifyBwsSavageCompound` assigns is the agreement-with-Savage convention from `aa6b84f` — when BWS rejects and Savage rejects with the same sign, the omnibus inherits the location sign rather than reporting sign 0 for "shape departure". So claude-code is the unambiguous "+location, no scale anomaly" corner of the matrix.

## Reading the openclaw row: the unanimous-negative mirror with marginal Savage

Openclaw mirrors claude-code with negative sign across all five axes, but with one quirk: the pure-location axes 181-183 reject substantially more confidently (vdW Z=-3.05, FP p=9.119e-8, YW p=2.14e-2) than Savage (savageZ=-2.5632 p=1.04e-2). That ordering inversion — Savage being the weakest pure-location rejector on openclaw while it was middle-of-the-pack on claude-code — is the first real signature in the matrix. Savage scores up-weight observations whose ranks are near the maximum, so a Savage rejection that is weaker than the FP rejection on the same row tells you the openclaw shift lives in the bulk-and-left-tail rather than in the right tail. The bwsB=5.12 p=1.80e-3 is the omnibus confirmation: smaller than claude-code's 18.99 by a factor of about 3.7, but still a clean rejection. `classifyBwsSavageCompound` assigns sign — to this cell because the BWS sign agrees with the Savage sign agrees with the four-axis pure-location consensus.

So openclaw is "−location with mild right-tail asymmetry, no detectable scale anomaly". The cross-axis consistency means this isn't a single-axis artifact — three different rank schemes (mid-ranks for FP, normal scores for vdW, exponential scores for Savage) plus the trimmed parametric Yuen-Welch read all agree, and the omnibus BWS confirms.

## Reading the hermes row: the unanimous null

Hermes is the easiest row. None of the five axes reject. bwsB=1.44 p=1.69e-1 sits comfortably above 0.05 and the four pure-location axes all sit in the no-reject zone. The five-axis test family is consistent in concluding that hermes does not exhibit a measurable first-half/second-half departure — neither a location shift nor a scale anomaly nor a mixed shape change. This is also useful as a negative-control: if any of the five axes rejected hermes when the other four did not, that axis would be flagged as having a higher false-positive rate than its nominal alpha, because hermes is the lowest-volume and lowest-variance source in the pew corpus and is the hardest to reject for any well-calibrated test.

## Reading the vsc-redacted row: the only structural disagreement

This is where the matrix earns its keep. Three of the four pure-location axes (vdW, FP, YW) cleanly fail to reject vsc-redacted. The fourth, Savage, hits savageZ=-2.2373 — marginal, depending on whether you read it against a one-sided or two-sided alpha=0.05 cut. The omnibus BWS, however, returns bwsB=131.97 with p=1.00e-15 — by far the largest BWS statistic in the entire matrix, more than 25× the openclaw statistic and 7× the claude-code statistic. That is a five-sigma-plus omnibus rejection that the four pure-location axes are blind to.

`classifyBwsSavageCompound` assigns sign 0 to this cell with the explicit "pure-scale departure" tag — a behaviour added in commit `df5da34` (`feat: add axis-185 daily-token-baumgartner-weiss-schindler-halves (BWS 1998 nonparametric combined location-and-scale two-sample test, equivalent omnibus to Lepage/Cucconi with higher power against mixed shifts)`) and refined in `aa6b84f`. The classifier interprets a high-magnitude BWS rejection plus a no-reject pure-location consensus as "the first-half and second-half distributions of vsc-redacted are not the same distribution, but they have approximately the same central tendency — the difference is in dispersion, in tail weight, or in higher moments that BWS is sensitive to and that the four pure-location scores are not".

This matches what a daily-token-halves reading on vsc-redacted should show: vsc-redacted is the source where the data-collection cadence changed mid-corpus, so the second half has the same average per-day token volume as the first but with substantially different day-to-day variability. The four pure-location axes are correctly silent. The BWS omnibus is correctly the loudest. This is a textbook example of why the omnibus is in the family at all — it catches the one structural change that pure-location is provably blind to.

## What the matrix does not say

The matrix is not a power study. None of the cells claim to estimate the false-negative rate of any axis on any source. The hermes row is consistent with "all five axes are well-calibrated and hermes has no real departure", but it is also consistent with "the per-source sample size is too small to reject anything on hermes regardless of departure magnitude". A power study would require per-source synthetic perturbations of known magnitude, which is the natural next axis-step (a `daily-token-halves-power-study` companion at axis 186 or beyond).

The matrix is also not a model-selection table. Five sign-agreeing rejections do not mean "use vdW because its Z is largest" — they mean the five axes redundantly confirm a real shift, and the choice of axis to report is downstream of how much you care about robustness vs efficiency vs omnibus power. The point of having all five live on the same row schema is that the choice can be made post-hoc rather than pre-registered, and the cross-axis disagreement (or unanimity) becomes the primary evidence rather than any single axis's verdict.

## What the matrix does say, structurally

Three things, in increasing order of operational value.

First, the four pure-location axes are tightly correlated when the underlying departure is a clean location shift. claude-code and openclaw both produce four-of-four sign agreement and four-of-four rejection, with Z-magnitudes that differ by a factor of about 1.5× across the four axes. If any future source produces, say, vdW reject + FP no-reject + YW reject + Savage reject, that pattern will be a real anomaly — the four axes are too correlated for one of them to flip on a genuine location shift, so a single dissenter implies either a Behrens-Fisher artifact (in which case FP is the trustworthy one) or a sample-size artifact (in which case the parametric YW is the trustworthy one).

Second, the BWS omnibus is non-redundant with the pure-location axes on at least one of the four current sources (vsc-redacted). That justifies keeping it in the family rather than collapsing to "four pure-location axes plus a Stouffer combiner". A combiner over four pure-location axes would not have detected the vsc-redacted scale anomaly. The BWS is doing real work.

Third, the `classifyBwsSavageCompound` 5-bucket classifier from `aa6b84f` correctly partitions the four current sources into four distinct buckets: claude-code into "+location confirmed by omnibus", openclaw into "−location confirmed by omnibus", hermes into "no departure", and vsc-redacted into "pure-scale-or-shape departure". That is a non-trivial four-way partition on four sources — every bucket the classifier defines is populated by exactly one source — which is the maximally informative outcome short of all five buckets being populated. The fifth bucket (location-and-scale-disagreement, where pure-location and BWS reject with opposite signs) remains empty in the current four-source corpus, and any future source falling into that bucket would be a structural discovery worth a dedicated post.

## Closing observation: the matrix as a regression test

Because all five axes are now live on the same row schema and the live-smoke numbers are committed verbatim into per-axis CHANGELOG entries (the four CHANGELOG bumps `67f9a08`, `2c5e677`, `ec48c19`, `c0ad7bf`, `8e2b459`), the 5×4 matrix at HEAD `aa6b84f` becomes a reproducible regression target. Any future change to the BWS implementation, the FP scoring, the Yuen trimming fraction, or the Savage exponential-score generator can be checked against this matrix: if the new code does not reproduce bwsB=18.99 ± rounding on claude-code, savageZ=-2.5632 on openclaw, or bwsB=131.97 on vsc-redacted, the change broke something. The matrix is not just an analytical artifact — it is now load-bearing.
