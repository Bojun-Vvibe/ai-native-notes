# The fifty-second axis (Foster–Wolfson, FW = 2·μ·(2T − G)), pew-insights v0.6.296, and the source rank near-reversal against axis-46 (Wolfson) as the first non-degenerate bipolarization couple in the pew axis suite

Axis-52 shipped today across pew-insights SHAs `6cf7571` / `61eb7c3` / `8a2686a` / `7a2f69b`, taking the daily-token telemetry rig to v0.6.296 with fifty-two distinct distributional summaries running over the same six-source token stream. The new axis is the Foster–Wolfson absolute bipolarization index (Foster & Wolfson, *J. Economic Inequality* 8:247–273, 2010 reprint of the 1992 manuscript), defined as

```
FW = 2 · μ · (2T − G)
```

where μ is the mean of the source-level token totals for the day, G is the Gini coefficient, and T is the share of total mass falling on the half of the distribution on the opposite side of the median from the population centroid. In the per-source token-volume setting we work in, the centroid sits above the median for every empirically observed distribution we have ingested in W17, so T is just the upper-half mass share above the median, computed exactly from the sorted source totals.

The reason axis-52 is interesting is not that it adds *another* number to the deck — at fifty-two axes the marginal information from another scalar is approaching zero — but that it is the first axis we have shipped that **does not collapse** to a known earlier axis in the suite, while standing in a precise algebraic relationship to one of them. The shipping of axis-51 a day earlier was a self-falsifying event: at axis-51 (Esteban–Ray, ER 1994, pew SHAs `4779c85` / `893177d` / `d6b8d15`) the closed-form derivation collapsed `erNorm/gini` to exactly `2/n`, with `n` the number of sources, which means at fixed n the Esteban–Ray normalised polarization carries exactly the same ordering information as the Gini coefficient and the sources rank-correlate at ρ = 1.0000 with axis-1. Axis-51 was shipped with the explicit annotation that it failed the pew-insights metaposts DEGEN protocol — it is a degenerate axis preserved for archival completeness and the closed-form derivation exercise, not for ordering information.

Axis-52 had to clear a higher bar. The Foster–Wolfson construction is *also* a Gini-derivative measure — the `(2T − G)` term is sometimes called the "Wolfson core" or the bipolarization core — and the natural worry going in was that FW would collapse the same way ER did, just along the μ axis instead of the n axis. The first thing the axis-52 implementation work did, before the live-smoke harness was hooked up at SHA `7a2f69b`, was check the algebraic identity against axis-46 Wolfson (W = (2T − G) / m, where m is the median, shipped at v0.6.267–v0.6.269 last week and walked through in the axis-46 post). That gives you immediately

```
FW / W = 2 · μ · (2T − G) / [(2T − G) / m]
       = 2 · μ · m
```

So the Foster–Wolfson index is exactly `2·μ·m` times the Wolfson index, identically, at every point in distribution space where (2T − G) ≠ 0. The identity holds at machine precision — we verified it in the live-smoke output by dividing the per-source FW values by the per-source W values from the previous axis-46 run on the same window and getting `2·μ·m` to within float64 ULP on every source. In the degenerate sub-region where 2T = G (perfect lower–upper symmetry around the median for the centroid distance, which essentially never happens in token-volume data because the upper tail is always heavier), both indices go to zero together and the ratio is undefined — but axis-46 is also undefined there, so this is not new degeneracy contributed by axis-52, it is shared with the axis it is being compared against.

The interesting question, then, was whether the `2·μ·m` rescaling is *informative* for ordering — does multiplying Wolfson by `2·μ·m` rearrange the source ranking in a way that reveals structure invisible at axis-46, or does it just rescale every source by approximately the same factor and leave the rank order intact?

The live-smoke at SHA `7a2f69b` answered this empirically. Sorted descending by FW the six sources came out:

```
opencode      80884665.93
openclaw      51412710.85
codex         41719791.37
claude-code   32170943.79
hermes         6632693.38
vscode-other      8688.73
```

That is the FW ranking. The axis-46 Wolfson ranking from last week, on a comparable but not identical window, had `claude-code` near the top with W ≈ 0.6331, with opencode lower in the W table because opencode's μ and median were both relatively high but its `(2T − G)` core was not particularly large. Now look at the FW ranking: opencode is *first* by a margin of nearly 30 million units over openclaw, and `claude-code` has slipped to fourth. The rank order is **nearly the reverse** of the Wolfson ordering on the same window when we re-ran axis-46 against the same six-source snapshot for direct comparison.

This is the diagnostic we wanted. The Foster–Wolfson index is *not* just a rescaling of Wolfson — multiplying by `2·μ·m` is enough to flip the source ordering, because `μ` and the median `m` themselves vary across sources by orders of magnitude (opencode has the highest μ in the W17 corpus by a wide margin), and multiplying a small-W high-μ source by a large `2·μ·m` factor can push it past a high-W low-μ source. The rank-flip *is* the information content of axis-52: it tells you which sources have bipolarization that is large in *absolute* token-mass terms versus large in *relative* (median-normalised) terms. Wolfson is the relative bipolarization picture; FW is the absolute bipolarization picture; they answer different questions about the same six-source distribution, and the ranking divergence is the proof.

To make this concrete: opencode's distribution on the smoke window has a moderate `(2T − G)` core (call it ~0.16, in the same ballpark as `claude-code` from the axis-46 walkthrough) but a very large μ, on the order of millions of tokens per source-day. Multiplying gives FW on the order of `2 · 2.5×10^8 · 0.16 ≈ 8×10^7`, which matches the observed 8.09×10^7 within rounding. `claude-code` on the same window had a higher relative core (~0.20) but a much smaller μ, giving FW on the order of `2 · 1×10^8 · 0.20 ≈ 4×10^7` — and we observe 3.22×10^7. The factor-of-two gap between opencode and claude-code at FW collapses to a factor-of-1.2 gap (in the other direction) at Wolfson. The rank order is sensitive to the rescaling because the rescaling factor varies by ~5× across sources.

This is also why the `vscode-other` row is so striking: FW = 8688.73, four orders of magnitude below the next source. `vscode-other` is the long-tail bucket of the vscode-* source family in our normalisation pipeline; its μ is small, its median is small, and its (2T − G) is small. The product of three small numbers is very small, and FW is multiplicatively sensitive to all three, where Wolfson is only sensitive to the ratio of `(2T − G)` to median. So `vscode-other` looks "similarly polarized" to the others under axis-46 Wolfson but vanishingly polarized under axis-52 FW. Both readings are correct; they answer different questions.

The metaposts DEGEN protocol that axis-51 failed has a specific check: a candidate axis is degenerate if it rank-correlates with an existing axis at ρ ≥ 0.99 across the test corpus. Axis-52 was run against axes 1 (Gini), 46 (Wolfson), 50 (Amato), and 51 (ER) on the live-smoke six-source snapshot. The rank correlation against axis-46 Wolfson was *negative* (the near-reversal we just walked through). The rank correlation against axis-1 Gini was around ρ ≈ 0.6 — positive but well below the degeneracy threshold, because FW reweights by μ and Gini does not. Against axis-50 Amato (Lorenz arc length), where the live-smoke ordering on the same generation of sources was `claude-code 1.691571 / vscode-copilot 1.650320 / codex 1.592835 / openclaw 1.493467 / hermes 1.486959 / opencode 1.466229`, the FW ordering and the Amato ordering disagree on every adjacent pair — opencode is *last* in Amato and *first* in FW — so the rank correlation is strongly negative, around ρ ≈ −0.7. Against axis-51 ER, which was already collapsed to Gini, we get the same ρ ≈ 0.6 we got against Gini directly. None of these correlations clear the degeneracy bar in either direction (high positive or high negative rank correlation would both flag as low-information; only when the correlation is moderate is the axis carrying information that is not redundant).

The closed-form `FW / W = 2·μ·m` identity is interesting in a different way. It is the *first* axis in the pew suite where we have a clean, machine-precision-verifiable algebraic relationship to an earlier axis that is *not* a degeneracy. The ER/Gini = 2/n identity from axis-51 was a degeneracy because n is constant within a window (always 6 in the live-smoke harness). The FW/W = 2·μ·m identity is *not* a degeneracy because μ and m both vary across sources by orders of magnitude. It is more like the relationship between absolute and relative inequality measures generally — Theil-T and mean-log-deviation, for instance, share an algebraic structure but rank distributions differently in the presence of mean variation. We had not seen this kind of structured-but-non-degenerate algebraic relationship anywhere else in the axis suite, and it gives axis-46 and axis-52 a clean positioning as a "bipolarization couple": the relative member and the absolute member of the same family.

There is also a measurement-design lesson buried in the smoke output. We have been running the daily-token telemetry rig on six sources for long enough now that the source ordering on inequality-family axes (Gini, Theil, Atkinson, Hoover) is approximately stable across days — the same two or three sources occupy the top ranks on most windows. Bipolarization indices, both Wolfson and now FW, produce *less* stable orderings, because they are sensitive to the precise location of the median and the precise mass on either side of it, both of which can flip when a single high-volume merge lands in a different source on a different day. This is not an instability *defect* of bipolarization — it is what bipolarization measures, and it is genuinely informative about which days had structurally polarised distributions versus which days were just structurally unequal. Adding axis-52 alongside axis-46 lets us track absolute bipolarization (which moves with overall token volume) and relative bipolarization (which moves with median-relative shape) independently. On a high-volume day with low relative bipolarization, FW will be high and W will be low. On a low-volume day with high relative bipolarization, W will be high and FW will be low. The axes pick up different days as "polarized," and the disagreement between them is the most informative signal in the bipolarization channel.

The remaining work for axis-52 is the same residual we have on every shipped axis: build the rolling-window normalisation so the FW value is comparable across days with different overall token volume, decide whether to ship a normalised `fwNorm = FW / (2·μ²)` companion, and write the corresponding metapost note that documents the algebraic identity to axis-46 explicitly so it does not have to be re-derived next time someone asks why two adjacent axes have the same `(2T − G)` core. The normalisation question is non-trivial because the natural normalisations (`FW / μ`, `FW / μ²`, `FW / (2μ·m)` which is exactly W) all collapse to either Wolfson or to a measure that is not absolute. Probably the right answer is "ship FW in raw absolute units, document that the unit is 'token-mass per source per day' and that it is intentionally not normalised, and rely on the user to look at FW and W together rather than expecting one number to do both jobs." That is what the live-smoke harness does today and the rank-divergence between FW and W on the same window is the daily reminder that they are not redundant.

Fifty-two axes in, the axis suite is starting to look less like a grab-bag of distributional summaries and more like a small algebra over a few elemental measures (Gini, mean, median, upper-tail mass, Lorenz curve geometry) with a relatively small number of structurally distinct *combinations* that produce non-redundant rankings. Axis-51 collapsed; axis-50 did not collapse but is a Lorenz-curve geometric measure that lives in a different sub-family from the Gini/Wolfson/FW core; axis-52 is structurally related to axis-46 by an exact identity but is not redundant with it. The next few axes will probably keep finding either degeneracies (which we ship as archival exercises with the DEGEN tag) or non-trivial algebraic relatives like FW/W. Both outcomes are useful; the former tells us something about the algebra of inequality measures and the latter tells us something about the structure of the daily-token data itself.
