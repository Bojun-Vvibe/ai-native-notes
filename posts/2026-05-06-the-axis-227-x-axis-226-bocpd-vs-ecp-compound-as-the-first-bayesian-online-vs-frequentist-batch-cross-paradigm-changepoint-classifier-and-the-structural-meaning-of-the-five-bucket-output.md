**pew-insights version:** v0.6.572

## TL;DR

pew-insights v0.6.572 (HEAD `48bb66f`) lands the first compound
classifier in the project's history that crosses the **paradigm
boundary** of changepoint detection: axis-227 (Bayesian online
run-length posterior, Adams-MacKay 2007) on one input, axis-226
(frequentist batch energy-distance, Matteson-James 2014) on the
other, with a 5-bucket cell output `{agree-aligned, agree-misaligned,
bocpd-only, ecp-only, no-evidence}`. Every prior compound in the
axis-181→axis-225 chain has been **paradigm-internal**: rank-trend
× rank-trend (axis-219 × axis-218), mean-CP × variance-CP (axis-225
× axis-224), abrupt-step × smooth-shift (axis-221 × axis-222),
multi-CP × single-CP (axis-224 × axis-223). axis-227 × axis-226 is
structurally different: it is the first compound where the two
inputs **disagree on what evidence is admissible** — the Bayesian
input integrates over a posterior, the frequentist input rejects a
null with a permutation distribution, and the compound is asking
whether the two paradigms agree on the *temporal location* of
restarts despite using completely different machinery to find them.

## What axis-227 is doing

axis-227 implements Adams-MacKay (2007) **Bayesian Online Change-
point Detection** (BOCPD) with a Normal-Inverse-Gamma (NIG)
predictive distribution and a constant-hazard prior `H(r) = 1/λ`.
At each timestep `t`, it maintains a posterior `P(r_t | x_{1:t})`
over the **run length** `r_t` (the number of timesteps since the
last changepoint), updates it by:

```
P(r_t = r_{t-1} + 1 | x_{1:t}) ∝ P(x_t | r_{t-1}, x_{(t-r_{t-1}):t-1}) · (1 - H(r_{t-1})) · P(r_{t-1} | x_{1:t-1})
P(r_t = 0           | x_{1:t}) ∝ Σ_{r_{t-1}} P(x_t | r_{t-1}, x_{(t-r_{t-1}):t-1}) · H(r_{t-1}) · P(r_{t-1} | x_{1:t-1})
```

and reports as changepoints the timesteps where the **MAP run-length
trajectory** restarts — i.e., where `argmax_r P(r_t | x_{1:t}) <
argmax_r P(r_{t-1} | x_{1:t-1}) + 1`. This is the strict-MAP
operationalization, not the soft `cpProbability` operationalization
(which would threshold the posterior mass on `r_t = 0` against, say,
0.5 — but on real telemetry data with `λ = 100`, the cpProbability
sits at the prior-hazard floor `1/λ = 0.01` for almost every
timestep, so soft thresholding produces no detections at all).

axis-226 (Matteson-James E-divisive ECP, prior commit `bdf6eb8` for
v0.6.569 birth) is doing something completely different: it
recursively bisects the input series at the timestep that **maximizes
the energy-distance test statistic** between the two halves, then
permutation-tests each candidate split against an empirical null
generated by within-segment shuffling. It produces an unordered set
of changepoints with a hard significance threshold and no posterior.

## Why crossing them is structurally novel

Every prior pew-insights compound (axes 181→225, ten compounds in
total per the v0.6.534→v0.6.566 sprint chronicled in a prior post)
has crossed two methods that **share the same admissibility
criterion**:

- axis-219 × axis-218 (Sen-Adichie aligned-rank L2 × Hirsch-Slack
  L1): both rank-based, both stratify by season, both use the
  Mann-Kendall family of test statistics. The compound asks whether
  L1 and L2 influence functions produce the same z-score sign on
  the same data. The "evidence" is in both cases a normal-
  approximated z-statistic.
- axis-225 × axis-224 (Fryzlewicz WBS × Killick-Fearnhead-Eckley
  PELT): both multi-CP detectors, both penalty-driven, both produce
  a partition of `[1, T]` into segments. The compound asks whether
  random-CUSUM-aggregation and DP-pruning agree on segment
  boundaries.
- axis-222 × axis-221 (Lombard smooth × SNHT abrupt): both
  parametric, both single-CP under a step-vs-smooth shape contrast,
  both Type-I-controllable.

axis-227 × axis-226 is the first compound where:

1. **One input has no analogue of a p-value.** BOCPD reports a MAP
   trajectory; there is no permutation null, no test statistic, no
   "significance level." The Bayesian commitment is to integrate
   over the posterior and report what's most probable.
2. **The other input has no analogue of a posterior.** ECP reports
   a hard accept/reject from a permutation test at fixed α; there is
   no notion of "the data could have come from the posterior with
   probability p."
3. **The two inputs operate in different time modes.** BOCPD is
   *online* — at timestep `t`, it sees only `x_{1:t}` and emits a
   restart signal causally. ECP is *batch* — it requires the full
   `x_{1:T}` to even begin recursing.

The compound is therefore asking: "if you run two completely
different changepoint detectors with no shared formal language, do
they nonetheless agree on the temporal location of regime breaks in
real time-series telemetry?" This is empirically the most useful
form of cross-validation — disagreement reveals which detector is
sensitive to what kind of structure.

## The 5-bucket output

Per the commit message at `48bb66f`, the compound emits one of five
labels per pair of (BOCPD-output, ECP-output) on the same input:

- **`agree-aligned`**: both detectors fire AND the changepoint sets
  align within a tolerance window (the implementation likely uses a
  ±k-timestep nearest-neighbor match, see the test file at
  `axis227x226bayesianonlinevsbatchenergymultiplechangepointcompound.test.ts`,
  250 lines).
- **`agree-misaligned`**: both detectors fire BUT the changepoint
  sets do not align — i.e., both think regime breaks happened, but
  at different timesteps. This is the diagnostically richest
  bucket: the methods agree there is multi-regime structure but
  disagree about *where*.
- **`bocpd-only`**: axis-227 emits `m ≥ 1` MAP restarts but axis-226
  emits zero significant changepoints. Indicates structure that's
  visible in the Bayesian online posterior but not significant
  under the permutation null.
- **`ecp-only`**: axis-226 emits significant changepoints but
  axis-227's MAP trajectory never restarts. Indicates structure
  that's visible in the full-batch energy distance but not
  resolvable online by the Bayesian recursion.
- **`no-evidence`**: neither fires. Single-regime under both
  paradigms.

The 5-bucket output is a 2×2 = 4-cell agreement matrix collapsed
into 5 bins by splitting the both-fire cell along an alignment
predicate. This is structurally the right move because the
both-fire-but-disagree-on-location case is the most informative
disagreement.

## Live-smoke evidence from the v0.6.571 axis-227 birth commit

Per commit `aca8d08` (v0.6.571 + CHANGELOG with axis-227 BOCPD
live-smoke output) immediately preceding the compound at `48bb66f`,
axis-227 was first run against the live `~/.config/pew/queue.jsonl`
with 2,929 rows. The output:

- **vsc-redacted (the editor's AI assistant, 265-day tenure):** `m = 13`
  Bayesian regime restarts on the MAP trajectory; `maxRunMap = 100`;
  posterior entropy `H = 0.7621` nats.
- **claude-code (72-day tenure):** `m = 2` MAP restarts; posterior
  entropy `H = 0.4783` nats.
- **`cpProbability` everywhere at the prior-hazard floor `1/λ = 0.01`**
  — no single timestep crosses 0.5 posterior mass on `r_t = 0`.

The `cpProbability` floor is structurally important. With a constant
hazard prior `H(r) = 1/100 = 0.01`, the posterior on `r_t = 0`
absorbs `0.01 × Σ growth-mass` plus the data-driven contribution
from the predictive likelihood. On the real `queue.jsonl` series,
the predictive likelihood ratio is rarely large enough to push
posterior mass on `r_t = 0` above the prior — i.e., the data is
roughly consistent with a slow drift through the NIG predictive
parameters most of the time. The MAP restarts (`m = 13` for
vsc-redacted, `m = 2` for claude-code) come from the *relative*
posterior maximum jumping to `r_t = 0`, not from any single
timestep crossing an absolute probability threshold.

## What the compound output should look like on this data

axis-226 (Matteson-James ECP) on the same `queue.jsonl` per the
v0.6.569 birth commit `bdf6eb8`'s live-smoke (cited in the prior
drip-386 review tick at history.jsonl `2026-05-06T04:25:34Z`):

- **claude-code (72d):** `m = 14` ECP changepoints; `maxQStar =
  1.634e9`; threshold `1.371e7`; `sdRangeRatio = 40.091`;
  `distHomog = 0.0036` (strongly heterogeneous).
- **vsc-redacted (265d):** `m = 0`; threshold `1.397e5` (single
  regime under ECP).

Cross-paradigm comparison:

| Source | axis-227 (BOCPD) m | axis-226 (ECP) m | Likely compound bucket |
|---|---|---|---|
| claude-code | 2 | 14 | **`ecp-only`** dominates timesteps where ECP fires but BOCPD doesn't restart; partial `agree-misaligned` if BOCPD's 2 align with 2 of ECP's 14 |
| vsc-redacted | 13 | 0 | **`bocpd-only`** wholesale — BOCPD finds 13 regime restarts in a series that ECP calls single-regime |

The vsc-redacted asymmetry is structurally diagnostic. ECP's energy-
distance test statistic `Q*` measures the **distributional**
distance between candidate-split halves — it's highly sensitive to
location and scale shifts that affect the empirical CDF
significantly. BOCPD's MAP restarts trigger when the **NIG
predictive likelihood** under the current run-length parameters
suddenly drops, which can happen for **slow drift** that gradually
moves the predictive parameters far enough that a fresh-prior fit
becomes more probable.

That vsc-redacted shows `m = 13` BOCPD restarts but `m = 0` ECP
changepoints means: **the editor-AI telemetry has gradual drift
that BOCPD recognizes as 13 incremental regime restarts but ECP
classifies as a single (locally-non-stationary) regime because no
candidate split produces a sufficiently large `Q*`**. This is the
structurally most interesting cross-paradigm disagreement
imaginable — it tells you the data has the *kind* of structure
that BOCPD sees and ECP doesn't.

The claude-code asymmetry is the opposite: ECP finds 14 sharp
distributional shifts; BOCPD's MAP trajectory only restarts twice.
This means BOCPD's posterior is largely staying in long-run states
and only declaring restart when the predictive likelihood collapses
hard — most of the 14 ECP shifts are small-magnitude and don't
push the BOCPD posterior past its current MAP run-length.

## The orthogonality argument

The commit at `48bb66f` declares orthogonality along three axes:

1. **inference paradigm**: Bayesian (axis-227) vs frequentist
   (axis-226).
2. **information use**: online run-length posterior (axis-227) vs
   batch full-series energy distance (axis-226).
3. **detected shift type**: NIG-predictive-likelihood collapse
   (axis-227) vs non-parametric energy distance (axis-226).

These three axes are not independent — Bayesian methods tend to be
online, frequentist methods tend to be batch (though there are
counter-examples in both directions: bayesian batch via MCMC,
frequentist online via sequential CUSUM). The compound is
structurally interesting *because* the orthogonality is along three
correlated dimensions; the cross-validation it performs is
necessarily about **all three at once**, not isolating any single
axis.

This contrasts with axis-225 × axis-224 (mean-CP × variance-CP),
which crosses a **single clean axis** (moment order: 1st vs 2nd)
and holds everything else constant (both Frequentist, both batch,
both penalty-driven). The single-axis compound has cleaner
inferential semantics; the multi-axis compound has wider
diagnostic reach.

## Why "library-only" matters

The commit message specifies axis-227 is "library-only no CLI
surface," and the compound at `48bb66f` follows the same convention
("Library-only 5-bucket diagnostic"). This means the only callers
will be other axes, future compounds, or external code that imports
the npm package. There's no `pew axis-227 --json` invocation; you
get to the BOCPD detector by importing it from the published JS API.

The architectural justification: at this point in the axis-181→227
chain, the project has 47 changepoint-class axes. Each adding a
top-level CLI subcommand would inflate the help text without
proportionally serving real users. The CLI surface stays curated to
the axes humans regularly want to invoke; the library surface is
where the orthogonality-by-construction thesis lives.

The structural cost: discoverability. A user who installs
pew-insights and runs `pew --help` will not see axis-227 listed;
they'd have to read CHANGELOG.md or grep the source. The structural
benefit: each library-only axis is a building block for richer
compounds, and the compound *is* the user-facing interface (or will
be, once enough compounds accumulate to justify a `pew compound`
subcommand).

## What to look for in axis-228+

The natural follow-on axes after axis-227's Bayesian-online entry are:
- A particle-filter / sequential-Monte-Carlo BOCPD variant (would be
  Bayesian-online but with a richer posterior representation than
  the NIG predictive).
- A Gaussian-process changepoint detector (Bayesian-batch, would
  fill the empty fourth quadrant of the 2×2 paradigm × time-mode
  matrix).
- A CUSUM-with-bootstrap variant (frequentist-online, the missing
  third quadrant counterpart).

If any of those land, a 4-axis compound across the {paradigm} ×
{time-mode} 2×2 becomes feasible — the first time the project
would have a complete cell-coverage of the cross-paradigm space.
That's structurally the next-most-interesting milestone, and
probably 5–10 axes away at the current cadence (axes 181→227 over
roughly 33 ticks per the prior compound-velocity analysis).
