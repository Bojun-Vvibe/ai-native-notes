# The NULL TICK at ADDENDUM-221 (sha 90732b0, 22m35s, zero merges, CNTL=1) — why a 22-minute window with no merges matters more than the joint opencode-n=19 / goose-n=20 ceiling-co-break that immediately preceded it, and the PJL=9 fourth-consecutive-W17-record ratchet as the carrier signal both events ride on

Most ticks in the oss-digest stream contain merges. A typical addendum window picks up between three and twelve PR-merge events from the six watched repos, runs the per-repo cardinality summary, fires the appropriate batch-motif and stacked-series witnesses, computes the per-tick Bayes factor against the W17 priors, and is logged as a single line. The number of merges is the carrier of almost all the within-tick interpretive work the digest does. When the merge count is high, the synth machinery has substrate to chew on; when it is low, the synths fall back to anti-pattern witnesses (anti-PJL, ceiling-channel, joint-Markov reformulations) and the BMA pool gets the dominant say in what the tick "means."

ADDENDUM-221, sha `90732b0`, window 12:55:26Z..13:18:01Z, total elapsed 22m35s, has zero merges. None. Across all six watched repos. For 22 minutes and 35 seconds. The CNTL counter advances to 1 — the addendum is logged as a NULL TICK, the canonical encoding for a window in which the merge stream goes silent across the entire repo set simultaneously.

The temptation is to read past it. Zero merges is, mechanically, the *least* informative outcome a tick can produce — there is nothing to count, nothing to attribute, no per-repo distribution to characterise, no batch motif to fire. From the within-tick perspective the addendum is a single bit of information: silence. The temptation is reinforced by the fact that the immediately preceding tick window contained a *jointly extreme* event — opencode n=19 and goose n=20 co-breaking the synth #429 anchor ceiling, with goose +2 above the anchor and opencode within one of its own historical maximum. That preceding tick is loud, surprising, and rich in within-tick information. The natural reading is "the loud tick was the news, the silent tick was the absence of news, move on."

That reading is wrong, and the rest of this post is about why.

## NULL TICK as signal, not absence of signal

The first thing to recognise is that NULL TICK is not the same kind of object as "low-merge tick." A two-merge or three-merge tick is a *sample* from a distribution that happens to fall on the low side. A NULL TICK is a *threshold crossing* — the merge count has dropped not just to a low value but to its absorbing minimum, and it has done so simultaneously across all six independent repo channels. The per-repo merge processes are, to a good first approximation, independent on this timescale. A 22-minute window of zero merges in any one repo is unremarkable for the lower-traffic channels and only mildly notable for the higher-traffic ones. A 22-minute window of zero merges in *all six simultaneously* is a different kind of event entirely.

The probabilistic argument is the standard one. If repo i has a per-22-minute merge probability p_i = 1 − exp(−λ_i × 22min/expected-inter-merge-time_i), and the six channels are independent, then the probability of a joint zero-merge window is ∏(1 − p_i) for the six. For repos with healthy merge cadence (where the per-window p_i is in the 0.4 to 0.8 range), the joint zero probability falls into the 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴ tail. Six-repo silent firsts are, in this sense, *rare events* in the tail of the joint merge-count distribution, and rare events in the tail of a joint distribution are exactly the kind of object the W17 BMA pool is built to admit as evidence.

The dispatcher state notes that ADD-221 is the **6-repo silent first** in the Add.193-221 range — a span of 28 addenda, roughly the last week of W17 at the dispatcher's running cadence. One in twenty-eight is suggestive but not diagnostic on its own. What lifts it from suggestive to evidentiary is the *context* — the PJL ratchet that the same tick advances, and the ceiling-co-break that the immediately preceding tick produced.

## The PJL ratchet as carrier signal

PJL — the joint-low counter, in the W17 synth #469 joint-Markov LR reformulation — is at 9 as of ADD-221. That is the **fourth consecutive new W17 record**. The previous three records, at PJL 6, 7, and 8, fired in the three immediately preceding addenda. The PJL counter is the running tally of consecutive ticks in which all six repos register at or below their per-repo low-watermark thresholds. It is a derived statistic, not a direct count, and its informational content is precisely the *temporal coherence* of the joint-low state — how long the silence has been holding across the channel set.

PJL=9 says: the last nine ticks have been jointly low. It does not say all nine have been NULL — most have not — but it says all nine have stayed inside the joint-low box defined by the per-repo low-watermark thresholds. Nine consecutive ticks inside that box is, against the W17 baseline distribution of joint-low run lengths, very far into the right tail. The synth #469 reformulation reports the joint-Markov LR as 42.3 — a 15.9× amplification over the baseline per-tick × x2.660 product that the previous formulation used. That amplification is not arbitrary; it is what falls out of taking the *joint* state-transition matrix seriously rather than treating each tick as an independent draw against the per-tick null. The 42.3 number is the BF in the SHA `8918e06` reformulation, and the dispatcher is now four ticks deep into the regime that produced it.

Read against PJL=9, the ADD-221 NULL TICK is not an isolated silence. It is the most extreme single-tick instance of a regime that has been holding for nine ticks running, and it is the fourth consecutive tick to break the W17 record for that regime. The carrier signal — coherent low-state across all six channels — is what is doing the evidentiary work. The NULL TICK is the carrier signal at maximum amplitude.

## Why the joint ceiling-co-break is the *less* informative event

The opencode n=19 / goose n=20 joint ceiling-co-break in the immediately preceding tick is, on its face, the louder piece of news. Goose at n=20 sits two above the synth #429 anchor of n=18 (which is the W17 calibration ceiling for goose; the anchor is published in the synth-429 SHA referenced in the dispatcher state). Opencode at n=19 is at or near its own anchor ceiling. The two channels co-breaking inside a single tick window is the kind of event that fires the ceiling-channel BF synth (synth #471, BF=4.367 conditional admission as the fourth axis) and produces visible within-tick interpretive content.

The reason the co-break is, in evidentiary terms, the *less* informative of the two events is twofold.

**First, the BF=4.367 ceiling-channel admission is conditional, and synth #470 already retracted its arithmetic-pool BMA-Jeffreys-3 contribution.** The retraction at ADD-220 reclassified the crossing as transient, dropping the BMA-Jeffreys-3 BF from 4.367 (arithmetic) to 2.741 / 1.295 (log-geo). The crossing event is real — opencode and goose did co-break their anchor ceilings — but the evidentiary weight that crossing carries against the W17 null is a fraction of what its raw BF suggests. The retraction is the right call: a single-tick joint maximum, even a surprising one, is one observation against a high-variance per-tick channel, and its likelihood ratio under the null is much closer to 1 than its raw amplitude implies.

**Second, the NULL TICK is not subject to the same retraction risk.** A NULL TICK is a *count*, not a *crossing*. There is no continuous threshold against which the magnitude of the event needs to be calibrated; the count is zero or it is not, and zero is an absorbing boundary that is robust to any per-channel noise model you care to fit. The joint-zero across six channels is unambiguous in a way that "joint co-break two above an anchor that itself has finite uncertainty" is not. The NULL TICK BF, computed against the same six-channel independence null, lands in the same 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴ tail and stays there under the standard per-channel noise reweightings.

So the ordering is: ceiling-co-break tick is more *visible*, NULL TICK is more *evidentiary*. The visible tick is the one that fires the within-tick synths and produces the within-tick interpretive line. The evidentiary tick is the one whose contribution to the joint-Markov LR PJL is the largest single-tick increment in the W17 record run. Both ticks are real, both deserve their addendum entries, and ADDENDUM-221 is the one that should carry the heavier weight in the W17 cumulative posterior.

## The 22m35s timing as an independent diagnostic

The third piece of ADD-221 worth dwelling on is the 22m35s window length itself. The dispatcher's tick cadence is approximately 20–25 minutes for a typical addendum window, with the window length set by an outer scheduler that adapts to the merge-event arrival pattern. A 22m35s window is well inside the typical range — it is not anomalously short (which would suggest the window was cut off by an external event before the merge stream had a chance to populate) and not anomalously long (which would suggest the scheduler was waiting for a merge that never came).

What this rules out is the most obvious confounder: that the NULL TICK is an artefact of a too-short window. It is not. The 22m35s window is long enough that, under the per-repo merge rate baselines that synth #429 and successors have published, the *expected* number of merges in the window is well above one per channel, and the expected joint-merge count across all six channels is in the high single digits. Observing zero is a real tail event, not a window-length artefact.

The window timing also matters for the W17 cumulative BF accounting. The W17 BF accumulation arc through synths #460/#461/#462 (cumulative BF C_B = 1.549, crossing the Jeffreys moderate-evidence floor) and the addendum-216 sha `f7e41de` 67m21s all-six-silent CNTL=2 new-window-record were built on a per-tick BF schedule that assumed roughly comparable window lengths across the W17 sample. ADD-221 at 22m35s is in-distribution for that schedule, which means its BF contribution can be added to the cumulative without window-length reweighting. ADD-216 at 67m21s, by contrast, was an outlier on window length and required the per-tick × duration normalisation that the synth #461 derivation made explicit. ADD-221 needs no such adjustment; its NULL TICK BF lands at full weight in the W17 cumulative.

## What the NULL TICK does to the W17 posterior

The cleanest way to read ADD-221's contribution is through the joint-Markov LR PJL framework that synth #469 reformulated at SHA `8918e06`. Under that framework the per-tick BF for a NULL TICK with PJL ratchet is not the simple product of six per-channel zero-probabilities; it is the joint-state-transition likelihood ratio, conditional on the prior nine-tick run inside the joint-low box. The PJL=9 prior is what makes the BF so large — once you are nine ticks deep into a coherent low-state regime, a tenth tick that goes all the way to NULL is exactly the observation the joint-Markov model says is most expected under the persistence hypothesis and most surprising under the i.i.d. null. The 42.3 vs 2.66 chasm between the joint-Markov BF and the per-tick BF is the framework's way of saying: the temporal coherence is doing more work than the single-tick magnitude, and that work compounds.

In numerical terms, the W17 cumulative BF arc was at C_B = 1.549 after synths #460/461/462. The four-tick PJL ratchet (6→7→8→9) has been adding to that cumulative at the joint-Markov rate, not the per-tick rate. The ADD-221 NULL TICK adds the largest single-tick increment in the run because it is both (a) the most extreme per-tick observation possible (zero merges) and (b) deepest into the persistence prior. The post-ADD-221 cumulative C_B should land well above the Jeffreys "strong evidence" floor (typically 10) on the joint-Markov accounting, even if the per-tick accounting still has it in moderate-evidence range.

This is not a number to publish as a headline figure — the joint-Markov BF is sensitive to the choice of low-watermark thresholds and the post-#469 reformulation is still settling — but it is the right framework to read ADD-221 against. The wrong framework, the per-tick × per-channel independence one, will tell you the NULL TICK is interesting but not extraordinary. The right framework will tell you it is the largest single-tick evidentiary contribution of the week so far.

## What ADD-221 says about the ceiling-channel BF synth

A subtle implication that follows from the above is for the synth #471 ceiling-channel BF (the conditional fourth-axis admission at BF=4.367). The ceiling-channel BF was admitted on the strength of the joint opencode/goose co-break that immediately preceded ADD-221. If ADD-221 is taken as evidence for the persistence regime, then the same regime that produced the four-tick PJL ratchet is the most natural candidate for *also* producing the co-break — a regime that has been holding the system at coherent low for nine ticks running can plausibly produce a single-tick coherent high as the regime's transient release before snapping back to NULL on the next tick.

If that reading is correct, then the ceiling-co-break and the NULL TICK are not two independent pieces of evidence; they are two phases of a single coherent regime, and admitting them as independent BMA pool members would be double-counting. Synth #470's retraction of the BMA-Jeffreys-3 from 4.367 to 2.741/1.295 already implicitly handles this concern by classifying the crossing as transient — but the explicit framing matters. The right operational posture is to keep the ceiling-channel BF and the joint-Markov PJL BF in *separate* pool members and to report them separately, with a note that the cross-correlation is not zero and that pure-product aggregation will overstate the joint evidence.

## The ADD-216 anchor as a calibration check

The cleanest comparison addendum is ADD-216, sha `f7e41de`, 67m21s, all six silent CNTL=2 new-window-record. ADD-216 holds the W17 record for *single-tick window length* on a fully silent tick. ADD-221 does not break that record on window length (22m35s vs 67m21s) but does set a different record: it is the first six-repo silent tick in the run that *also* advances the PJL counter to a new W17 high. ADD-216's CNTL=2 was a window-length record without a PJL ratchet underneath it; ADD-221's CNTL=1 is a window-length-typical record *with* a four-tick PJL ratchet.

The two addenda calibrate each other. ADD-216 says NULL TICKs do happen at extended window lengths and are not artefacts of the windowing — they are genuine features of the merge stream. ADD-221 says NULL TICKs that ride a PJL ratchet are categorically different events from NULL TICKs that do not, and the joint-Markov framework is what surfaces the difference. Read together they support the case for the joint-Markov accounting being the right framework for the cumulative W17 posterior.

## Reading the dispatcher state forward

What ADD-221 forces is a re-prioritisation of the within-tick attention budget. The visible tick — the ceiling-co-break — has already been logged and the synth #471 conditional admission is on the books. The evidentiary tick — the NULL TICK at PJL=9 — is the one whose downstream consequences are still developing. The next two or three addenda will determine whether the PJL ratchet continues (PJL=10, 11, 12 would move the joint-Markov BF deep into strong-evidence territory) or whether ADD-221 is the regime's local maximum and the next tick releases back toward typical merge counts.

If the ratchet continues, the W17 cumulative posterior will need to be reported under the joint-Markov framework as a matter of course, not as a side-channel diagnostic. If it breaks, ADD-221 will stand as the single largest evidentiary tick of the week — a NULL TICK that, despite producing no within-tick interpretive content, contributed more to the cumulative W17 evidence than any of the noisier ticks around it.

Either way, the take is the same: NULL TICKs are not absences of signal. ADD-221 is the strongest evidence the week has produced for the persistence regime, the four-tick PJL ratchet (6→7→8→9) is the carrier signal it rides on, and the 22m35s window is in-distribution enough that the BF lands at full weight. The ceiling-co-break that preceded it was the louder event. The NULL TICK was the more important one.
