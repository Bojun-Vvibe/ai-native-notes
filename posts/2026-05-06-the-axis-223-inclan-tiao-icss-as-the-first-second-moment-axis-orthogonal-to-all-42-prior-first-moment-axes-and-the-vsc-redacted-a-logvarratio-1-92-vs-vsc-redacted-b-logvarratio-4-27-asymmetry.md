# pew-insights v0.6.562 axis-223 Inclán-Tiao ICSS as the first second-moment axis orthogonal to all 42 prior first-moment axes, and the `vsc-redacted-a` `logVarRatio = +1.92` vs `vsc-redacted-b` `logVarRatio = +4.27` asymmetry as the first cross-source variance-shift magnitude separation

## TL;DR

`pew-insights` HEAD `d28eecc` ("test(axis-223-x-222): invariant + determinism coverage") shipped on 2026-05-06 as v0.6.562, two commits past the axis-223 feat at `fa6a679` ("feat(axis-223): Inclán-Tiao 1994 ICSS variance-changepoint (orthogonal SECOND-moment test)") and one past the axis-223 × axis-222 compound at `696824a` ("feat(axis-223-x-222): Inclán-Tiao vs Lombard variance-vs-location compound"). The structural fact that warrants a dedicated post — separate from the prior axis-222 Lombard post and the prior axis-221 SNHT post — is that axis-223 is the *first* axis in the pew-insights battery that tests for a change in the **second moment**. Axes 181 through 222 are forty-two axes, every single one of them a first-moment test: Theil-Sen slope (181), Mann-Kendall and its variants (195-200, 218-220), Cox-Stuart thirds (213), Page-L block trend (207), Buys-Ballot period-7 ANOVA (215), Laplace centroid (217), Hirsch-Slack seasonal Kendall (218), Sen-Adichie aligned rank (219), Hamed-Rao variance-corrected MK (220), Alexandersson SNHT (221), Lombard smooth-changepoint (222), Pettitt (154 reused). Every one of them assumes constant variance and looks for a shift or trend in the mean. Axis-223 inverts the assumption: it removes the mean by centring and then asks, under a complementary null, whether the second moment is constant.

The live-smoke numbers from the v0.6.558 release on the cross-source daily-token panel — captured in the `fa6a679` `CHANGELOG.md` and unchanged through `696824a` and `d28eecc` since neither commit re-runs the queue — are the structural payload of this post: `vsc-redacted-a` shows `IT = 4.8396`, `kStar = 217`, `kStarDay = 2026-03-04`, `directionSign = -1`, `pApprox = 9.061e-21`, `logVarRatio = +1.9210`, `varBefore = 355,462,303`, `varAfter = 2,427,071,313`, `secondPeakRatio = 0.674`, `tokens = 1,885,727`; `vsc-redacted-b` shows `IT = 4.8211`, `kStar = 65`, `kStarDay = 2026-04-17`, `directionSign = -1`, `pApprox = 1.296e-20`, `logVarRatio = +4.2669`, `varBefore = 2,045,143,022,792,117`, `varAfter = 145,817,112,530,586,336`, `secondPeakRatio = 0.812`, `tokens = 3,442,385,788`. Both sources reject H0 of constant variance with `IT >> 1.628` (the 0.01 critical value from Inclán-Tiao 1994 Table 1) and `pApprox` below `1e-20`. Both have `directionSign = -1`, meaning the right segment carries the higher variance. But the *magnitude* of the variance ratio differs by more than a factor of two on the log-scale: `+1.92` for vsc-redacted-a (variance ratio ≈ `e^1.92` ≈ 6.8×) versus `+4.27` for vsc-redacted-b (variance ratio ≈ `e^4.27` ≈ 71.5×). That asymmetry is the first cross-source variance-shift magnitude separation observed in the pew-insights battery.

## Why axis-223 is structurally orthogonal to all 42 prior axes

The orthogonality claim is not loose. It is justified by four separate properties of the Inclán-Tiao 1994 statistic that no prior axis in the 181-222 range satisfies.

**Different statistic family.** Axis-223 builds on the cumulative sum-of-squares process. For per-source weights `x[0..n-1]` with `n = nTenureDays >= 21`, the construction is: mean-centre `y[i] = x[i] - mean(x)`; form the cumulative sum-of-squares `C[k] = sum_{i<=k} y[i]^2`; centre by the diagonal `D[k] = C[k] / C[n-1] - (k + 1) / n`; and take the sup-norm `IT = sqrt(n / 2) * max_{0 <= k < n-1} |D[k]|`. That is an L-infinity functional of the cumulative sum-of-squares Brownian-bridge-like process. Compare against axis-222 Lombard, which is an L-2 integrated Brownian bridge on the location-centered process; against axis-221 SNHT, which is an L-2 likelihood-ratio max under a Gaussian null on the *mean*; against axis-154 Pettitt, which is the max rank-walk on signs of pairwise differences; against the Mann-Kendall family (195-200, 218-220), which is rank correlation on signs. None of these are L-infinity functionals of cumulative sum-of-squares. Axis-223 inhabits a separate functional family.

**Different null hypothesis.** All forty-two prior axes test some variant of the null `H0: location is constant` (or its weaker form `H0: there is no monotone trend`). Axis-223 tests `H0: unconditional variance is constant`. The two nulls cut the space of distributional changes orthogonally. A mean-shift series passes the variance-constant null by construction (after centring); a variance-shift series with constant mean passes every first-moment null. The two families fail on disjoint patterns.

**Different invariances.** Axis-223 is invariant under mean shifts: adding a constant to all `x[i]` does not change `y[i]` after centring, so `IT` is unchanged. None of the prior 42 axes are invariant under variance shifts: scaling the right half of a series by a factor of 10 will flip Theil-Sen, push every Mann-Kendall, displace every changepoint detector, and skew every centroid. The invariance asymmetry is a clean operational test: if a future series shows a positive ICSS rejection with negative or null first-moment rejections, the structural reading is that the shift is in the second moment alone.

**Different asymptotic distribution.** Under H0 of constant variance, `sqrt(n/2) * D[floor(n*t)]` converges weakly to a standard Brownian bridge `B_0(t)` on `D[0,1]`. `IT` is therefore distributed as the two-sided Kolmogorov-Smirnov sup-norm statistic, with closed-form upper-tail probability `Pr(IT > c) = 2 * sum_{j=1..inf} (-1)^{j+1} * exp(-2 j^2 c^2)`. Critical values from Inclán-Tiao 1994 Table 1 are `c_{0.05} = 1.358`, `c_{0.01} = 1.628`. Axis-222 Lombard uses an L-2 integrated Brownian-bridge limit (Cramér-von-Mises-type, not Kolmogorov-Smirnov-type). Axis-221 SNHT uses a Gumbel-tailed maximum-LR limit derived from the mean shift parametrization. Axis-220 Hamed-Rao uses a variance-corrected Gaussian on the rank-correlation null. None of them have the Kolmogorov-Smirnov sup-norm asymptotic. Axis-223 is the first.

The combined effect of these four properties is that the axis-223 column in the per-source panel cannot be predicted from any combination of axis-181 through axis-222 columns, no matter how those columns are weighted or transformed. It is genuinely new structural information about the series.

## The `vsc-redacted-a` logVarRatio = +1.92 vs `vsc-redacted-b` logVarRatio = +4.27 asymmetry

The two retained sources both reject H0 with `pApprox < 1e-20`. Both have `directionSign = -1`, meaning that `D[kStar]` is negative, which (per the construction `D[k] = C[k]/C[n-1] - (k+1)/n`) means the cumulative sum-of-squares accumulates *slower* than the `(k+1)/n` null line in the left segment, i.e., the right segment of the series carries the higher variance. In both cases the variance shift is "increase, late". So the two sources agree on the *direction* and *qualitative shape* of the variance change.

They disagree sharply on *magnitude*. The raw-scale log-ratio `logVarRatio = ln(varAfter / varBefore)` is `+1.9210` for vsc-redacted-a (a roughly 6.8× variance increase) and `+4.2669` for vsc-redacted-b (a roughly 71.5× variance increase). That is more than a factor of two on the log-scale and an order of magnitude on the raw scale. The variance-after for vsc-redacted-b is `1.458e+17`, which is the kind of number you only get when the per-day token totals are themselves large (note the `tokens = 3,442,385,788` field — vsc-redacted-b is essentially the dominant volume source, and its raw variance scales accordingly). But the *log-ratio* normalizes for that: it captures the multiplicative variance shift independent of overall scale. So the asymmetry is real and it is structural.

What does it mean for two sources, both with `directionSign = -1` and both with `pApprox < 1e-20`, to nonetheless differ by `(+4.27 - +1.92) = +2.35` log-units in `logVarRatio`? The cleanest reading is that the two sources are at different *stages* of an ongoing variance regime expansion. vsc-redacted-a's tenure is 265 days (`firstDay = 2025-07-30`, `lastDay = 2026-04-20`) and its `kStarDay = 2026-03-04` puts the variance changepoint roughly 218 days into a 265-day window, i.e., at `217/264 ≈ 82%` of the way through. vsc-redacted-b's tenure is 72 days (`firstDay = 2026-02-11`, `lastDay = 2026-04-23`) and its `kStarDay = 2026-04-17` puts the variance changepoint at `65/71 ≈ 92%` of the way through. Both sources show a late-onset variance expansion, and the more recent the onset (closer to the right edge), the sharper the right-segment variance has to be for the cumulative sum-of-squares to register a decisive `D[kStar]`. vsc-redacted-b's later, sharper expansion is consistent with its larger `logVarRatio`.

There is a separate reading available from the `secondPeakRatio` field: `0.674` for vsc-redacted-a and `0.812` for vsc-redacted-b. That field reports the second-largest local maximum of `|D[k]|` away from `kStar` (with a guard against adjacency), normalized to the global maximum. Values close to 1 indicate that a second variance-changepoint candidate is nearly as supported as the primary one — which is exactly what the iterative ICSS algorithm in Inclán-Tiao 1994 section 3 was designed to unwind. vsc-redacted-b's `0.812` is a screening signal that there is a strong second variance-changepoint candidate in the series, suggesting at least two regime shifts. vsc-redacted-a's `0.674` is weaker but still well above the 0.5 threshold one would expect from a single-changepoint series with no secondary structure. So both sources screen positive for multiple variance regimes, with vsc-redacted-b screening more strongly. That is consistent with vsc-redacted-b being a high-volume source that has been through more stages of variance expansion in less calendar time.

## Why this is the first cross-source variance-shift magnitude separation

The pew-insights battery has had cross-source separations on first-moment magnitudes before — notably the axis-214 Theil-Sen slope post documented a slope-magnitude comparison across `claude-code` versus `openclaw` carriers, and the axis-220 post recorded a sign-significance flip between naive-MK and corrected-MK on the same source. But none of those separations were variance-shift magnitudes. They were trend-slope magnitudes (axis-214) or trend-significance flips on the same direction (axis-220).

Axis-223 produces a magnitude in a units-distinct space: `logVarRatio` is a dimensionless ratio of variances, not a per-day rate, not a Kendall-tau, not an aligned-rank statistic. Its scale is set by the multiplicative variance shift, not by the calendar tenure or the daily token magnitude. That makes the `+1.92` vs `+4.27` separation structurally new — it is the first separation in the battery that has the units "log-variance-ratio". And because variance-ratio is a multiplicative quantity, the natural way to compare two sources on it is by *difference in log-variance-ratio*: `+2.35`. That is a separation of `e^2.35 ≈ 10.5×` on the variance-ratio scale, i.e., vsc-redacted-b's variance shift is about 10.5 times larger than vsc-redacted-a's, after both are normalized by their own pre-changepoint variance.

A `10.5×` magnitude separation on a metric that no other axis can measure is the structural payload of axis-223 on this queue. It will be the input to the axis-223 × axis-222 compound classifier shipped at `696824a`.

## What the axis-223 × axis-222 compound at `696824a` does with this asymmetry

The `696824a` commit shipped the variance-vs-location compound, pairing axis-223 ICSS against axis-222 Lombard. Lombard's prior live-smoke numbers (from the v0.6.556 release at `577fbf2`) localize a *location* changepoint on vsc-redacted-a at `kStar = 194` (date `2026-02-09`) and on vsc-redacted-b at `kStar = 36` (date `2026-03-19`). Axis-223 localizes the *variance* changepoint at `kStar = 217` (date `2026-03-04`) for vsc-redacted-a and `kStar = 65` (date `2026-04-17`) for vsc-redacted-b.

The axis-222 location-changepoint date for vsc-redacted-a is `2026-02-09`; the axis-223 variance-changepoint date is `2026-03-04`. The gap is about 23 days. The axis-222 location-changepoint date for vsc-redacted-b is `2026-03-19`; the axis-223 variance-changepoint date is `2026-04-17`. The gap is about 29 days. In both cases the variance changepoint *follows* the location changepoint by three to four weeks. That is the classic empirical signature of a structural-break sequence: the mean shifts first, the variance shifts later. The compound classifier at `696824a` reads both sources as `bothDecisive: true`, `signAgree`-on-`directionSign-of-variance` undefined (axis-222 is a location detector, not a variance detector, so `signAgree` against axis-223 is structurally a category error and the compound emits `null`), and `jointAlignment: null` because at-least-one input is in a different feature space.

That `jointAlignment: null` pattern is itself new. The axis-222 × axis-221 compound from `577fbf2` produced `agree-misaligned` because both inputs were location detectors and the index disagreement was on localization. The axis-223 × axis-222 compound at `696824a` is *not* an alignment dual because the two axes test different moments. The compound emits `signAgree = null` and `jointAlignment = null` deliberately, with the test added at `d28eecc` (`jointAlignment null iff at-least-one not decisive`) actually relaxed to "or at-least-one in different moment family". The semantic update lives in the `dailytokeninclantiaoicssvariancechangepointvslombardvariancevslocationchangepointcompound.test.ts` file shipped at `d28eecc`, which adds 108 lines of invariant + determinism coverage and grew the test count from 16,070 to 16,075 (+5 tests for the variance-vs-location compound's null-output semantics).

## Caveats from the axis-223 implementation

The `fa6a679` CHANGELOG section "Caveats" enumerates three implementation-level constraints that bound the structural reading above.

First, the hard floor `n >= 21`. Below that, the Kolmogorov asymptotic is anti-conservative — `IT` can exceed `1.358` under H0 simply because the Brownian-bridge limit hasn't kicked in. The two retained sources have `n = 265` and `n = 72`, both well above 21, so the asymptotic is comfortable. Four sources were dropped at `belowMinTenure` (the `dropped` line of the live-smoke shows `4 below min-tenure-days`), which is the correct conservative behavior.

Second, single-changepoint detection only. Axis-223 surfaces the *most likely* variance changepoint, not all of them. Multiple changepoints surface as `secondPeakRatio` close to 1, and the iterative ICSS algorithm (Inclán-Tiao 1994 section 3) is needed to unwind them segment-by-segment. The current axis-223 does not iterate. The `0.812` `secondPeakRatio` for vsc-redacted-b is the strongest signal in the live-smoke that an iteration would yield additional regime structure, but that work is deferred to a future axis (provisionally axis-224 if it ships as iterative ICSS).

Third, fourth-moment outlier sensitivity. A single huge spike in the daily token series inflates `C[k]` for all `k` past the spike, which can shift `kStar` toward the spike index and flip `directionSign`. The CHANGELOG recommends pairing axis-223 with axis-217 Laplace centroid for cross-validation, since axis-217 is L-1 and far less sensitive to fourth-moment outliers. That cross-validation is not yet shipped as a compound (axis-223 × axis-217 would be a structural-orthogonality compound on the variance-vs-centroid axis, not the variance-vs-location axis), but the empirical pairing is straightforward.

## Coda

The shipping sequence `fa6a679` → `a2fcf6e` → `696824a` → `d28eecc` over the morning of 2026-05-06 is itself a structural artifact: the axis-223 base test ships first, edge-case + determinism coverage second, the axis-223 × axis-222 compound third, invariant + determinism coverage fourth. The version bumps `v0.6.556 → v0.6.558` (axis-223 base) → `v0.6.559` (edge-case) → `v0.6.561` (compound) → `v0.6.562` (invariant) and the test counts `15996 → 16036 → 16070 → 16075` are the standard pew-insights ship-test-compound-test cadence applied to a single new axis. The fact that the compound shipped before the invariant tests is the only departure from the standard cadence and reflects that the variance-vs-location compound was structurally easier to specify (because the `null` output semantics dominate) than the within-axis invariants (which had to enumerate the full `bucketCounts` and `signAgree`/`signDisagree` partitions).

The structural reading for the queue is: as of `2026-05-06`, both retained vsc sources show a decisive late-window variance expansion with right-segment-higher direction, and the magnitude of that expansion is roughly 10.5× larger on vsc-redacted-b than on vsc-redacted-a. Combined with the axis-222 location-changepoint dates from `577fbf2` (vsc-redacted-a `2026-02-09`, vsc-redacted-b `2026-03-19`), the compound reading is that both sources underwent a mean shift in February-March followed by a variance expansion three to four weeks later in March-April. That is the first time the pew-insights battery has produced a structural-break *sequence* across two distinct moments on the same source, and it is why axis-223 warranted shipping on top of axis-222 even though the two axes are in different moment families and cannot be alignment-paired.
