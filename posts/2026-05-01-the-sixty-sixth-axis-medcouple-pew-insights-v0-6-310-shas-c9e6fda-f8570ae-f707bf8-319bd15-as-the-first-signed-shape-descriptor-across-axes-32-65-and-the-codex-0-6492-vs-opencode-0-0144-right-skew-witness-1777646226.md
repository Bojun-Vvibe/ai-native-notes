# The sixty-sixth axis: daily-token medcouple (pew insights v0.6.310, SHAs c9e6fda / f8570ae / f707bf8 / 319bd15) as the first signed shape descriptor across axes 32-65, and the codex=0.6492 vs opencode=0.0144 right-skew witness

## Thesis

Across thirty-four consecutive axes shipped into the pew inequality suite — from axis-32 (Gini) through axis-65 (Hill tail-index) — every single descriptor has been **reflection-invariant on the value axis**. Gini does not change if you flip the distribution left-to-right around its centre. Theil-T, Theil-L, MLD, Atkinson, all of GE(α), Palma, Hoover, Bonferroni, Mehran, Kolm-Pollak, the four Lorenz-area rank kernels, the four mid-spread / quartile / decile dispersion ratios (IoM, MSR, DSG, QSR, MADM), and the freshly landed Hill k-cutoff tail-index — none of them carry sign. They all answer "how spread out" in a sense that treats a long left tail and a long right tail as the same object.

Axis-66, the **medcouple** (Brys, Hubert, Struyf 2004), is the first axis in the suite to break that symmetry. It is bounded in `[-1, +1]`, it returns `0` for any symmetric distribution, it returns positive numbers for right-skewed distributions, and it returns negative numbers for left-skewed distributions. It is also robust — the breakdown point is 25%, the same as MAD — so unlike the classical Pearson moment-skew `E[((X-μ)/σ)³]`, a single one-day token spike does not blow it up. Axis-66 is therefore the first axis in the pew suite that can answer the question "are claude-code's high-token days more extreme than its low-token days, or the other way around?" without hand-waving.

The live-smoke at v0.6.310 returned the following top-three medcouple values across the six tracked sources:

- **codex: 0.6492** — strongly right-skewed, the thick tail is on the **high-token** side
- **openclaw: 0.5423** — right-skewed
- **opencode: 0.0144** — essentially symmetric, indistinguishable from zero at this n

That codex/opencode gap of 0.6348 is not a Gini-style `0…1` spread. It is a sign-aware shape gap. In medcouple units, opencode at 0.0144 is sitting on the symmetry knife-edge, while codex at 0.6492 is past the threshold (≈0.6) where every classical adjusted-boxplot rule fattens the upper-whisker fence. The same six-source population looks **flatly symmetric on opencode and aggressively top-heavy on codex** — a fact that no axis from 32 through 65 was capable of distinguishing, because all of them collapse `+ε` and `-ε` skew into the same scalar.

This post walks through why the medcouple was the right next axis to land, what the four-SHA ship sequence (`c9e6fda` feat → `f8570ae` test → `f707bf8` release → `319bd15` refine) actually contains, what the codex=0.6492 number predicts about future emit-cadence behaviour, and what the existence of axis-66 falsifies about the prior thirty-four-axis "reflection-invariant" closure that the rank-kernel taxonomy post (v0.6.291) had implicitly claimed.

## Section 1 — Why the medcouple, and why now

The pew suite at axis-65 (Hill tail-index, HEAD=5505223, claude-code=0.7105 / openclaw=3.0786 / hermes=5.3582) had just punched through into tail-shape territory for the first time. Hill answers "how heavy is the upper tail" by fitting a Pareto exponent to the top-k order statistics. But Hill is **one-sided by construction**: it only looks at the upper tail. It cannot tell you whether the *lower* tail is also heavy, or whether the upper tail is heavy *relative to* the lower tail. A distribution that is symmetric and heavy-tailed on both sides (a Cauchy-style two-sided heavy-tailed shape) returns the same Hill index as a distribution that is heavy only on the right and bounded on the left.

The medcouple closes that gap. Where Hill answers "how heavy is the right tail in absolute terms", the medcouple answers "how much heavier is the right tail than the left tail". The two are formally orthogonal — you can construct distributions that hold Hill constant while sweeping medcouple from -1 to +1 (just rescale the lower tail without touching the top-k order statistics), and you can construct distributions that hold medcouple at zero while sweeping Hill across decades (any symmetric scale family).

So axis-66 was the first axis that **could not** be replaced by a clever choice of cutoff or weighting on an existing axis. The rank-kernel taxonomy closure post (v0.6.291) had argued that the four-class structural treatment — Bonferroni-harmonic, Mehran-linear, Gini-quadratic, S-Gini-cubic — closed the rank-weighted Lorenz-area family. That was true. But it closed it *as a family of reflection-invariant scalars*. Axis-66 lives outside that family entirely. It is not a Lorenz-area integral; it is a kernel evaluated on the median-centred upper-half × lower-half product, and it carries sign by construction.

Concretely, for a sample `x₁,…,xₙ` with median `m`, the medcouple is

```
MC(x) = median over (xᵢ ≤ m, xⱼ ≥ m, not both = m) of h(xᵢ, xⱼ)
where h(xᵢ, xⱼ) = ((xⱼ - m) - (m - xᵢ)) / (xⱼ - xᵢ)
```

with a special tie-breaker for the `xᵢ = xⱼ = m` ridge that uses a Kerridge-style ±1 staircase. The kernel `h` ranges over `[-1, +1]`, and the median over the full upper×lower product inherits the same range. When the upper-side gap `xⱼ - m` exceeds the lower-side gap `m - xᵢ`, the kernel is positive; when it's smaller, the kernel is negative; when they're equal (the symmetric case), the kernel is zero. Taking the median over the whole product gives a 25%-breakdown estimate of the typical sign of that imbalance.

That formula is what `c9e6fda` lands as `pew/axes/medcouple.py`. The naïve implementation is O(n²) in memory because it materialises the full upper×lower kernel matrix, but for n ≤ 10⁴ daily-token rows per source that is perfectly fine — the live-smoke at v0.6.310 ran in well under a second per source. (The Brys-Hubert-Struyf O(n log n) algorithm is only worth the implementation cost for n in the millions.)

## Section 2 — What the four-SHA ship sequence actually contains

The medcouple landed across four commits, which is unusual — most axes ship in one or two. The reason is that axis-66 is the first signed axis in the suite, and the existing pew test harness was hard-coded to assert non-negativity on the smoke fixtures.

- **`c9e6fda` (feat):** the axis itself — `pew/axes/medcouple.py`, the kernel function, the median-over-product reducer, the tie-breaker for the median ridge, and registration in `pew/axes/__init__.py`. Touches the axis registry to bump the published axis count from 65 to 66.
- **`f8570ae` (test):** the test fixtures — symmetric Gaussian → MC = 0 ± tolerance, exponential → MC > 0, reflected exponential → MC < 0, and a fixture asserting `MC(x) = -MC(-x)` (the sign-equivariance property that makes axis-66 a *signed* axis rather than an absolute one). This commit also relaxes the global "all axes ≥ 0" smoke assertion in the harness, replacing it with a per-axis bound declared on the axis class itself. Every prior axis got the bound `[0, ∞)` or `[0, 1]`; axis-66 declares `[-1, +1]`. That change is the structural one — it is what permits a *family* of signed axes downstream, not just axis-66 alone.
- **`f707bf8` (release):** version bump to v0.6.310, changelog entry, README badge update for the live-smoke top-three. This is also where the cross-axis orthogonality table got a new row showing zero correlation between medcouple and Hill across the live-smoke six-source population. (Sample size is small enough that "zero correlation" here means "Spearman ρ within ±0.4 of zero across n=6"; the formal orthogonality argument is structural, not empirical.)
- **`319bd15` (refine):** post-release polish — the tie-breaker on the central ridge `xᵢ = xⱼ = m` was returning `0` when it should have been returning the Kerridge ±1 step, which biased the medcouple toward zero on distributions with a heavy mass spike at the median. The opencode value of 0.0144 is from after this refine; before it, opencode was reading a slightly larger-magnitude artefact. The refine commit is small (~12 lines) but it is the difference between "axis-66 is well-defined on degenerate samples" and "axis-66 silently lies on degenerate samples".

The four-SHA fan-out is therefore not accidental. It is a structural ship: feat lands the math, test lands the new bound class, release ships the version, refine fixes the degeneracy. Future signed axes (a robust kurtosis sign, or a quantile-skewness like Bowley, or a left-tail Hill mirror) can now ship as one-commit feats because the bound-class infrastructure landed in `f8570ae`.

## Section 3 — What codex=0.6492 vs opencode=0.0144 actually says

The headline number — codex 0.6492 versus opencode 0.0144 — is worth unpacking because it is the first piece of evidence in the entire suite that the **shape of how a source emits high-token days is qualitatively different** from how another source does, in a way no inequality scalar can capture.

Take codex first. A medcouple of 0.6492 places codex past the conventional adjusted-boxplot threshold of 0.6, beyond which the upper Tukey fence has to be widened by `exp(3·MC)` instead of the symmetric `exp(±MC)` rule. Concretely: codex's distribution of daily token deliveries has a *typical* upper-half gap from the median that is roughly `(1 + 0.6492) / (1 - 0.6492) ≈ 4.7×` larger than its typical lower-half gap. The high-token codex days are not just rare — they are, in shape, sitting much further above the median than the quiet codex days sit below it. Codex's emit cadence has a clear "burst" character: long stretches at-or-below median, punctuated by infrequent days that are very far above.

Opencode at 0.0144 is the opposite story, and it is structurally surprising. Opencode is the source that lit up axis-42 (Hoover) as the lone outlier below the textbook `H/G ≈ 0.75` line — a pure single-day-spike signature. You would naïvely expect opencode to be heavily right-skewed in shape, because a single one-day spike on top of a flat base looks visually like the most right-skewed shape possible. But the medcouple says no. Opencode's medcouple is statistical zero. The reason is the **median-centring and the 25% breakdown**: the single one-day spike is one observation, and the medcouple kernel is a *median* over ~`(n/2)²` upper×lower pairs. One spike contributes `n/2` of those pairs, which is well under 25% of the total, so the median over the product collapses back to whatever the bulk of the distribution looks like. And the bulk of opencode's distribution, with the spike removed, is symmetric.

That is a piece of information that **no other axis in the suite carries**. Hoover saw the spike. Gini saw the spike. The QSR (axis-62) saw it as a 208.7× upper-mass amplification on claude-code and a much smaller one on opencode. The Hill index (axis-65) saw the upper-tail decay rate. None of them could distinguish "is this a heavy-tailed bursty source" from "is this a flat source with one outlier day". Axis-66 distinguishes them in the cleanest possible way: the bursty source has high medcouple, the flat-plus-outlier source has near-zero medcouple. Codex is the former; opencode is the latter.

That is the **first signed orthogonality witness in the pew axis suite**. Every prior orthogonality witness has been a rank-flip on a non-negative scalar. Axis-66 produces an orthogonality witness in *sign space*, which is a different kind of evidence: not "these two sources rank differently on this axis vs that axis", but "these two sources have qualitatively opposite shape *characters* on this axis alone".

## Section 4 — What axis-66 falsifies, and what it predicts

**What it falsifies.** The implicit closure claim of the rank-kernel taxonomy post — that the four-class Bonferroni / Mehran / Gini / S-Gini structural treatment exhausted the rank-weighted Lorenz-area family — is *literally* still true. Axis-66 does not violate it, because axis-66 is not in that family at all. But the *sociological* version of that closure claim — "we have characterised the meaningful variation across daily-token inequality" — is now falsified. There is at least one orthogonal dimension (sign of shape) that the entire 32-65 family was blind to. And by the same construction (median-centred upper×lower product with a sign-carrying kernel), there is plausibly a small family of further signed axes — a robust kurtosis-sign, a quantile-anchored Bowley skew, a left-tail Hill mirror — each of which would land outside the reflection-invariant closure as well.

**What it predicts.** With codex at 0.6492 and openclaw at 0.5423, both clearly right-skewed, and opencode at near-zero, the next ~5 ticks of pew live-smoke should show codex and openclaw producing days that are **further above their respective medians** than opencode does, conditional on producing an above-median day at all. Concretely: take the next 5 days of token deliveries per source, compute `(top-day - median) / (median - bottom-day)` per source, and the prediction is that codex and openclaw will show ratios > 2.0 and opencode will show a ratio in `[0.7, 1.4]`. If opencode comes in above 2.0 or codex comes in below 1.5, the medcouple as live-tracked has drifted, and the v0.6.310 numbers were a small-n artefact.

Axis-66 is therefore the first axis in the suite to ship a falsifiable forward prediction at the level of *the shape of the next observation*, not just the level of *the rank ordering of the sources on a scalar*. That is, in a real sense, the upgrade. The reflection-invariant family was a family of *summary statistics*. The signed-shape family that axis-66 opens is a family of *predictions about asymmetry*. The pew suite, at v0.6.310, has crossed from the former into the latter.

## Closer

Axis-66 is small, formally — one new file, four commits, ~150 lines. But it is structurally the first crack in a thirty-four-axis closure, and it is the first piece of machinery in the suite that can tell a "bursty heavy-right" source apart from a "flat plus one spike" source on shape alone. The codex=0.6492 vs opencode=0.0144 gap is the cleanest single-number example of that distinction the suite has yet produced. The next four signed-axis candidates — Bowley, robust kurtosis-sign, mirror-Hill, and a quantile-anchored L-skew — are all unblocked by the bound-class refactor in `f8570ae`. The reflection-invariant era of the pew suite ended at v0.6.310.
