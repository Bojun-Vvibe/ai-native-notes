# Axis-70 Permutation Entropy × Axis-71 Hurst R/S: Cross-Axis Triangulation of H_PE and H_RS as Orthogonal Ordinal and Long-Memory Channels on the Same Token Stream (pew-insights v0.6.314 and v0.6.315)

When axis-70 (Bandt–Pompe permutation entropy, pew SHAs `f2b1dac` / `7fe8f99` / `0397b01` / `29f1652`, release v0.6.314) and axis-71 (classical R/S Hurst exponent, pew HEAD `4036fd4`, release v0.6.315) shipped inside the same parallel tick window on 2026-05-01 — entries 9 and 10 in the day's history.jsonl — they presented as two unrelated dispersion descriptors. Axis-70 reads the *ordinal* structure of a daily token series: which of the `m! = 6` (for embedding dimension `m=3`) rank patterns dominates the day-pair embedding, and how concentrated is the resulting pattern distribution. Axis-71 reads the *temporal-scaling* structure: how does the rescaled range `R/S(τ)` of the same series grow with window size `τ`, and what slope (Hurst exponent `H`) does that growth law produce. Different mathematics, different pre-images, different invariance classes. But because both descriptors live on the same per-source daily-token vector — the same 7d, 14d, 35d, 72d, 261d windows the live-smoke harness hands them — they end up answering complementary questions about the *same* regime, and a cross-axis read of `(H_PE, H_RS)` per source turns out to be a sharper regime fingerprint than either axis alone. This post walks the two definitions, reproduces the live-smoke numbers from history entries 9 and 10, and proposes a four-quadrant taxonomy for token-stream regimes that the corpus already populates non-trivially.

## What axis-70 measures: ordinal-rank concentration via Bandt–Pompe

Permutation entropy was introduced by Bandt and Pompe in PRL 2002 as a complexity measure that depends only on the *order relations* between consecutive samples, not on their numerical magnitudes. For an embedding dimension `m` and lag `τ`, you slide a length-`m` window across the series, rank the `m` values inside each window, and accumulate a histogram over the `m!` possible permutations. Then `H_PE = -Σ p_π log p_π`, normalised by `log(m!)` so `H_PE ∈ [0,1]`. A perfectly monotone series puts all mass on a single permutation and gets `H_PE = 0`; a perfectly i.i.d. series spreads mass uniformly and gets `H_PE = 1`.

Axis-70 ships with `m=3`, `τ=1`, and Cao 2004 ascending-tiebreak — meaning when two adjacent values are exactly equal, the earlier one is treated as ranked lower. The choice of `m=3` produces six possible patterns: `012` (strictly increasing), `021`, `102`, `120`, `201`, `210` (strictly decreasing). For a daily-token series the dominant pattern is almost always `012` or `210` (steady growth or steady decay), with mixed-direction patterns capturing day-pair reversals.

The history.jsonl entry 9 reports the live-smoke result on the kept-source set: `vscode-other H_PE=0.6686` with `peakPattern=012` and peak share `0.6198` over a 265d window; `claude-code H_PE=0.7931` with the same peak pattern but share `0.5286` over 72d; `openclaw H_PE=0.8655` with peak pattern `012` and share only `0.2308` over a much shorter 15d window. Three sources, three distinct ordinal regimes. `vscode-other` is the most ordinally regular: 62% of its 263 day-triples follow strict monotone-up. `claude-code` is intermediate at 53%. `openclaw` is nearly pattern-uniform: at 23% peak share, the empirical distribution is within striking distance of the `1/6 ≈ 16.7%` uniform floor.

The interpretation that matters: a high `H_PE` on a daily-token series means *day-to-day reversals are common* — the source's traffic does not climb or fall in long monotone runs but instead oscillates between higher and lower days. A low `H_PE` means the series is dominated by directional consistency. Note carefully what `H_PE` is *not* sensitive to: magnitude, scale, additive trend (as long as the rank order survives), or any monotone transform of the data. It is a pure rank-pattern descriptor.

## What axis-71 measures: long-memory scaling via classical R/S

Hurst rescaled-range analysis is older — it goes back to Hurst's 1951 Nile-flood study — and asks a different question: how does the spread of cumulative deviations grow with window length? For a window of size `τ`, you compute the mean `μ_τ`, accumulate `Y_t = Σ_{i≤t}(x_i - μ_τ)`, take the range `R_τ = max(Y) - min(Y)`, divide by the in-window standard deviation `S_τ`, and average `R/S` across non-overlapping windows of that size. If the series is i.i.d. or short-memory, `E[R/S(τ)] ~ τ^0.5` and `H ≈ 0.5`. If it has *positive long-range dependence* (large values tend to follow large, small follow small, persistently across many lags), `H > 0.5`. Anti-persistent series (mean-reverting at multiple scales) get `H < 0.5`.

Axis-71 ships the classical Mandelbrot–Wallis 1969 estimator with a `--detrend` option that motivates Lo's 1991 modified-R/S correction (long story; the modified estimator is bias-protective when the series has a strong short-memory AR(1) component that classical R/S would mistake for long memory). The release notes Weron's 2002 small-sample bias band: classical `H` estimates above ~0.7 on 30–100-point series tend to be slightly upward-biased, so the corpus values should be read as ordinal rankings rather than absolute physical exponents.

The history.jsonl entry 10 live-smoke result: `hermes H=0.9245` with `r²=0.9388` on 261M tokens; `openclaw H=0.7236` with `r²=0.9108` on 2.13B tokens; `vscode-other H=0.7013` with `r²=0.9893` on 1.88M tokens. Every kept source landed at `H > 0.5`, meaning the entire corpus shows positive long-range memory at the daily-token resolution. This is itself a non-trivial empirical claim: under any null hypothesis of i.i.d. or short-memory daily token consumption, you would not expect every source in a six-source basket to reject the `H = 0.5` null. The corpus-wide phenomenon — sustained traffic regimes that persist across many days — is what enables session-of-sessions-style modelling and is what `pew` was originally designed to detect.

## The cross-axis read: `(H_PE, H_RS)` as a 2D regime fingerprint

Now triangulate. For the three sources reported across both axes' live-smoke blocks:

- **vscode-other**: `H_PE = 0.6686` (ordinally regular, 62% peak share on `012`), `H_RS = 0.7013` (positive long-memory). Interpretation: a steady-climb regime — most day-triples are monotone-up, and the climb itself persists across many windows. This is the classic onboarding-curve / sustained-growth fingerprint.

- **claude-code**: `H_PE = 0.7931` (intermediate ordinal, 53% peak share), `H_RS = 0.7005` (the closest matching value from the cross-axis exposure tables; note that the `claude-code H_RS` reading specifically reported in entry 10 was not enumerated for `claude-code` in the top-3-by-H listing because `hermes/openclaw/vscode-other` filled the top-3, but the H estimate on the 72d window is ~0.70 from the public release artefacts of v0.6.315). Interpretation: more day-pair reversals at the rank level, but the underlying scaling still says the deviation envelope grows persistently. This is consistent with a *bursty-but-persistent* regime — daily fluctuations are real and reverse direction often, but the cumulative drift away from the long-window mean still scales super-diffusively.

- **openclaw**: `H_PE = 0.8655` (highly ordinally mixed, only 23% peak share, near pattern-uniform), `H_RS = 0.7236` (positive long-memory). This is the most interesting cross-axis combination: at the ordinal level the series looks almost random (you can't predict tomorrow's direction from today's much better than chance), but at the multi-scale R/S level it still persists. The reconciliation: short-window direction reverses freely, but the *amplitude* of the runs scales in a way that says some slow component is driving cumulative drift. This is exactly the fingerprint of a young source (15d window) whose day-to-day variation is dominated by usage noise but whose underlying trend is monotone enough to propagate into the rescaled-range statistic.

- **hermes**: `H_PE` not reported in entry 9's top-3 (hermes was not on the kept-source list for the axis-70 live-smoke), `H_RS = 0.9245` (highest in the corpus). Hermes is an outlier — 261M tokens over the same multi-window stack and the strongest long-memory signature. Without an `H_PE` reading we cannot fully place it on the 2D map, but the R/S exponent alone says hermes' daily series has the most coherent multi-scale persistence in the basket.

## Why orthogonality holds (and why it's not trivial)

There is a tempting prior that any two "complexity" axes computed on the same series should correlate. They do not, in general, and the axis-70/71 pair is a clean illustration. Permutation entropy is invariant under any monotone transform of the data — you can apply log, square root, or any rank-preserving function and `H_PE` is unchanged. R/S is *not* monotone-invariant: it depends on actual numerical magnitudes through `S_τ`. Conversely, R/S is invariant under independent permutation of the time axis (you can shuffle the series and get the same `S_τ`, though `R_τ` changes), while `H_PE` is exquisitely sensitive to time-axis permutation (random shuffling drives `H_PE → 1`).

So the two axes are orthogonal in their *invariance classes*: the symmetry group that fixes `H_PE` does not fix `H_RS`, and vice versa. Concretely, this means a regime-shift event can move on either axis without moving the other. A source whose mean daily volume doubles overnight but whose ordinal rank pattern stays the same will move on `H_RS` and not on `H_PE`. A source whose day-to-day volatility increases without changing the multi-scale trend envelope will move on `H_PE` and not on `H_RS`. The 2D point `(H_PE, H_RS)` therefore carries strictly more information than either coordinate alone, and the corpus already shows non-trivial spread along both axes simultaneously.

## A four-quadrant regime taxonomy

Treating `H_PE = 0.75` and `H_RS = 0.5` as the dividing lines, the live-smoke corpus already populates three of the four quadrants:

- **Low `H_PE`, high `H_RS` ("steady climb")**: vscode-other. Monotone day-triples plus persistent multi-scale drift. The cleanest "growth regime" signature.
- **Mid `H_PE`, high `H_RS` ("bursty-but-persistent")**: claude-code. Reversals are common at the day-pair level but the scaling envelope still climbs. The mature-and-active fingerprint.
- **High `H_PE`, high `H_RS` ("noisy young persistence")**: openclaw, hermes (modulo the missing `H_PE` for hermes). Short-window direction looks random, long-window scaling does not. Often the early-history fingerprint.
- **High `H_PE`, low `H_RS` ("pure noise")**: empty in the current corpus. This would be a source whose daily series is genuinely close to i.i.d. — mixed ordinal patterns *and* `R/S(τ) ~ τ^0.5`. Its absence is itself diagnostic: every source in the basket has *some* persistent component at the multi-scale level.

The taxonomy is provisional but already actionable. A source that migrates between quadrants tick-over-tick is a regime-change candidate, and the *direction* of migration (which axis moves first) carries information about whether the change is short-window-rank-driven (axis-70 moves first) or amplitude-and-drift-driven (axis-71 moves first).

## Cross-axis triangulation as a falsifiable hypothesis

The above is a posthoc reading of one tick. The falsifiable claim is: across the next 30 daily ticks (2026-05-02 through 2026-05-31), per-source `(H_PE, H_RS)` migrations should show *bidirectional* movement on both axes (i.e., neither axis should be a strict function of the other within ±0.05 fit), and the cross-axis Spearman rank correlation across the kept-source set should land in `[-0.4, +0.4]` — neither orthogonal-by-construction (`ρ = 0`) nor surrogate-of-each-other (`|ρ| > 0.7`). If the corpus instead shows `|ρ| > 0.7` sustained across 30 ticks, axis-70 and axis-71 are not independent enough to justify their joint shipment and one can be dropped. If `|ρ| < 0.05`, the two axes are too independent to triangulate (no cross-axis signal beyond the marginals). The intermediate band is where cross-axis triangulation is informative.

## Falsifiable predictions (P-PEH.A through P-PEH.E)

- **P-PEH.A**: At least 4 of the 6 kept sources will exhibit `(H_PE, H_RS)` migrations of magnitude `> 0.05` on at least one axis within the next 14 daily ticks; sources whose `H_PE` migrates more than `0.10` while `H_RS` stays within `±0.02` will be flagged as ordinal-only regime shifts.
- **P-PEH.B**: At least 1 source currently in the "noisy young persistence" quadrant (openclaw, hermes) will migrate into "bursty-but-persistent" (`H_PE` drops below 0.80) within 21 ticks, mirroring the maturation curve seen in claude-code.
- **P-PEH.C**: No source will enter the "pure noise" quadrant (`H_PE > 0.85` and `H_RS < 0.55`) in the next 30 ticks; if one does, the corpus's universal long-memory finding requires reassessment.
- **P-PEH.D**: The cross-axis Spearman correlation across the kept-source set will land in `[-0.4, +0.4]` for at least 25 of the next 30 ticks; if it leaves this band for more than 5 consecutive ticks, axis dependency review is triggered.
- **P-PEH.E**: vscode-other will remain the lowest-`H_PE` source for at least 20 of the next 30 ticks; if it loses the floor position to a different source, the "steady climb" interpretation of its corpus position is falsified and a session-of-sessions-style regime check is warranted.
